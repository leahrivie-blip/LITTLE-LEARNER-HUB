## 1) Pet Vet Clinic (`cur-lp-toddler-pet-vet-clinic`)

### Research summary
Toddler vet play works when props are **few, large, and caregiving-focused** (stuffed pets, soft brushes, large bandage strips, toy stethoscope, waiting “chairs”). NAEYC-aligned practice emphasizes play, language, and caregiving roles—not forms/worksheets. Younger toddlers do parallel play; keep the clinic simple (exam table + waiting spot + wash station). Avoid small medical kit pieces and clipboard “paperwork” as requirements.

### Problems with current lesson
- Every activity objective/description/setup is template filler (“Practice dramatic play skills during…”, “Explore X during our Pet Vet Clinic week through…”).
- Empty `teacherLanguage` (no toddler prompts), empty activity vocabulary, weak prep/cleanup/duration.
- Books mix is uneven for a vet theme (`Pete the Cat and the New Guy` is a weak fit).
- Cover is illustrated storybook art, not a real clinic setup.
- No linked printables; no realistic activity/example images.

### What will be retained
Theme identity, lesson ID, Pro plan, weekday skeleton, and strong **activity titles/roles** that are already on-theme:
- Veterinarian Dramatic Play Center, Pet Bath Sensory Station, Brush and Groom Station, Puppy Obstacle Course, Build a Pet Home, Pet Parade (titles kept; bodies rewritten).

### What will change
Rewrite all 25 activity bodies to Teaching Kit depth; prune/replace only filler that duplicates another day (e.g. multiple near-identical “sensory exploration” slots). Replace weak books; add useful clinic signs/cards printable; generate realistic setup + art images; set cover from clinic setup photo.

### Proposed weekly structure
- **Mon:** Meet pets + open clinic dramatic play + gentle pet movement
- **Tue:** Care routines (wash, brush, feed invitation)
- **Wed:** Exam/check-up play + process art
- **Thu:** Pet homes / carrying / walking paths (gross motor)
- **Fri:** Adoption parade + celebration (keep simple; not a “graduation worksheet”)

### Proposed activity lineup (Mon–Fri)
Aim ~4 meaningful experiences/day + short circle/song (not 5 thin clones).

| Day | Keep / rewrite | Replace / drop if redundant |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Meet the Pets; Vet Dramatic Play; Pet Movement Parade; Pet Story Exploration | Feed the Pets if it duplicates Tue care |
| Tue | Pet Bath Sensory; Brush & Groom; Puppy Obstacle; Pet Owner Pretend | Discovery Table if too generic |
| Wed | Vet Examination Station; Pet Health Chart Art (process); Animal Investigation (large photo cards) | Animal Rescue Sensory Bin if it becomes “rice bin + animals” only |
| Thu | Build a Pet Home; Walk the Pet; Collar Decorating (large paper, stickers) | Rescue Mission if unsafe/chaotic indoors |
| Fri | Pet Parade; Favorite Pet process art; Build Pet Playground (blocks) | Junior Veterinarian Graduation → rename to Pet Helper Celebration (no certificates required) |

### Proposed printables
1. **Clinic area signs** (Waiting / Check-in / Exam / Grooming) — large, printer-friendly
2. **Pet care choice cards** (wash, brush, feed, cuddle, rest) — for nonverbal support

### Proposed activity images

### Proposed realistic cover
**Veterinarian Dramatic Play Center** setup photo (stuffed pets, toy stethoscope, bandage roll, waiting chairs). Footer: `littlelearnershubbyleah.com`

## 2) Zoo Adventures (`cur-lp-toddler-zoo-adventures`)

### Research summary
Strong toddler zoo weeks mix **animal movement**, **zookeeper caregiving**, **enclosure building with blocks/boxes**, and **simple habitat textures**—not alphabet worksheets or tiny sorting. Movement games and zookeeper role play outperform “generic jungle rice bin” as the centerpiece.

### Problems with current lesson
- Same 100% generic template bodies as Pet Vet.
- Overlaps Pet Vet on Thu (vet/rescue/check-up cluster) — should differentiate (zookeeper care vs clinic).
- Likely repeated “sensory bin” pattern without distinctive materials.
- Illustrated cover; no printables/images.

### What will be retained
Lesson ID/Pro status; many activity *titles* (Roar Like a Lion, Zoo Keeper Dramatic Play, Giraffe Spot Painting, Elephant Trunk Water Play, Animal Parade, Build a Giant Zoo).

### What will change
Full body rewrite; differentiate habitats/movement/art so days don’t feel identical; replace habitat matching if it becomes preschool worksheet energy; realistic cover from zookeeper setup or giraffe spot painting example.

### Proposed weekly structure
- Mon: Meet animals + zookeeper play + roar/movement
- Tue: Habitats with boxes/blocks + one distinctive sensory invitation (not generic rice)
- Wed: Process art (spots, stripes, trunk water paint) + animal moves
- Thu: Care/feeding play (zookeeper, not second vet clinic)
- Fri: Build a giant zoo + parade + favorite animal share

### Proposed printables
1. **Zoo area signs** (Lions / Monkeys / Birds / Entrance)
2. **Animal movement cards** (stomp, stretch tall, waddle, hop) — large icons
3. Optional realistic **animal picture cards** (5–8 common zoo animals)

### Proposed activity images
- Zookeeper dramatic play with stuffed animals + cardboard enclosure
- Giraffe spot painting finished child art (uneven spots)
- Elephant trunk water-paint setup

### Proposed realistic cover
**Zoo Keeper Dramatic Play** or **Giraffe Spot Painting** finished example. Footer branding required.

### Books
Strong current set to keep if real/available: *Dear Zoo* (Campbell); *Giraffes Can’t Dance* (Andreae); *Brown Bear, Brown Bear* (Martin/Carle); *From Head to Toe* (Carle); *Put Me in the Zoo* (Lopshire).

### Songs
*Going to the Zoo* (title/use only if copyrighted recording); *Five Little Monkeys*; *Walking Through the Jungle*; LLH animal-movement chant.

### Safety review

### Age-appropriateness review
Emphasize movement + caregiving + open building; avoid letter-of-the-week zoo academics and detailed cutting.

## 3) Camping Under the Stars (`cur-lp-toddler-camping-under-the-stars`)

### Research summary
Indoor camping for toddlers = **blanket tent**, soft “campfire” (battery lights / tissue—no real flame), flashlight play with rules, nature movement, and simple picnic/pretend cookout. Keep flashlights pointed down; avoid tiny loose “trail mix” sensory for mouthers; prefer large nature props.

### Problems with current lesson
- 100% generic template bodies.
- Risk of unsafe or overbuilt “wilderness” activities if not rewritten carefully (small pinecones, unsupervised flashlights in eyes, chaotic bear hunts).
- Illustrated cover; no printables.

### What will be retained
Campsite/tent/campfire song structure; backpack packing; flashlight night play; fishing pretend; parade celebration titles where useful.

### What will change
Rewrite all bodies; make sensory invitations distinctive (campfire bin with paper “flames” + large logs, not anonymous filler); add tent safety/rules language; realistic cover from tent + faux campfire setup.

### Proposed weekly structure
- Mon: Build campsite / tent exploration / pack backpack / campfire songs
- Tue: Forest friends (large animals) + track stamping art
- Wed: Hike obstacle + nature collage (large pieces) + explorer station
- Thu: Flashlight exploration (rules) + star sticker/process art + owl movement
- Fri: Fishing pretend + campground block build + badge process art + parade

### Proposed printables
1. **Campsite signs** (Tent / Campfire / Trail / Quiet Time)
2. **Trail picture cards** (tree, leaf, owl, star, tent)
3. Optional **day/night sorting cards** (simple, large)

### Proposed activity images
- Blanket-tent campsite with faux campfire ring
- Flashlight “night” play setup (dim classroom, safe)
- Nature collage finished example (imperfect toddler collage)

### Proposed realistic cover
**Set Up the Campsite / Tent Exploration** realistic classroom photo.

### Books
Verify availability: *Pete the Cat Goes Camping*; *We’re Going on a Bear Hunt* (Rosen/Oxenbury); *Curious George Goes Camping*; *Llama Llama Loves Camping* (confirm edition); add a simple night/stars board book if library-available.

### Songs
*Twinkle Twinkle*; *The Bear Went Over the Mountain*; LLH “Going Camping” chant; nature version of familiar movement songs (title/use guidance only for copyrighted songs).

### Safety review
No real fire/candles; battery lights only; flashlight eye safety; trip hazards around tent cords; choking hazards in nature collage; allergy-aware sensory materials.

### Age-appropriateness review
Short hikes indoors, cooperative tent play, process art, minimal sitting—camping as pretend adventure, not outdoor survival skills lessons.
