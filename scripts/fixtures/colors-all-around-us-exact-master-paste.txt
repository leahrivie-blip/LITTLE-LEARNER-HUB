Colors All Around Us

Age Band: Infant 0–6 Months

Weekly overview

Babies explore color through simple visual tracking, tummy-time displays, mirrors, movement, safe textures, light, music, and responsive caregiver interaction. The week focuses on looking, reaching, tracking, kicking, grasping, and noticing changes rather than expecting infants to identify or name colors. Activities are intentionally short, flexible, and appropriate for babies from birth through 6 months.

Learning objectives

* Build visual attention and tracking skills.
* Encourage reaching, swiping, kicking, and early grasping.
* Provide simple experiences with bright colors and strong visual contrast.
* Support tummy-time engagement and head control.
* Strengthen caregiver-child connection through responsive talking, singing, and observation.
* Encourage sensory exploration using safe textures, movement, mirrors, and light.
* Introduce color words naturally through everyday caregiver language.

Materials list

* Large colorful scarves
* Black-and-white contrast cards
* Bright color cards
* Baby-safe mirror
* Large soft fabric squares
* Large soft balls
* Large baby-safe grasping rings
* Clear heavy-duty zip sensory bags
* Washable non-toxic paint
* White cardstock
* Painter’s tape
* Large colorful ribbons secured to a ring or wand for teacher use
* Soft blankets and tummy-time mats
* Baby-safe textured toys
* Bright fabric or felt pieces
* Securely sealed lightweight sensory bottle for teacher use
* Colorful board books
* Baby wipes
* Cleaning supplies

Teacher preparation / Toolkit

Prepare visual materials before babies arrive so activities can remain calm and responsive. Keep displays simple with only one to three visual elements at a time. Place materials where babies can see them clearly without becoming overstimulated. All sensory bags, hanging visuals, ribbons, cards, and small components remain under direct adult supervision and out of mouthing range unless specifically designed as baby-safe grasping materials. Follow each baby’s cues and end activities when the baby looks away, fusses, arches, becomes sleepy, or shows signs of needing a break.

Prep checklist

* Clean and sanitize all reusable infant materials.
* Check scarves, rings, balls, mirrors, and textured toys for damage.
* Prepare color and contrast cards.
* Secure sensory bags with strong tape before use.
* Prepare tummy-time spaces.
* Choose several colorful board books.
* Prepare washable paint and wipes for teacher-led footprint art.
* Confirm all hanging or teacher-held materials remain out of entanglement range.
* Remove choking hazards and loose decorative pieces.
* Keep activities ready for short individual or very small-group experiences.

Observation focus

Watch for visual tracking, sustained looking, head turning, reaching, swiping, kicking, grasping, excitement, calming, vocalizations, changes in facial expression, and preferences for particular colors, contrasts, movements, or textures. Notice how each baby communicates interest and when they need a break.

Family connection

Invite families to notice colors during everyday routines. They can name the color of a blanket, shirt, toy, cup, or object while talking naturally with their baby. Encourage families to slowly move a favorite colorful toy within the baby’s view and notice whether the baby follows it with their eyes or turns their head toward it.

Milestones

* Looks toward interesting sights and objects.
* Follows moving objects with the eyes.
* Turns the head toward visual stimulation.
* Holds the head up for increasing periods during tummy time.
* Moves arms and legs with excitement.
* Swipes toward or reaches for nearby objects.
* Grasps or briefly holds age-appropriate objects.
* Responds to familiar voices and caregiver interaction.
* Shows preferences through looking, movement, facial expressions, and vocalizations.

Monday — Look at Color

Activity 1

Name: Rainbow Scarf Tracking

Weekday: Monday

Category/domain: Cognitive / Visual Development

Age: 0–6 months

Duration: 2–5 minutes

Objective: Encourage babies to visually follow a slow-moving colorful object.

What children will do: Babies watch a bright scarf move slowly through their field of vision and may turn their eyes or head to follow it.

Materials: One large lightweight brightly colored scarf.

Teacher prep: Choose a clean scarf with no loose threads or decorations.

Setup: Position baby comfortably on their back, in the caregiver’s arms, or supported during tummy time. Hold the scarf where baby can clearly see it.

Steps:

1. Gain baby’s attention with the still scarf.
2. Slowly move the scarf from one side to the other.
3. Pause and allow baby time to find it again.
4. Move it gently upward and downward.
5. Stop when baby looks away or becomes tired.

Questions: Do you see the bright scarf? Where did it go? You found it again. Are you watching it move?

Observation focus: Notice eye movement, head turning, sustained looking, excitement, and attempts to reach.

Safety: Adult holds the scarf at all times. Never place loose fabric over baby’s face or leave it within unsupervised reach.

Cleanup: Place scarf in the appropriate laundry or sanitizing area after use.

Indoor/Outdoor options: Indoors near natural light or outdoors in full shade while an adult controls the scarf.

Tips: Move much slower than you think is necessary. Young babies need extra time to visually locate moving objects.

Substitutions: Use a large bright fabric square or lightweight baby-safe cloth.

Support adaptations: Keep the scarf stationary longer for very young babies or babies who need more processing time.

Added challenge: For older infants, pause the scarf slightly to one side and allow baby an opportunity to reach toward it.

Mixed-age: Older babies may practice reaching and grasping while younger babies simply watch.

Observation prompts: Did baby follow the scarf across midline? Did baby turn the head? Which movement held attention longest?

Vocabulary: Red, blue, yellow, bright, move, up, down

Image request: Realistic infant classroom activity showing a caregiver-held bright scarf positioned safely in front of a baby on a clean play mat. Natural daycare lighting, simple real materials, no staged or glossy appearance.

Activity 2

Name: Black, White and One Bright Color

Weekday: Monday

Category/domain: Cognitive / Visual Development

Age: 0–6 months

Duration: 3–5 minutes

Objective: Support visual attention using strong contrast paired with one bright color.

What children will do: Babies look at simple black-and-white patterns alongside one bright red or yellow image.

Materials: Two black-and-white contrast cards and one bright color card.

Teacher prep: Choose large uncluttered cards with bold patterns.

Setup: Place cards upright near the baby’s tummy-time area where they can be seen but not grabbed or mouthed.

Steps:

1. Begin with one black-and-white card.
2. Allow baby time to look.
3. Place one bright color card beside it.
4. Slowly alternate which card is centered.
5. Narrate baby’s attention without expecting a response.

Questions: What are you looking at? You see the bright red card. Your eyes found the stripes. Which one are you watching?

Observation focus: Notice which card receives the longest look and whether baby shifts gaze between the images.

Safety: Keep paper cards outside mouthing range and supervise continuously.

Cleanup: Wipe laminated cards if needed and return them to storage.

Indoor/Outdoor options: Best indoors in a calm, evenly lit area.

Tips: Present only a few visuals at once to prevent overstimulation.

Substitutions: Use black-and-white fabric patterns and one brightly colored cloth.

Support adaptations: Move cards slightly closer or simplify the display to one card.

Added challenge: Slowly move the bright card from one side of the contrast card to the other.

Mixed-age: Older babies may reach toward preferred cards while younger babies focus on looking.

Observation prompts: Which contrast drew attention first? Did baby shift gaze? How long did baby remain engaged?

Vocabulary: Black, white, red, bright, stripes, look

Image request: Realistic tummy-time setup with two bold black-and-white cards and one bright red visual card positioned safely in front of an infant mat.

Example images: Simple uncluttered tummy-time display using high-contrast cards with one strong color accent.

Activity 3

Name: Color Song and Object

Weekday: Monday

Category/domain: Language / Social-Emotional

Age: 0–6 months

Duration: 3–5 minutes

Objective: Pair simple color words with caregiver voice, repetition, and a real object.

What children will do: Babies listen while the caregiver sings or chants a short color phrase and shows one colorful object.

Materials: One large brightly colored baby-safe ball or toy.

Teacher prep: Choose one clean object that is easy for baby to see.

Setup: Hold baby comfortably or place baby on a mat facing the caregiver.

Steps:

1. Show the object.
2. Name its color.
3. Sing a simple repeated phrase such as red ball, red ball, look at the red ball.
4. Pause for baby’s movement, gaze, or vocalization.
5. Repeat using the same object rather than rapidly changing materials.

Questions: Do you see the red ball? You are looking at it. Did you hear my song? Where is the ball?

Observation focus: Watch for eye contact, smiles, vocalizations, body movement, and attention to the object or caregiver.

Safety: Use only age-appropriate baby-safe objects with no small pieces.

Cleanup: Sanitize the object according to classroom procedures.

Indoor/Outdoor options: Can be used anywhere a calm one-to-one interaction is possible.

Tips: The relationship and repetition matter more than teaching the actual color name.

Substitutions: Use a colorful teether, soft block, or fabric square.

Support adaptations: Reduce singing volume and movement for babies sensitive to stimulation.

Added challenge: Older infants can be invited to reach toward the object between verses.

Mixed-age: Use simple looking for younger babies and reaching or grasping for older babies.

Observation prompts: Did baby respond more to the voice or object? Did baby vocalize during pauses?

Vocabulary: Ball, red, look, bright, song

Image request: Realistic caregiver-and-infant interaction with one bright baby-safe ball, simple daycare environment, natural expression and lighting.

Example images: Teacher holding one colorful object while calmly talking or singing to an infant.

Tuesday — Tummy-Time Colors

Activity 4

Name: Color Tummy-Time Gallery

Weekday: Tuesday

Category/domain: Physical / Visual Development

Age: 0–6 months

Duration: 2–7 minutes

Objective: Encourage head lifting and visual attention during tummy time.

What children will do: Babies view a small gallery of bold colorful images while practicing tummy time.

Materials: Three large color cards, tummy-time mat, optional rolled receiving blanket for appropriate support.

Teacher prep: Select three simple cards with one large image or color field on each.

Setup: Position the cards upright at baby’s eye level a short distance in front of the tummy-time space.

Steps:

1. Position baby safely for tummy time.
2. Begin with one card directly ahead.
3. Add a second card only if baby remains comfortable.
4. Slowly shift attention between the cards by talking and pointing.
5. End tummy time when baby shows signs of fatigue.

Questions: You lifted your head. Do you see the yellow one? You found the blue picture. What are your eyes watching?

Observation focus: Head lifting, head turning, visual attention, arm positioning, and tolerance for tummy time.

Safety: Tummy time is awake and directly supervised only. Follow individual developmental and medical guidance.

Cleanup: Wipe mat and cards and return materials to storage.

Indoor/Outdoor options: Indoors or outside in full shade on a clean protected surface.

Tips: Several short tummy-time experiences are more appropriate than forcing one long session.

Substitutions: Use colorful board book pages or large fabric squares.

Support adaptations: Use chest-to-chest tummy time or caregiver-supported positioning for babies not comfortable on the floor.

Added challenge: Place two cards slightly apart to encourage gentle head turning.

Mixed-age: Adjust positioning and duration based on each baby’s head control.

Observation prompts: How long did baby comfortably lift the head? Did baby turn toward one particular color?

Vocabulary: Yellow, blue, look, lift, turn, bright

Image request: Realistic daycare tummy-time setup with three simple colorful cards at infant eye level, uncluttered environment, natural lighting.

Example images: Baby tummy-time station with bold visual cards arranged safely in front of the mat.

Activity 5

Name: Mirror Colors

Weekday: Tuesday

Category/domain: Social-Emotional / Visual Development

Age: 0–6 months

Duration: 3–5 minutes

Objective: Encourage attention to faces and color through mirror exploration.

What children will do: Babies look into a baby-safe mirror surrounded by a few large colored shapes.

Materials: Baby-safe unbreakable mirror and large securely attached colored shapes.

Teacher prep: Inspect the mirror and ensure all decorative shapes are firmly secured and inaccessible for mouthing.

Setup: Position the mirror safely at baby’s eye level during floor play or tummy time.

Steps:

1. Invite baby to notice their reflection.
2. Point to the reflection and talk warmly.
3. Point to one colored shape.
4. Move your face slightly and allow baby to watch.
5. Keep the interaction short and responsive.

Questions: Who is in the mirror? I see you. Do you see the blue shape? There is your face.

Observation focus: Eye contact with reflection, smiles, gaze shifts, head movement, and vocal responses.

Safety: Use only an infant-safe shatter-resistant mirror and supervise continuously.

Cleanup: Clean the mirror with an appropriate classroom-safe cleaner.

Indoor/Outdoor options: Best indoors without harsh glare.

Tips: Keep decorations minimal so baby’s reflection remains the main focus.

Substitutions: Use a securely mounted classroom infant mirror.

Support adaptations: Hold baby in your lap facing the mirror if floor positioning is uncomfortable.

Added challenge: Older babies may reach toward their reflection or nearby secured color shapes.

Mixed-age: Adjust distance and positioning based on each baby’s ability to hold the head or sit with support.

Observation prompts: Did baby focus on their own face? Did baby notice the color shapes or caregiver reflection?

Vocabulary: Mirror, face, blue, look, smile

Image request: Realistic baby-safe mirror setup in an infant classroom with a few simple flat color accents around the mirror.

Example images: Clean infant mirror area with minimal colored shapes and no loose decorations.

Wednesday — Touch and Color

Activity 7

Name: Color Fabric Fan

Weekday: Wednesday

Category/domain: Sensory / Language

Age: 0–6 months

Duration: 3–5 minutes

Objective: Introduce color and texture through safe caregiver-guided fabric exploration.

What children will do: Babies look at and, when developmentally appropriate, touch several large fabric pieces presented one at a time.

Materials: Three large fabric squares in different colors and textures.

Teacher prep: Wash fabrics and inspect for loose threads, buttons, or decorations.

Setup: Sit with baby or position baby comfortably on a clean mat.

Steps:

1. Show one fabric square.
2. Name the color and describe the texture.
3. Allow older babies to touch it while the teacher holds it.
4. Replace it with a second fabric.
5. Watch for preferences and stop when baby loses interest.

Questions: This one feels soft. Do you see the yellow cloth? You touched the blue fabric. Which one are you watching?

Observation focus: Looking, reaching, grasping, finger movement, facial reactions, and preferences.

Safety: Adult controls all fabric. Never leave loose fabric with an unattended infant or near the face.

Cleanup: Launder fabrics when needed and store dry.

Indoor/Outdoor options: Indoors or outside in full shade.

Tips: Present fabrics one at a time instead of creating a large busy pile.

Substitutions: Use baby-safe textured cloth books or clean washcloths.

Support adaptations: Provide visual exploration only for babies not yet reaching.

Added challenge: Older babies can grasp one fabric while the teacher gently presents another.

Mixed-age: Younger babies observe while older babies touch and grasp.

Observation prompts: Which texture received the strongest response? Did baby attempt to grasp the fabric?

Vocabulary: Soft, smooth, yellow, blue, touch, fabric

Image request: Realistic caregiver-led infant sensory activity with three large colored fabric squares, safe simple classroom setup.

Example images: Teacher presenting one large fabric swatch at a time to an infant.

Objective: Encourage reaching, swiping, and early grasping.

Support adaptations: Hold the ring closer and more stationary.

Added challenge: Slowly move the ring slightly to one side to encourage controlled reaching.

Mixed-age: Adjust distance according to each baby’s reaching ability.

Observation prompts: Which hand did baby use? Did baby open the hand before contact? How long was the ring held?

Activity 9

Name: Sealed Color Squish Art

Weekday: Wednesday

Category/domain: Art / Sensory

Age: 3–6 months

Duration: 3–5 minutes

Objective: Provide a mess-free early art experience through movement and visual color changes.

What children will do: Babies press, pat, or kick the outside of a securely sealed paint bag and watch colors move inside.

Materials: Heavy-duty clear zip bag, two small amounts of washable non-toxic paint, strong painter’s tape, white cardstock inside bag if desired.

Teacher prep: Place paint inside the bag, remove excess air, seal completely, and tape all edges securely. Inspect for leaks before bringing it near baby.

Setup: Secure the bag flat to the floor, highchair tray only when developmentally appropriate, or another stable surface.

Steps:

1. Show baby the sealed bag.
2. Place it within comfortable hand or foot reach.
3. Allow baby to press or kick the outside.
4. Describe how colors move and mix.
5. Remove the bag immediately if any seal weakens.

Questions: You made the color move. What happened when your foot kicked? Look at the colors spreading. You pressed the blue paint.

Observation focus: Kicking, pressing, hand movement, cause-and-effect attention, and visual engagement.

Safety: Paint stays completely sealed and inaccessible. Direct adult supervision is required. Discard immediately if damaged or leaking.

Cleanup: Wipe outside of bag and dispose of or store sealed artwork according to classroom policy.

Indoor/Outdoor options: Best indoors on a washable surface.

Tips: Use only two colors to keep the visual effect simple and clear.

Substitutions: Use washable gel or baby-safe sensory material sealed inside the bag according to program policy.

Support adaptations: Teacher may gently press the bag while baby watches if baby is not yet reaching.

Added challenge: Older babies can use both hands or alternate hand and foot movements.

Mixed-age: Younger babies watch color movement while older babies actively press and kick.

Observation prompts: Did baby notice the connection between movement and changing paint? Which movement was repeated?

Vocabulary: Press, kick, move, blue, yellow, mix

Image request: Realistic mess-free infant art setup with a securely taped clear sensory bag containing two bright paint colors, no loose paint accessible.

Example images: Clear sealed paint bag taped flat to a clean infant activity surface.

Name: Rainbow Light Watch

Weekday: Thursday

Category/domain: Sensory / Cognitive

Age: 0–6 months

Duration: 2–5 minutes

Objective: Encourage visual attention to gentle changes in colored light.

Substitutions: Use a commercially made classroom light projector designed for young children at a safe indirect setting.

Support adaptations: Keep the colored reflection stationary.

Added challenge: Slowly move the reflection from one side of baby’s visual field to the other.

Mixed-age: Older babies may reach toward the projected color while younger babies watch.

Observation prompts: Did baby turn toward the light? Which color or movement held attention longest?

Friday — Color Keepsakes and Favorites

Activity 13

Name: My Color Footprint Keepsake

Weekday: Friday

Category/domain: Art / Social-Emotional

Age: 0–6 months

Duration: 5–8 minutes

Objective: Create a simple teacher-led color keepsake while supporting positive one-to-one interaction.

What children will do: Babies experience a brief teacher-supported footprint process while the adult creates a colorful print.

Materials: Washable non-toxic child-safe paint, sponge or soft applicator, white cardstock, wipes, warm water and cloth as needed.

Teacher prep: Prepare paper, paint, and cleanup supplies before bringing baby to the activity. Confirm program policy and family considerations for skin-contact art materials.

Setup: Work one-to-one with baby in a secure comfortable position.

Steps:

1. Talk calmly with baby.
2. Apply a very thin layer of washable paint to the foot.
3. Gently press the foot onto cardstock.
4. Lift immediately.
5. Clean baby’s foot completely.
6. Add teacher-written name and date after baby is finished.

Questions: I am touching your foot gently. Look at your colorful footprint. Your toes made a picture. All clean now.

Observation focus: Baby’s response to gentle touch, changes in expression, movement, and comfort during the interaction.

Safety: Use only approved washable non-toxic materials. Avoid broken or irritated skin. Never leave paint on baby’s skin and prevent hands or feet from reaching the mouth before cleaning.

Cleanup: Wash baby’s foot thoroughly, sanitize the work area, close paint, and allow artwork to dry out of reach.

Indoor/Outdoor options: Best indoors near a sink or cleanup station.

Tips: Keep the design simple. The baby’s actual footprint should be the focus rather than heavy decorations.

Substitutions: Create a no-paint photo keepsake if skin-contact art is not appropriate.

Support adaptations: Stop immediately if baby appears uncomfortable and use the photo alternative.

Added challenge: Not applicable. Keep this experience short and responsive rather than increasing complexity.

Mixed-age: Older babies may watch the teacher prepare the paper while younger babies need more positioning support.

Observation prompts: How did baby respond to touch? Did calm narration help baby remain comfortable?

Vocabulary: Foot, toes, color, print, gentle, clean

Image request: Realistic teacher-led infant footprint keepsake setup with white cardstock, small amount of washable paint, wipes ready, clean uncluttered daycare table.

Example images: Simple infant footprint art station emphasizing safety, minimal paint, and immediate cleanup.

Name: Favorite Color Look

Category/domain: Cognitive / Observation

Age: 0–6 months

Duration: 3–5 minutes

Objective: Observe individual visual preferences without expecting babies to identify colors.

What children will do: Babies view two large color cards while the caregiver watches where they look and how long they attend.

Support adaptations: Present one card at a time before offering two.

Added challenge: For older babies, position cards slightly farther apart to encourage more noticeable head turning.

Mixed-age: Adjust distance and card spacing according to visual and motor development.

Activity 15

Name: Our Color Baby Book

Weekday: Friday

Category/domain: Language / Social-Emotional

Age: 0–6 months

Duration: 5–8 minutes

Objective: Build connection, early literacy, and color language using familiar baby photos.

What children will do: Babies look at simple pages showing familiar babies or classroom objects paired with one bold color.

Materials: Printed classroom photos, colored cardstock or simple color backgrounds, protective page sleeves or laminated teacher-made book.

Teacher prep: Use approved photos according to program permissions. Assemble a very simple sturdy book with one photo and one dominant color per page.

Setup: Hold baby comfortably and position the book where it can be viewed clearly.

Steps:

1. Show one page at a time.
2. Name the baby or familiar object.
3. Name the dominant color naturally.
4. Pause for looking, movement, or vocalization.
5. Repeat favorite pages.

Questions: Look, there you are. You have a blue blanket. Do you see your friend? Look at that bright yellow page.

Observation focus: Attention to familiar faces, smiles, vocalizations, reaching toward pages, and listening to caregiver language.

Safety: Adult holds the book for younger infants. Ensure laminated edges are smooth and materials are securely enclosed.

Cleanup: Wipe book according to classroom sanitation procedures.

Indoor/Outdoor options: Suitable for any calm reading space.

Tips: Familiar faces usually make this activity more meaningful than generic clip art.

Substitutions: Use a commercially made high-contrast color board book.

Support adaptations: Present only one or two pages at a time.

Added challenge: Older babies can help touch or turn sturdy pages with adult assistance.

Mixed-age: Younger infants focus on looking while older infants may reach, touch, or assist with page turning.

Observation prompts: Did baby respond differently to familiar faces? Which page held attention longest? Did baby vocalize during the book?

Vocabulary: Baby, friend, blue, yellow, book, look

Image request: Realistic infant classroom photo book with large familiar baby photos and simple bold color backgrounds, held safely by a caregiver.

Example images: Clean teacher-made infant board-style book using familiar classroom photos and one strong color per page.
