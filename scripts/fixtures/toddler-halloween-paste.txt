Toddler Halloween 

TITLE:
Pumpkins, Colors, and Fall Fun

AGE GROUP:
Toddler (18–36 months)

PLAN TYPE:
Free

THEME:
Cute Halloween and fall activities with pumpkins, colors, movement, art, pretend play, and sensory exploration. No scary monsters, frightening faces, sharp tools, candles, or choking-size decorations.

WEEKLY OVERVIEW:
Children will explore pumpkins and fall colors through sensory play, process art, movement, sorting, counting, books, songs, and pretend play. Activities are simple, hands-on, and appropriate for toddlers with close adult supervision.

WEEKLY LEARNING OBJECTIVES:
Children will practice:
- Describing colors, textures, sizes, and movement
- Using early vocabulary such as pumpkin, orange, bumpy, smooth, heavy, light, roll, basket, and fall
- Strengthening hand control through painting, sorting, carrying, and placing
- Practicing early counting from 1–5
- Exploring cause and effect through rolling and painting
- Participating in pretend play and simple social routines
- Following one-step directions
- Making choices and communicating preferences

WEEKLY MATERIALS:
Whole pumpkins, soft pumpkin balls, large pumpkin-shaped paper, washable non-toxic paint, large sponges, paintbrushes, trays, laminated color cards, large fabric leaves, large felt pieces, baskets, cloth bags, gardening gloves, orange and yellow scarves, books, music, wipes, damp cloths, smocks, painter’s tape, and large toddler-safe pretend-play props.

WEEKLY SAFETY:
Use only whole pumpkins or soft lightweight pumpkin balls. Adults handle all cutting, carving, candles, pumpkin seeds, and small decorations. Check every material for choking risk. Do not use loose beads, buttons, small pom-poms, hard candy, uncooked seeds, or tiny plastic decorations. Use washable non-toxic paint. Supervise children closely during every activity. Stop an activity if a child mouths materials, becomes overwhelmed, or needs a break.

MONDAY

ACTIVITY NAME:
Pumpkin Touch and Notice

WEEKDAY:
Monday

CATEGORY / DEVELOPMENTAL DOMAIN:
Sensory Exploration / Early Science / Language Development

RECOMMENDED AGE:
Toddler (18–36 months); offer seated support, simple choices, and hand-over-hand help when needed.

ESTIMATED DURATION:
10–15 minutes

ACTIVITY OBJECTIVE:
Toddlers will explore an intact pumpkin using sight, touch, and smell while practicing descriptive words such as orange, bumpy, smooth, heavy, light, stem, and round.

WHAT CHILDREN WILL DO:
Children will sit near a low table and explore one or two whole pumpkins. They may gently touch the skin, notice the stem, compare sizes, and smell the outside of the pumpkin while the teacher narrates.

MATERIALS:
One or two whole pumpkins, low tray, damp cloth, washable placemat, and a large basket.

TEACHER PREPARATION:
Wash the pumpkins and wipe the outside. Place one pumpkin on a stable tray. Check that the pumpkin has no sharp broken stem pieces. Prepare a quiet space with enough room for children to sit safely.

SETUP:
Place the pumpkin on the tray with the basket nearby. Keep the pumpkin whole. Do not cut it open during this activity.

STEP-BY-STEP DIRECTIONS:
1. Invite children to sit near the pumpkin.
2. Say, “This is a pumpkin. It is orange and round.”
3. Invite children to gently touch the pumpkin.
4. Point out the stem, ridges, and skin.
5. Compare a large pumpkin and a small pumpkin if available.
6. Ask children whether the pumpkin feels smooth, bumpy, heavy, or light.
7. End the activity before children become tired or begin mouthing the pumpkin.

TEACHER ROLE:
Stay within arm’s reach. Model gentle touching. Use short sentences and repeat vocabulary. Follow each child’s comfort level instead of requiring touch.

TEACHER QUESTIONS:
What color do you see? Is it smooth or bumpy? Where is the stem? Is this pumpkin big or small? Does it feel heavy or light?

LEARNING AND OBSERVATION:
Observe whether children look toward the pumpkin, reach toward it, touch gently, use a word or gesture, compare sizes, or imitate the teacher’s descriptive language.

SAFETY AND SUPERVISION:
Use whole pumpkins only. Watch for mouthing, pulling the stem, or pushing the pumpkin. Keep the pumpkin stable so it cannot roll onto a child.

CLEANUP:
Wipe the pumpkin and tray. Return the pumpkin to a safe display area. Wash hands if needed.

GROUP AND SETTING:
Small group, indoor

TEACHER TIPS:
Offer looking first and touching second. Some toddlers may prefer to observe rather than touch.

SUPPLY SUBSTITUTIONS:
No pumpkin: use a large orange squash or a realistic pumpkin photo. No tray: use a washable placemat on the floor.

OBSERVATION PROMPTS:
Does the child use a sensory word, gesture toward a feature, or compare the two pumpkins?

VOCABULARY:
pumpkin, orange, round, bumpy, smooth, stem, heavy, light, big, small

SUPPORT ADAPTATIONS:
Allow children to explore from a seated position. Offer hand-over-hand support only when welcomed. Use a photo for children who do not want to touch the pumpkin.

CHALLENGE ADAPTATIONS:
Invite children to compare two pumpkins and identify which one is bigger, smaller, heavier, or lighter.

IMAGES:
Setup photo needed. Show a realistic toddler classroom table with whole pumpkins, a tray, and an adult nearby. Do not show cut pumpkins, knives, seeds, or scary decorations.

TUESDAY

ACTIVITY NAME:
Pumpkin Sponge Painting

WEEKDAY:
Tuesday

CATEGORY / DEVELOPMENTAL DOMAIN:
Creative Art / Fine-Motor Development / Cause and Effect

RECOMMENDED AGE:
Toddler (18–36 months); use washable paint and provide close adult supervision.

ESTIMATED DURATION:
15–20 minutes

ACTIVITY OBJECTIVE:
Toddlers will use a large sponge to make orange and yellow marks on a pumpkin-shaped page while practicing pressing, lifting, and choosing colors.

WHAT CHILDREN WILL DO:
Children will dab paint onto a large pumpkin outline using a large sponge. They may add yellow or brown marks with teacher support.

MATERIALS:
Large pumpkin-shaped paper, washable non-toxic orange paint, washable yellow paint, optional brown paint, large sponge pieces, paint tray, smock, wipes, and drying rack.

TEACHER PREPARATION:
Cover the table. Put a small amount of paint in shallow trays. Tape one large pumpkin page to each child’s work area. Cut sponges large enough that they cannot be swallowed.

SETUP:
Place one paper pumpkin and one large sponge at each spot. Keep paint trays within adult reach but away from children who may grab or spill them.

STEP-BY-STEP DIRECTIONS:
1. Show the pumpkin page and say, “We are painting a pumpkin.”
2. Demonstrate dipping the sponge lightly into orange paint.
3. Show how to press and lift the sponge.
4. Invite children to make marks on the pumpkin.
5. Offer yellow or brown paint one at a time.
6. Narrate the children’s actions without correcting their artwork.
7. Place finished pages on a drying rack.

TEACHER ROLE:
Help children use a small amount of paint. Describe pressing, lifting, dabbing, and choosing. Allow imperfect marks and process-focused art.

TEACHER QUESTIONS:
What color are you using? Can you press the sponge? Where did your paint go? Do you want orange or yellow?

LEARNING AND OBSERVATION:
Observe whether children grasp the sponge, press and lift, choose a color, imitate the demonstration, and remain engaged.

SAFETY AND SUPERVISION:
Use only washable non-toxic paint. Keep paint away from eyes and mouths. Supervise children closely and stop mouthing of sponges.

CLEANUP:
Wipe hands and tables. Wash reusable sponges. Close paint containers. Place artwork on a drying rack.

GROUP AND SETTING:
Small group, indoor

TEACHER TIPS:
Do not expect the artwork to look like a perfect pumpkin. The goal is process art and hand control.

SUPPLY SUBSTITUTIONS:
No sponge: use a large washable paintbrush. No pumpkin paper: draw a large pumpkin outline on plain paper.

OBSERVATION PROMPTS:
Does the child press and lift the sponge, choose a color, or make repeated marks?

VOCABULARY:
paint, orange, yellow, dab, press, lift, sponge, pumpkin, color, wet

SUPPORT ADAPTATIONS:
Offer hand-over-hand help. Use one color at a time. Let children paint with a brush if they dislike the sponge texture.

CHALLENGE ADAPTATIONS:
Invite children to make dots, lines, and large or small marks.

IMAGES:
Setup photo and finished example photo needed. Show real toddler process art with imperfect orange paint marks, not a perfect adult-made craft.

WEDNESDAY

ACTIVITY NAME:
Fall Color Sorting

WEEKDAY:
Wednesday

CATEGORY / DEVELOPMENTAL DOMAIN:
Early Math / Color Recognition / Fine-Motor Development

RECOMMENDED AGE:
Toddler (18–36 months); begin with two colors and add more only when children are ready.

ESTIMATED DURATION:
10–15 minutes

ACTIVITY OBJECTIVE:
Toddlers will notice and match orange, yellow, brown, and green fall colors by placing large fabric or felt pieces on matching color cards.

WHAT CHILDREN WILL DO:
Children will choose a large fall-colored piece and place it on the matching color card. The teacher will name the color and help children make a match.

MATERIALS:
Large laminated orange, yellow, brown, and green color cards, large fabric leaves, large felt pieces, shallow basket, and floor mat.

TEACHER PREPARATION:
Check every piece for choking risk. Use large pieces that cannot fit into a child’s mouth. Place two color cards out first and add more as children are ready.

SETUP:
Place color cards on a mat. Put the large fabric pieces in a basket beside the cards.

STEP-BY-STEP DIRECTIONS:
1. Show the orange card and say, “Orange.”
2. Pick up one orange piece.
3. Place it on the orange card.
4. Invite a child to choose a piece.
5. Help the child compare the piece with the cards.
6. Name the match.
7. Continue with two to four colors depending on the group.

TEACHER ROLE:
Model matching without rushing. Use color words repeatedly. Give children time to make choices and correct mistakes independently.

TEACHER QUESTIONS:
What color is this? Where does orange go? Can you find yellow? Do these colors match?

LEARNING AND OBSERVATION:
Observe whether children notice color differences, make a match, use color words, or ask for help.

SAFETY AND SUPERVISION:
Use only large pieces. Do not use small buttons, beads, mini pom-poms, loose seeds, or tiny plastic decorations.

CLEANUP:
Count the pieces as they return to the basket. Wipe laminated cards and store materials in a labeled container.

GROUP AND SETTING:
Small group, indoor

TEACHER TIPS:
Start with orange and yellow. Add brown and green only if the children remain successful and engaged.

SUPPLY SUBSTITUTIONS:
No felt pieces: use large fabric squares or large laminated fall pictures.

OBSERVATION PROMPTS:
Does the child match by color, point to a named color, or change the piece after noticing a mismatch?

VOCABULARY:
orange, yellow, brown, green, match, same, different, leaf, fall

SUPPORT ADAPTATIONS:
Use only two color cards. Offer hand-over-hand help and allow pointing instead of speaking.

CHALLENGE ADAPTATIONS:
Invite children to sort by color and then count how many pieces are on each card.

IMAGES:
Setup photo needed. Show large color cards and oversized fabric pieces on a clean classroom mat. No tiny loose pieces.

THURSDAY

ACTIVITY NAME:
Pumpkin Rolling and Chasing

WEEKDAY:
Thursday

CATEGORY / DEVELOPMENTAL DOMAIN:
Gross Motor / Cause and Effect / Early Science

RECOMMENDED AGE:
Toddler (18–36 months); use a soft or lightweight pumpkin ball for younger toddlers.

ESTIMATED DURATION:
10–15 minutes

ACTIVITY OBJECTIVE:
Toddlers will roll and chase a pumpkin-shaped object while noticing direction, speed, distance, and cause and effect.

WHAT CHILDREN WILL DO:
Children will sit or stand with the teacher and roll a soft pumpkin ball across an open floor. They may chase it, retrieve it, and roll it back.

MATERIALS:
Soft pumpkin ball or lightweight artificial pumpkin, open floor space, painter’s tape, and basket.

TEACHER PREPARATION:
Clear the floor of furniture and small objects. Test the pumpkin object to make sure it is lightweight and does not have sharp pieces.

SETUP:
Place two pieces of painter’s tape on the floor to show a start line and a stopping area. Keep the basket nearby.

STEP-BY-STEP DIRECTIONS:
1. Sit across from the children.
2. Say, “I am rolling the pumpkin.”
3. Roll it slowly toward a child.
4. Invite the child to push or roll it back.
5. Use words such as fast, slow, near, far, stop, and go.
6. Let children retrieve the pumpkin with adult support.
7. End before the group becomes too active.

TEACHER ROLE:
Stay close and guide turns. Help children wait briefly, roll safely, and avoid throwing the pumpkin.

TEACHER QUESTIONS:
Where did it go? Was it fast or slow? Can you roll it to me? Did it stop near or far?

LEARNING AND OBSERVATION:
Observe whether children push with both hands, track the moving object, follow the path, take turns, and use movement words.

SAFETY AND SUPERVISION:
Use a soft lightweight object. Keep children separated enough to avoid collisions. Do not use a hard heavy pumpkin.

CLEANUP:
Return the ball to the basket. Remove painter’s tape if needed. Check the floor before the next activity.

GROUP AND SETTING:
Small group, indoor or outdoor

TEACHER TIPS:
Use slow rolling first. Turn it into a calm back-and-forth game instead of a race.

SUPPLY SUBSTITUTIONS:
No pumpkin ball: use a large soft orange ball.

OBSERVATION PROMPTS:
Does the child track the object, attempt to roll it, or use a gesture to request another turn?

VOCABULARY:
roll, stop, go, fast, slow, near, far, pumpkin, turn, ball

SUPPORT ADAPTATIONS:
Let children roll from a seated position. Move closer so less force is needed.

CHALLENGE ADAPTATIONS:
Invite children to roll toward a basket or between two wide floor markers.

IMAGES:
Setup photo needed. Show a realistic toddler rolling a soft orange pumpkin ball on an open classroom floor with an adult nearby.

FRIDAY

ACTIVITY NAME:
Pumpkin Patch Pretend Play

WEEKDAY:
Friday

CATEGORY / DEVELOPMENTAL DOMAIN:
Dramatic Play / Language Development / Social-Emotional Development

RECOMMENDED AGE:
Toddler (18–36 months); provide simple roles and adult modeling.

ESTIMATED DURATION:
15–20 minutes

ACTIVITY OBJECTIVE:
Toddlers will participate in simple pumpkin-patch pretend play while practicing carrying, choosing, requesting, and using fall vocabulary.

WHAT CHILDREN WILL DO:
Children will carry large pumpkins, place them in baskets, pretend to shop, and choose a pumpkin for the classroom pretend patch.

MATERIALS:
Large pumpkins or soft pumpkin toys, baskets, cloth bags, adult-sized gardening gloves, simple signs, play cash register, and large pretend coins.

TEACHER PREPARATION:
Check all props for safety. Remove any tiny accessories. Set up a simple pretend pumpkin stand with two or three large pumpkins.

SETUP:
Place pumpkins on a low table or floor mat. Put baskets beside them. Display simple signs that say Pumpkin Patch, Orange, and Basket.

STEP-BY-STEP DIRECTIONS:
1. Invite children to the pretend pumpkin patch.
2. Model picking up a lightweight pumpkin.
3. Place it in a basket.
4. Invite children to choose a pumpkin.
5. Use phrases such as, “I want orange,” “Put it in,” and “My basket.”
6. Pretend to pay with the adult-controlled cash register.
7. End with a short cleanup routine.

TEACHER ROLE:
Model simple pretend-play language. Give children choices without requiring them to speak. Support sharing and turn-taking.

TEACHER QUESTIONS:
Which pumpkin do you want? What color is it? Can you put it in the basket? Is it big or small?

LEARNING AND OBSERVATION:
Observe whether children carry objects safely, make choices, imitate pretend actions, use words or gestures, and participate in cleanup.

SAFETY AND SUPERVISION:
Use lightweight pumpkins and large props only. Do not use small coins, tiny decorations, hard candy, or miniature pumpkins if children mouth objects.

CLEANUP:
Invite children to return pumpkins to the basket. Count large props together and wipe surfaces.

GROUP AND SETTING:
Small group, indoor

TEACHER TIPS:
Keep the pretend play simple. Toddlers do not need a complicated store setup to practice language and social play.

SUPPLY SUBSTITUTIONS:
No pretend cash register: use a cardboard box. No pumpkins: use large orange fabric balls.

OBSERVATION PROMPTS:
Does the child make a choice, use a gesture, carry an item, or imitate the teacher’s pretend action?

VOCABULARY:
pumpkin, patch, basket, orange, choose, carry, put in, big, small, mine, more

SUPPORT ADAPTATIONS:
Offer one choice at a time. Let children watch before joining. Use picture choices for children with limited speech.

CHALLENGE ADAPTATIONS:
Invite children to sort pumpkins by size or color before placing them in baskets.

IMAGES:
Setup photo and finished pretend-play scene needed. Show a realistic, slightly messy toddler pumpkin-patch center with baskets and large pumpkins.

FAMILY CONNECTION:
Invite families to look for pumpkins, leaves, and fall colors together. Families may send a picture of a favorite fall activity or talk with the child about what they noticed.

TEACHER NOTES:
Keep the Halloween theme gentle and optional. Some families may prefer a fall-only version. Offer pumpkins, leaves, colors, and harvest language without requiring costumes or scary decorations.

WEEKLY OBSERVATION:
Document whether children participate in sensory exploration, use fall vocabulary, make color matches, roll an object, engage in pretend play, follow simple directions, and communicate choices.

WEEKLY CLEANUP:
Wash reusable materials, wipe pumpkins and trays, store large pieces in labeled bins, dry artwork, and remove any small or unsafe decorations.

IMAGE STYLE:
Use realistic classroom photos with natural lighting, real materials, imperfect toddler artwork, and slightly messy setups. Avoid cartoons, perfect staged crafts, scary faces, monsters, gore, candles, knives, and tiny loose parts.
