WEEKLY_OVERVIEW:
Infants explore friendly zoo animals through secure caregiver interactions, animal sounds, high-contrast pictures, movement, textures, music, and supervised sensory play. Experiences are designed for both younger infants and mobile infants, with short repeated invitations that follow each baby's cues and routines.

LEARNING_OBJECTIVES:
Build receptive language through repeated animal names and sounds
Strengthen visual tracking, reaching, grasping, tummy-time, sitting, and crawling skills
Encourage joint attention and back-and-forth communication
Explore safe textures, colors, movement, and cause and effect
Support secure relationships through responsive caregiver play

FAMILY_CONNECTION:
Invite families to name an animal their baby notices at home, in a book, or on a walk. Encourage caregivers to repeat one animal sound, pause for the baby's response, and celebrate any look, movement, smile, sound, or sign as communication.

OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Observe visual tracking, response to familiar voices, reaching, grasping, tolerance for tummy time, sitting balance, crawling attempts, imitation of sounds or gestures, joint attention, sensory preferences, and ways each infant communicates more or all done.

ADAPTATIONS:
Offer activities during calm alert periods and stop when an infant turns away, arches, cries, or shows fatigue. Position younger infants on a caregiver's lap, side-lying, or supported tummy time. Give mobile infants extra floor space for crawling and cruising. Use large lightweight materials and reduce visual or sound stimulation when needed.

MONDAY

DAILY_THEME:
Meet the Zoo Animals

DAILY_OBJECTIVES:
Build joint attention and receptive language
Practice visual tracking and reaching
Support secure caregiver interaction

DAILY_VOCABULARY:
Zoo, animal, look, lion, elephant, more

DAILY_MATERIALS:
Board book, large animal figures, high-contrast cards, play mat, scarf

DAILY_LEARNING_DOMAINS:
Language & Literacy, Cognitive Development, Physical Development, Social Emotional

DAILY_BOOK:
Dear Zoo | Rod Campbell

DAILY_SONG:
We're Going to the Zoo

CIRCLE_TIME:
Seat infants safely with caregivers or on the floor. Show one large animal at a time, name it slowly, make its sound, pause, and respond to each infant's gaze, movement, smile, or vocalization.

OUTDOOR_PLAY:
Take a stroller or blanket walk and notice birds, squirrels, shadows, and movement. Connect observations to zoo language by saying, “We are looking for animals.”

DAILY_OBSERVATIONS:
Notice which animals hold attention, whether infants track side to side, and how they request continuation or a break.

DAILY_ADAPTATIONS:
Move cards slowly 8-12 inches from younger infants. Place figures slightly out of reach for mobile infants. Use one item at a time for infants who become overstimulated.

SAFETY_NOTES:
Use only large washable materials with no loose parts. Maintain direct supervision and follow safe positioning practices.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Animal Picture Tracking
CATEGORY:
Open-Ended Exploration
OBJECTIVE:
Strengthen visual tracking and shared attention.
DESCRIPTION:
Infants follow large animal images while a caregiver names the animal and responds to the baby's cues.
MATERIALS:
High-contrast animal cards, play mat
SETUP:
Place infant in a safe supported position and hold one card within comfortable viewing distance.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Move slowly, narrate simply, pause often, and follow the infant's gaze.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“Look, a lion. Lion says roar. You found it. More lion?”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Show one animal card at the infant's midline.
2. Wait for the infant to notice it.
3. Move it slowly left, right, up, and back to center.
4. Name the animal and imitate its sound.
5. Stop or repeat based on the infant's cues.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Visual tracking, receptive language, joint attention, communication
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Record tracking range, head movement, eye contact, vocalization, and sustained attention.
VOCABULARY:
Look, lion, elephant, animal, more
EXTENSIONS:
Pair the card with a matching large animal figure.
ADAPTATIONS:
Use black-and-white images for younger infants and two-choice matching for older mobile infants.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Keep cards intact and out of the mouth unless specifically manufactured as washable infant materials.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: supported tracking; 6-12 months: reach, choose, crawl toward, or match.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Zoo Animal Reach and Grasp
CATEGORY:
Fine Motor
OBJECTIVE:
Encourage reaching, grasping, transferring, and cause-and-effect learning.
DESCRIPTION:
Large soft animal figures are positioned for infants to touch, grasp, transfer, and explore.
MATERIALS:
Large soft animal figures, scarf, play mat
SETUP:
Arrange two or three animals within safe reach.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Offer support without placing objects directly in the infant's hand; celebrate attempts.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“You reached for the zebra. You are holding it. Zebra has stripes.”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Place one animal near the infant's hand.
2. Gently attract attention by moving it or making its sound.
3. Allow time for reaching and grasping.
4. Encourage transferring between hands or retrieving after a gentle drop.
5. Repeat with another animal and end when interest fades.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Hand-eye coordination, grasping, bilateral hand use, persistence
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Observe hand preference, grasp type, transfer attempts, and frustration tolerance.
VOCABULARY:
Reach, hold, zebra, stripes, drop
EXTENSIONS:
Hide part of an animal under a scarf for simple object permanence play.
ADAPTATIONS:
Use lightweight fabric animals for younger infants and place animals farther apart for crawlers.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Check toys for damage before use and sanitize after mouthing.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: bat and grasp; 6-12 months: transfer, retrieve, crawl, and place in a container.

TUESDAY

DAILY_THEME:
Lion Roars and Elephant Trumpets

DAILY_OBJECTIVES:
Encourage sound awareness and early imitation
Build core and upper-body strength
Explore loud and soft contrasts without overwhelming infants

DAILY_VOCABULARY:
Lion, roar, elephant, trumpet, loud, soft

DAILY_MATERIALS:
Lion puppet, elephant puppet, mirror, rolled towel, shaker

DAILY_LEARNING_DOMAINS:
Language & Literacy, Physical Development, Social Emotional, Music & Movement

DAILY_BOOK:
Good Night, Gorilla | Peggy Rathmann

DAILY_SONG:
The Animals at the Zoo

CIRCLE_TIME:
Use puppets to make gentle lion and elephant sounds. Pause for infant responses and repeat sounds at a comfortable volume.

OUTDOOR_PLAY:
Offer supervised blanket tummy time in the shade and imitate quiet outdoor sounds.

DAILY_OBSERVATIONS:
Observe startle responses, sound localization, vocal imitation, smiles, and tolerance for tummy time.

DAILY_ADAPTATIONS:
Keep sounds soft for noise-sensitive infants. Use lap play instead of floor play when needed.

SAFETY_NOTES:
Never make sudden loud sounds near an infant's ears. Keep tummy-time sessions brief and fully supervised.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Roar and Respond Mirror Play
CATEGORY:
Music & Movement
OBJECTIVE:
Support vocal turn-taking, facial imitation, and social connection.
DESCRIPTION:
Caregiver and infant look into a mirror while making gentle animal sounds and copying expressions.
MATERIALS:
Unbreakable mirror, lion and elephant puppets
SETUP:
Secure mirror at floor level or hold it safely during lap play.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Imitate the infant first, then model one simple animal sound.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“I hear you. Roar. Your turn. I see your smile.”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Help the infant notice their reflection.
2. Copy one of the infant's sounds or expressions.
3. Introduce a gentle roar or trumpet sound.
4. Pause and wait for any response.
5. Repeat the exchange two or three times.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Social reciprocity, self-awareness, listening, early vocal imitation
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Notice eye contact, smiles, vocal turn-taking, and attention to reflection.
VOCABULARY:
Roar, trumpet, face, smile, your turn
EXTENSIONS:
Add a slow puppet peek-a-boo beside the mirror.
ADAPTATIONS:
Use only facial expressions for infants sensitive to sound.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Use an infant-safe unbreakable mirror with smooth edges.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: face-to-face imitation; 6-12 months: gesture, sound, and puppet-choice turn-taking.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Elephant Tummy-Time Reach
CATEGORY:
Gross Motor
OBJECTIVE:
Strengthen head control, shoulders, arms, trunk, and forward reaching.
DESCRIPTION:
An elephant puppet motivates infants during supported tummy time or seated reaching.
MATERIALS:
Elephant puppet, rolled towel, play mat, shaker
SETUP:
Prepare a firm floor surface and place a rolled towel under the chest if appropriate.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Stay at eye level, encourage briefly, reposition as needed, and stop at fatigue cues.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“Elephant is here. Lift, look, reach. You did it.”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Position the infant safely for tummy time or supported sitting.
2. Present the elephant at eye level.
3. Move it slowly from center to each side.
4. Invite one reach, touch, or head turn.
5. Provide rest and repeat only while the infant remains comfortable.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Head control, upper-body strength, reaching, visual tracking
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Observe head lift, weight bearing, symmetry, endurance, and reaching direction.
VOCABULARY:
Elephant, lift, look, reach, rest
EXTENSIONS:
Place the puppet a short distance ahead for pre-crawlers and crawlers.
ADAPTATIONS:
Use caregiver chest-to-chest tummy time or side-lying when floor tummy time is not tolerated.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Infants must remain awake and directly supervised during all tummy-time experiences.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: supported tummy time; 6-12 months: seated reach, pivot, or crawl toward puppet.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Monkey Peek-a-Boo
CATEGORY:
Dramatic Play
OBJECTIVE:
Develop object permanence and social anticipation.
DESCRIPTION:
A monkey puppet hides behind a scarf and returns during a responsive peek-a-boo game.
MATERIALS:
Monkey puppet, lightweight scarf
SETUP:
Sit face-to-face with the infant in a safe position.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Watch for anticipation, keep pauses brief, and let the infant control repetitions.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“Where is monkey? Peek-a-boo. Monkey came back. More?”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Show and name the monkey.
2. Cover the monkey partly with the scarf.
3. Pause and reveal it slowly.
4. Repeat with the scarf covering the caregiver's face or the puppet.
5. Invite older infants to pull the scarf away.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Object permanence, anticipation, communication, social connection
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Notice searching, smiling, reaching, vocalizing, and requesting repetition.
VOCABULARY:
Monkey, where, peek, back, more
EXTENSIONS:
Hide the monkey partly behind a cushion for mobile infants.
ADAPTATIONS:
Use very short pauses for younger infants and let sensory-sensitive infants touch the scarf first.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Never leave scarves with infants unattended and keep fabric away from the airway.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: visual peek-a-boo; 6-12 months: pull scarf, search, and retrieve.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Crawl to the Monkey
CATEGORY:
Gross Motor
OBJECTIVE:
Encourage rolling, pivoting, crawling, cruising, and purposeful movement.
DESCRIPTION:
The monkey puppet is placed at an achievable distance to invite movement.
MATERIALS:
Monkey puppet, play mat, firm cushions or tunnel
SETUP:
Clear a safe floor path and position the monkey where the infant can succeed.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Encourage effort, adjust distance, and avoid physically pulling the infant.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“Monkey is waiting. You are moving closer. Reach and touch.”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Place the monkey in view.
2. Encourage a head turn, roll, pivot, crawl, or cruise based on ability.
3. Move the monkey only slightly if motivation is needed.
4. Let the infant reach and celebrate contact.
5. Repeat from another safe position.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Motor planning, strength, balance, persistence, spatial awareness
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Observe movement strategy, symmetry, endurance, and response to encouragement.
VOCABULARY:
Crawl, roll, reach, closer, monkey
EXTENSIONS:
Create a short cushion path for confident crawlers.
ADAPTATIONS:
Place the monkey beside the infant for side-lying reach or on a stable surface for supported standers.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Use firm low materials, maintain arm's-reach supervision, and remove choking hazards.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: turn, roll, or reach; 6-12 months: pivot, crawl, pull to stand, or cruise.

DAILY_THEME:
Giraffe Tall and Zebra Stripes

DAILY_OBJECTIVES:
Explore visual patterns and contrast
Practice reaching high and low
Build descriptive language through repetition

DAILY_VOCABULARY:
Giraffe, tall, zebra, stripes, high, low

DAILY_MATERIALS:
Giraffe and zebra cards, striped fabric, stacking cups, painter's tape, mirror

DAILY_LEARNING_DOMAINS:
Cognitive Development, Physical Development, Sensory Exploration, Language & Literacy

DAILY_BOOK:
Brown Bear, Brown Bear, What Do You See? | Bill Martin Jr.

DAILY_SONG:
The Animals at the Zoo

CIRCLE_TIME:
Compare a tall giraffe picture and a striped zebra picture. Trace stripes with a finger and use high and low gestures.

OUTDOOR_PLAY:
Look at long shadows, fence lines, tree trunks, and other safe patterns outdoors.

DAILY_OBSERVATIONS:
Observe contrast preference, visual scanning, reaching height, and response to repeated descriptive words.

DAILY_ADAPTATIONS:
Use bold black-and-white stripes for younger infants and low taped pictures for crawlers.

SAFETY_NOTES:
Secure all taped materials firmly and remove them when edges loosen.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Zebra Stripe Sensory Touch
CATEGORY:
Sensory Play
OBJECTIVE:
Explore visual contrast and safe tactile differences.
DESCRIPTION:
Infants touch smooth, ribbed, and soft striped fabric while hearing simple descriptive language.
MATERIALS:
Large striped fabric squares with securely finished edges, play mat
SETUP:
Place two fabrics flat and inspect for loose threads.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Model gentle touch, label reactions, and allow infants to decline.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“Zebra has stripes. This feels smooth. This one feels bumpy.”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Show one striped fabric square.
2. Touch it to the infant's hand or foot only after observing comfort.
3. Allow grasping, patting, or rubbing.
4. Compare with a second texture.
5. End by naming the preferred texture.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Sensory processing, visual attention, grasping, descriptive language
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Record preferred texture, withdrawal, grasp duration, and visual interest.
VOCABULARY:
Zebra, stripes, smooth, bumpy, soft
EXTENSIONS:
Place fabric partly under a cup for retrieval.
ADAPTATIONS:
Offer only visual exploration for infants who avoid touch.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Use large washable fabrics with no loose threads and supervise mouthing closely.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: hand and foot touch; 6-12 months: grasp, pull, uncover, and compare.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Tall Giraffe Cup Stack
CATEGORY:
Fine Motor
OBJECTIVE:
Explore high and low, stacking, knocking down, and cause and effect.
DESCRIPTION:
Caregiver stacks large cups into a tall giraffe tower for infants to reach, touch, or knock down.
MATERIALS:
Large stacking cups, giraffe card
SETUP:
Build a short stable stack on the floor.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Keep the stack achievable, narrate position words, and invite repetition.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“Tall giraffe. Cups go up. You knocked them down.”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Show the giraffe card and say tall.
2. Stack two or three cups.
3. Invite the infant to touch or knock them down.
4. Help older infants place one cup on another.
5. Repeat with high and low language.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Cause and effect, hand-eye coordination, spatial concepts, persistence
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Observe reaching accuracy, grasp, release, anticipation, and problem-solving.
VOCABULARY:
Tall, high, low, up, down
EXTENSIONS:
Hide a zebra card under one cup.
ADAPTATIONS:
Use one cup at a time for younger infants and a low stack for infants with limited reach.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Use lightweight infant-safe cups and keep the activity on the floor.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: watch and bat; 6-12 months: knock down, nest, retrieve, and attempt stacking.

FRIDAY

DAILY_THEME:
Zoo Celebration and Favorite Animals

DAILY_OBJECTIVES:
Review animal names, sounds, movements, and textures
Encourage choice-making and communication
Celebrate individual growth and preferences

DAILY_VOCABULARY:
Favorite, choose, more, all done, animal, zoo

DAILY_MATERIALS:
Week's animal figures, cards, puppets, scarves, shakers, baskets

DAILY_LEARNING_DOMAINS:
Language & Literacy, Social Emotional, Cognitive Development, Physical Development, Creative Arts

DAILY_BOOK:
Good Night, Gorilla | Peggy Rathmann

DAILY_SONG:
We're Going to the Zoo

CIRCLE_TIME:
Offer two animal choices, sing the chosen animal's verse, and repeat each infant's favorite movement or sound.

OUTDOOR_PLAY:
Hold a gentle zoo parade with stroller riding, caregiver-carried movement, crawling, or supported walking.

DAILY_OBSERVATIONS:
Note clear preferences, communication attempts, recognition of repeated materials, and new motor skills.

DAILY_ADAPTATIONS:
Keep the celebration calm and offer a quiet one-on-one version for infants who need less stimulation.

SAFETY_NOTES:
Maintain routine care schedules, safe sleep practices, and direct supervision throughout celebration activities.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Choose Your Zoo Animal
CATEGORY:
Open-Ended Exploration
OBJECTIVE:
Encourage intentional choice-making and communication.
DESCRIPTION:
Infants choose between two familiar zoo animals and lead a short responsive interaction.
MATERIALS:
Two large animal figures or puppets, basket
SETUP:
Place two choices within view and reach.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Honor gaze, reach, body movement, vocalization, or sign as a valid choice.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“You looked at the lion. You chose lion. More lion or all done?”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Present two animals.
2. Wait quietly for a response.
3. Name the selected animal.
4. Make its sound or movement and pause.
5. Offer another turn or end based on cues.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Choice-making, communication, memory, agency, joint attention
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Observe consistent gaze choice, reaching, vocalization, gestures, and response to more or all done.
VOCABULARY:
Choose, favorite, lion, monkey, more, all done
EXTENSIONS:
Let mobile infants carry or place the chosen animal in a basket.
ADAPTATIONS:
Hold choices farther apart for clearer gaze selection or present one at a time when two are overwhelming.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Use only large intact washable materials.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: gaze or body orientation; 6-12 months: reach, point, sign, retrieve, or place.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Zoo Music and Movement Review
CATEGORY:
Music & Movement
OBJECTIVE:
Integrate rhythm, imitation, body awareness, and social enjoyment.
DESCRIPTION:
Infants revisit favorite animal motions using scarves, shakers, lap movement, crawling, or supported standing.
MATERIALS:
Scarves, infant shakers, animal puppets
SETUP:
Clear a safe movement area and offer one instrument per infant.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Match movement to each infant's developmental level and avoid forced participation.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“Shake like a monkey. Reach tall like a giraffe. Stomp gently like an elephant.”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Begin with a quiet welcome beat.
2. Show an animal and model one movement.
3. Help infants participate through lap movement, reaching, shaking, crawling, or standing.
4. Pause between animals for rest and response.
5. End with a slow goodbye verse.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Rhythm, imitation, gross motor development, body awareness, social connection
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Observe movement imitation, instrument grasp, bilateral use, balance, excitement, and regulation.
VOCABULARY:
Shake, tall, stomp, swing, stop
EXTENSIONS:
Invite older infants to select the next animal card.
ADAPTATIONS:
Use quiet humming and slow scarf movement for infants sensitive to sound or motion.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Use sealed infant shakers, supervise scarves, and support infants during standing movements.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: lap rhythm, tracking, and assisted movement; 6-12 months: shake, crawl, bounce, pull to stand, and choose.
