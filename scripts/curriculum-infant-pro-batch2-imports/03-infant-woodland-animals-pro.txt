WEEKLY_OVERVIEW:
Infants discover gentle woodland animals through nature-inspired textures, sounds, movement, pictures, songs, and responsive caregiver play. Each day highlights a familiar forest animal while supporting visual tracking, grasping, tummy time, sitting, crawling, object permanence, early language, and secure social interaction. All experiences use large infant-safe materials and follow each baby's cues.

LEARNING_OBJECTIVES:
Build receptive language through repeated animal and nature words
Strengthen reaching, grasping, rolling, sitting, crawling, and cruising
Explore safe natural colors, textures, sounds, and patterns
Develop object permanence and cause-and-effect understanding
Encourage joint attention, choice-making, and early communication
Support emotional regulation through calm responsive play

FAMILY_CONNECTION:
Encourage families to notice trees, leaves, birds, shadows, and safe outdoor sounds during walks. Suggest naming one animal, making its sound, and pausing for the baby's response. Families may send a photo of a local nature sight for classroom conversation.

OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Observe visual tracking, sensory preferences, grasping, transfer between hands, tummy-time tolerance, sitting balance, crawling or cruising attempts, recognition of repeated animals, imitation of sounds or movements, and communication through gaze, movement, vocalization, gesture, or emerging signs.

ADAPTATIONS:
Use one calm invitation at a time and shorten activities when infants look away or show fatigue. Position younger infants in supported lap play, side-lying, or tummy time. Give mobile infants safe floor space for crawling and retrieving. Replace textured materials with visual-only exploration when needed.

MONDAY

DAILY_THEME:
Welcome to the Woodland

DAILY_OBJECTIVES:
Introduce woodland animals and forest vocabulary
Build visual tracking and joint attention
Encourage reaching and choice-making

DAILY_VOCABULARY:
Forest, tree, animal, fox, owl, look

DAILY_MATERIALS:
Board book, animal cards, fox and owl puppets, play mat, green scarf

DAILY_LEARNING_DOMAINS:
Language & Literacy, Cognitive Development, Social Emotional, Physical Development

DAILY_BOOK:
Owl Babies | Martin Waddell

DAILY_SONG:
The Animals in the Forest

CIRCLE_TIME:
Show one large woodland animal at a time, name it, make a gentle sound, and pause for infant responses. Move a green scarf slowly like leaves in the trees.

OUTDOOR_PLAY:
Take a stroller or blanket nature look and name trees, sky, birds, light, and shade.

DAILY_OBSERVATIONS:
Notice which pictures hold attention, tracking range, reaching attempts, and communication of more or all done.

DAILY_ADAPTATIONS:
Use bold high-contrast cards for younger infants and place puppets slightly out of reach for mobile infants.

SAFETY_NOTES:
Use only large washable materials, supervise scarves continuously, and protect infants from direct sun and unsafe temperatures.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Forest Animal Picture Tracking
CATEGORY:
Open-Ended Exploration
OBJECTIVE:
Strengthen visual tracking, receptive language, and shared attention.
DESCRIPTION:
Infants visually follow large fox and owl cards while the caregiver names each animal and responds to cues.
MATERIALS:
Large fox and owl cards, play mat
SETUP:
Position the infant safely and hold one card within a comfortable viewing distance.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Move slowly, narrate simply, pause, and follow the infant's gaze.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“Look, fox. Fox is brown. Owl says hoot. You found owl.”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Present one card at midline.
2. Wait for the infant to notice it.
3. Move it slowly left, right, up, and back to center.
4. Name the animal and make a soft sound.
5. Repeat or stop according to the infant's cues.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Visual tracking, receptive language, joint attention, communication
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Observe eye and head movement, sustained attention, smiles, vocalization, and reaching.
VOCABULARY:
Look, fox, owl, brown, hoot
EXTENSIONS:
Pair each card with a matching large puppet.
ADAPTATIONS:
Use one black-and-white image for younger infants and two-choice matching for older infants.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Keep paper cards out of the mouth unless laminated and fully sealed.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: supported tracking; 6-12 months: choose, reach, crawl toward, or match.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Woodland Puppet Reach
CATEGORY:
Fine Motor
OBJECTIVE:
Encourage reaching, grasping, transfer, and purposeful movement.
DESCRIPTION:
Large soft fox and owl puppets invite infants to reach, touch, grasp, and retrieve.
MATERIALS:
Large soft puppets, play mat
SETUP:
Place two puppets within safe view and achievable reach.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Allow time for attempts and support without placing objects directly into the infant's hand.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“You reached for fox. Hold fox. Owl is over here.”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Present one puppet near the infant's hand.
2. Move it gently to attract attention.
3. Allow reaching, batting, grasping, or mouthing.
4. Encourage transfer or retrieval after a gentle drop.
5. Offer the second puppet for comparison.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Hand-eye coordination, grasping, bilateral use, persistence
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Observe grasp type, transfer attempts, preferred hand, and frustration tolerance.
VOCABULARY:
Reach, hold, fox, owl, drop
EXTENSIONS:
Hide part of a puppet beneath the green scarf.
ADAPTATIONS:
Use lightweight fabric puppets for younger infants and wider spacing for crawlers.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Inspect puppets for loose seams and sanitize after mouthing.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: bat and grasp; 6-12 months: transfer, retrieve, crawl, and place in a basket.

TUESDAY

DAILY_THEME:
Soft Rabbits and Hopping Movement

DAILY_OBJECTIVES:
Explore soft texture safely
Encourage leg movement, rolling, crawling, and supported bouncing
Build anticipation through repeated songs

DAILY_VOCABULARY:
Rabbit, bunny, soft, hop, up, down

DAILY_MATERIALS:
Rabbit puppet, soft textured fabric, mirror, floor cushions, shaker

DAILY_LEARNING_DOMAINS:
Sensory Exploration, Physical Development, Language & Literacy, Music & Movement

DAILY_BOOK:
Goodnight Moon | Margaret Wise Brown

DAILY_SONG:
Sleeping Bunnies

CIRCLE_TIME:
Sing Sleeping Bunnies softly, pause before “hop,” and help infants participate through leg kicks, lap bouncing, crawling, or supported standing.

OUTDOOR_PLAY:
Offer blanket movement in the shade, supported sitting, or a short stroller ride while watching for birds and rabbits.

DAILY_OBSERVATIONS:
Observe texture response, anticipation, kicking, rolling, crawling, balance, and regulation.

DAILY_ADAPTATIONS:
Use visual-only texture exploration for infants who avoid touch and keep bouncing very gentle.

SAFETY_NOTES:
Support head and trunk during movement and use only large securely finished fabrics.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Soft Bunny Texture Touch
CATEGORY:
Sensory Play
OBJECTIVE:
Explore safe tactile differences and descriptive language.
DESCRIPTION:
Infants touch, pat, grasp, or visually examine a large soft fabric square while hearing rabbit and soft vocabulary.
MATERIALS:
Large soft fabric square with finished edges, rabbit puppet
SETUP:
Inspect fabric and place it flat on the play mat.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Offer touch gradually, watch reactions, and never force sensory contact.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“Soft bunny. Your hand touched soft fabric. More or all done?”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Show the rabbit puppet.
2. Present the soft fabric near the infant's hand or foot.
3. Allow patting, grasping, rubbing, or looking.
4. Compare the puppet and fabric textures.
5. End when the infant disengages.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Sensory processing, grasping, body awareness, receptive language
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Record approach, withdrawal, grasp duration, mouthing, and preference.
VOCABULARY:
Rabbit, soft, touch, hand, more
EXTENSIONS:
Place the fabric partly over the puppet for a simple reveal.
ADAPTATIONS:
Offer only visual comparison for touch-sensitive infants.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Supervise fabric continuously and remove loose threads immediately.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: hand or foot touch; 6-12 months: grasp, pull, uncover, and compare.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Bunny Hop Body Play
CATEGORY:
Gross Motor
OBJECTIVE:
Support body awareness, rhythm, leg strength, and movement imitation.
DESCRIPTION:
Caregivers pair a gentle bunny song with developmentally appropriate kicking, bouncing, crawling, or supported standing.
MATERIALS:
Rabbit puppet, shaker, play mat
SETUP:
Clear a safe movement space and position each infant according to ability.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Match movement to developmental level, maintain support, and stop at fatigue cues.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“Bunny sleeps. Wake up, bunny. Hop gently. Up and down.”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Begin with the rabbit puppet resting quietly.
2. Sing a slow sleeping verse.
3. Pause, then model hop with the puppet.
4. Help infants kick, bounce on a lap, crawl, or bend knees while supported.
5. Return to a calm resting verse.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Rhythm, gross motor control, anticipation, social enjoyment
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Observe kicking, trunk control, crawling, supported standing, smiles, and overstimulation cues.
VOCABULARY:
Bunny, sleep, wake, hop, up, down
EXTENSIONS:
Invite mobile infants to crawl toward the rabbit puppet.
ADAPTATIONS:
Use only slow puppet movement for infants who dislike bouncing.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Never bounce forcefully and always support head, neck, and trunk as needed.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: assisted leg movement and lap rhythm; 6-12 months: crawl, bounce, squat with support, or cruise.

WEDNESDAY

DAILY_THEME:
Bear Tummy Time and Hide-and-Find

DAILY_OBJECTIVES:
Strengthen head, shoulder, arm, and trunk control
Develop object permanence
Encourage persistence and help-seeking

DAILY_VOCABULARY:
Bear, big, hide, find, under, back

DAILY_MATERIALS:
Bear puppet, brown scarf, rolled towel, stacking cups, play mat

DAILY_LEARNING_DOMAINS:
Physical Development, Cognitive Development, Social Emotional, Language & Literacy

DAILY_BOOK:
Brown Bear, Brown Bear, What Do You See? | Bill Martin Jr.

DAILY_SONG:
Teddy Bear, Teddy Bear

CIRCLE_TIME:
Show the bear puppet, sing a slow Teddy Bear verse, and hide the puppet partly behind a scarf before bringing it back.

OUTDOOR_PLAY:
Provide supervised tummy time or seated reaching on a blanket in the shade.

DAILY_OBSERVATIONS:
Observe head lift, weight bearing, searching, anticipation, frustration tolerance, and recovery.

DAILY_ADAPTATIONS:
Use caregiver chest tummy time or side-lying when floor tummy time is not tolerated.

SAFETY_NOTES:
Tummy time is awake and directly supervised. Scarves are removed immediately after play.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Bear Tummy-Time Reach
CATEGORY:
Gross Motor
OBJECTIVE:
Strengthen upper-body control and purposeful reaching.
DESCRIPTION:
A bear puppet motivates infants during supported tummy time, side-lying, sitting, or crawling.
MATERIALS:
Bear puppet, rolled towel, play mat
SETUP:
Prepare a firm safe floor surface and a rolled towel if support is appropriate.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Stay at eye level, encourage briefly, reposition, and stop at distress or fatigue.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“Bear is here. Lift and look. Reach for bear. Time to rest.”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Position the infant safely.
2. Present bear at eye level.
3. Move bear slowly from center to each side.
4. Invite a head turn, reach, pivot, or crawl.
5. Provide rest and repeat only while comfortable.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Head control, upper-body strength, reaching, visual tracking, persistence
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Observe symmetry, head lift, weight bearing, endurance, and reaching direction.
VOCABULARY:
Bear, lift, look, reach, rest
EXTENSIONS:
Place bear a short distance ahead for crawlers.
ADAPTATIONS:
Use chest-to-chest tummy time, side-lying, or supported sitting.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Infant must remain awake and directly supervised at all times.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: supported tummy time and tracking; 6-12 months: pivot, crawl, pull to stand, or cruise.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Where Is Bear?
CATEGORY:
Cognitive Development
OBJECTIVE:
Develop object permanence and searching skills.
DESCRIPTION:
A bear puppet is partly or fully hidden beneath a scarf or large cup for infants to find.
MATERIALS:
Bear puppet, brown scarf, large stacking cups
SETUP:
Place materials on a clean floor mat.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Use brief pauses, reveal before frustration, and celebrate searching attempts.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“Where is bear? Bear is under the scarf. You found bear. Bear came back.”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Show and name bear.
2. Cover part of bear with the scarf.
3. Pause and reveal bear slowly.
4. Invite older infants to pull the scarf or lift a cup.
5. Repeat in a new location.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Object permanence, memory, problem-solving, fine motor control
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Observe searching, reaching, lifting, persistence, and anticipation.
VOCABULARY:
Where, bear, hide, under, find, back
EXTENSIONS:
Hide bear partly behind a stable cushion for crawlers.
ADAPTATIONS:
Keep part of bear visible for infants beginning object permanence.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Never leave scarves unattended and use only large lightweight cups.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: watch reveal and reach; 6-12 months: pull, lift, search, and retrieve.

THURSDAY

DAILY_THEME:
Owl Sounds and Nighttime Light

DAILY_OBJECTIVES:
Develop sound localization and vocal turn-taking
Explore light and dark contrast
Support calm attention and regulation

DAILY_VOCABULARY:
Owl, hoot, night, light, dark, quiet

DAILY_MATERIALS:
Owl puppet, high-contrast night cards, flashlight controlled by adult, dark scarf, mirror

DAILY_LEARNING_DOMAINS:
Language & Literacy, Cognitive Development, Sensory Exploration, Social Emotional

DAILY_BOOK:
Owl Babies | Martin Waddell

DAILY_SONG:
Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star

CIRCLE_TIME:
Make a gentle owl hoot, pause for infant responses, and show a high-contrast moon or star card.

OUTDOOR_PLAY:
Notice shade and sunlight without directing light into infants' eyes.

DAILY_OBSERVATIONS:
Observe sound localization, vocal response, visual tracking, calming, and sensitivity to contrast or light.

DAILY_ADAPTATIONS:
Use quiet humming instead of owl sounds and omit flashlight play for light-sensitive infants.

SAFETY_NOTES:
Adults control the flashlight and never shine it into eyes. Keep sounds soft.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Hoot and Respond
CATEGORY:
Music & Movement
OBJECTIVE:
Encourage listening, sound localization, and vocal turn-taking.
DESCRIPTION:
A caregiver uses an owl puppet and gentle hoot sounds, pausing for any infant response.
MATERIALS:
Owl puppet, unbreakable mirror
SETUP:
Seat or position infant safely face-to-face with the caregiver.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Imitate the infant first, model a soft hoot, and wait without pressuring.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“I hear you. Owl says hoot. Your turn. You made a sound.”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Copy one natural infant sound.
2. Show the owl puppet.
3. Make one soft hoot.
4. Pause for gaze, movement, smile, or vocalization.
5. Repeat two or three turns and end calmly.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Auditory attention, communication, social reciprocity, imitation
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Observe head turns, eye contact, vocal timing, smiles, and regulation.
VOCABULARY:
Owl, hoot, hear, quiet, your turn
EXTENSIONS:
Move the owl gently from one side to the other for sound localization.
ADAPTATIONS:
Use facial expression and silent puppet movement for sound-sensitive infants.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Keep sounds low and never vocalize close to the infant's ear.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: listen and respond through movement; 6-12 months: imitate sound, gesture, or puppet movement.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Moonlight Tracking
CATEGORY:
Sensory Play
OBJECTIVE:
Support visual tracking and awareness of light and dark contrast.
DESCRIPTION:
An adult moves a soft light spot across the wall or floor while pairing it with quiet night vocabulary.
MATERIALS:
Adult-controlled flashlight, moon and star cards, dark scarf
SETUP:
Dim the area only slightly while maintaining full safe visibility.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Move light slowly, monitor comfort, and stop immediately if the infant turns away or squints.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“Light moves slowly. Moon. Star. Now the light is all done.”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Show a moon card in normal room light.
2. Turn on the flashlight pointed at a wall or floor away from faces.
3. Move the light spot slowly a short distance.
4. Pause and allow the infant to track or reach toward the area.
5. Turn it off and model all done.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Visual tracking, contrast awareness, attention, receptive language
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Observe tracking range, squinting, avoidance, reaching, and sustained attention.
VOCABULARY:
Light, dark, moon, star, move
EXTENSIONS:
Place the owl puppet near the light spot for older infants.
ADAPTATIONS:
Use high-contrast cards only when dim light is not appropriate.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Never shine light into eyes or create a fully dark room.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: short midline tracking; 6-12 months: wider tracking, reaching, or crawling toward a lit wall area.

FRIDAY

DAILY_THEME:
Woodland Favorites and Nature Celebration

DAILY_OBJECTIVES:
Review animal names, movements, sounds, and textures
Encourage choice-making and communication
Celebrate individual growth and preferences

DAILY_VOCABULARY:
Favorite, choose, animal, forest, more, all done

DAILY_MATERIALS:
Week's animal puppets, cards, scarves, shakers, books, baskets

DAILY_LEARNING_DOMAINS:
Language & Literacy, Social Emotional, Cognitive Development, Physical Development, Creative Arts

DAILY_BOOK:
Brown Bear, Brown Bear, What Do You See? | Bill Martin Jr.

DAILY_SONG:
The Animals in the Forest

CIRCLE_TIME:
Offer two woodland animal choices, sing the chosen animal's verse, and repeat the infant's favorite movement or sound.

OUTDOOR_PLAY:
Hold a calm woodland parade through stroller movement, caregiver-carried movement, crawling, or supported walking.

DAILY_OBSERVATIONS:
Document preferred animals, clear choices, recognition of repeated materials, communication attempts, and new motor skills.

DAILY_ADAPTATIONS:
Offer a quiet one-on-one celebration for infants who need less stimulation.

SAFETY_NOTES:
Maintain direct supervision, routine care schedules, safe outdoor conditions, and safe sleep practices.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Choose a Woodland Friend
CATEGORY:
Open-Ended Exploration
OBJECTIVE:
Encourage intentional choice-making and early communication.
DESCRIPTION:
Infants choose between two familiar woodland animals and lead a short responsive play exchange.
MATERIALS:
Two large animal puppets, basket
SETUP:
Place choices within view and reach with clear space between them.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Honor gaze, reach, body orientation, sound, gesture, or sign as a valid choice.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“You looked at owl. You chose owl. More owl or all done?”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Present two animals.
2. Wait quietly for a response.
3. Name the selected animal.
4. Make its sound or movement and pause.
5. Offer another turn or end according to cues.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Agency, communication, memory, joint attention, social connection
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Observe consistent gaze, reaching, vocalization, gestures, and requests for repetition.
VOCABULARY:
Choose, favorite, owl, bear, more, all done
EXTENSIONS:
Let mobile infants carry or place the chosen animal in a basket.
ADAPTATIONS:
Present one item at a time when two choices are overwhelming.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Use only large intact washable materials.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: gaze or body orientation; 6-12 months: reach, point, retrieve, exchange, or sign.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Forest Music and Movement Review
CATEGORY:
Music & Movement
OBJECTIVE:
Integrate rhythm, imitation, body awareness, and social enjoyment.
DESCRIPTION:
Infants revisit favorite woodland motions with shakers, scarves, crawling, lap movement, or supported standing.
MATERIALS:
Infant-safe shakers, green scarves, animal puppets
SETUP:
Clear a safe movement area and keep scarves in adult hands unless directly supervised.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Match movement to ability, offer rest, and avoid forced participation.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“Hop like bunny. Crawl like bear. Hoot like owl. Stop and rest.”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Begin with a quiet welcome beat.
2. Show one animal and model one movement.
3. Help infants participate through tracking, kicking, reaching, shaking, crawling, or standing.
4. Pause between animals for response and rest.
5. End with a slow goodbye verse and all done.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Rhythm, imitation, gross motor development, regulation, social connection
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Observe movement imitation, instrument grasp, balance, excitement, fatigue, and recovery.
VOCABULARY:
Hop, crawl, hoot, shake, stop, rest
EXTENSIONS:
Invite older infants to choose the next animal card.
ADAPTATIONS:
Use humming and slow puppet movement for infants sensitive to sound or motion.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Use sealed infant shakers, supervise scarves, and support all standing movement.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: lap rhythm, tracking, and assisted movement; 6-12 months: shake, crawl, bounce, pull to stand, choose, and cruise.
