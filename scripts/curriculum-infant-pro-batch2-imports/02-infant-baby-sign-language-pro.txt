WEEKLY_OVERVIEW:
Infants build early communication through responsive caregiving and repeated exposure to useful signs such as more, milk, eat, all done, help, and sleep. Teachers pair each sign with spoken words, facial expressions, objects, routines, and pauses so infants can respond through gaze, movement, vocalization, reaching, gesture, or emerging signs. The goal is connection and understanding, never requiring infants to perform a sign.

LEARNING_OBJECTIVES:
Strengthen receptive language and joint attention
Introduce meaningful signs connected to daily routines
Encourage turn-taking, gesture imitation, and vocal communication
Help caregivers recognize each infant's individual communication cues
Support emotional security by responding consistently to needs
Build hand awareness and purposeful movement

FAMILY_CONNECTION:
Share the week's signs with families and encourage them to use the same spoken word and sign during natural routines. Remind families that every look, reach, sound, body movement, and gesture is communication and that signs should support speech rather than replace it.

OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Observe eye contact, shared attention, anticipation during routines, imitation of hand movements, response to spoken words, use of gaze or reach to choose, emerging gestures, vocal turn-taking, and reduced frustration when adults respond consistently.

ADAPTATIONS:
Model signs slowly within the infant's visual field without moving the infant's hands into position. Use fewer signs at one time for infants who need reduced stimulation. Pair signs with real objects and predictable routines. Accept all communication attempts and respond immediately when possible.

MONDAY

DAILY_THEME:
More and All Done

DAILY_OBJECTIVES:
Introduce two contrasting communication concepts
Encourage choice-making and turn-taking
Help teachers identify each infant's signals for continuation and stopping

DAILY_VOCABULARY:
More, all done, again, stop, choose

DAILY_MATERIALS:
Favorite toys, bubbles used by adult, photo cards, mirror

DAILY_LEARNING_DOMAINS:
Language & Literacy, Social Emotional, Cognitive Development, Fine Motor

DAILY_BOOK:
Baby Signs | Joy Allen

DAILY_SONG:
More Please Song

CIRCLE_TIME:
Show the signs for more and all done while singing a short repetitive song. Pause after each verse and honor any infant response as communication.

OUTDOOR_PLAY:
Pause a stroller walk or blanket game and model more before continuing and all done before transitioning.

DAILY_OBSERVATIONS:
Notice how each infant communicates continue, stop, interest, fatigue, and frustration.

DAILY_ADAPTATIONS:
Use gaze choices for younger infants and object choices for mobile infants. Keep adult sign movements slow and clear.

SAFETY_NOTES:
Adults control bubbles at all times. Never force hand movements or continue an activity after distress cues.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
More or All Done Bubble Turns
CATEGORY:
Social Emotional
OBJECTIVE:
Build turn-taking and connect communication with predictable adult responses.
DESCRIPTION:
An adult blows a small group of bubbles, pauses, models more, and watches for the infant's response before continuing or ending.
MATERIALS:
Bubbles, more and all done photo cards
SETUP:
Seat or position infants safely while the adult remains in control of the bubble container.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Model signs, pause long enough for a response, name the infant's communication, and honor all done cues.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“Bubbles. More? You reached. More bubbles. You turned away. All done.”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Blow a small group of bubbles away from the infant's face.
2. Pause and model more while saying the word.
3. Watch for gaze, smile, reach, movement, sound, or sign.
4. Continue when the infant shows interest or model all done when the infant disengages.
5. End calmly and repeat the spoken word and sign.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Joint attention, cause and effect, communication, emotional trust, turn-taking
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Record response type, waiting tolerance, anticipation, and clarity of stop signals.
VOCABULARY:
More, bubbles, again, all done, stop
EXTENSIONS:
Repeat with a favorite song, shaker, or rolling ball.
ADAPTATIONS:
Use a quiet scarf game instead of bubbles for visually or sensory-sensitive infants.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Keep solution out of reach, avoid blowing directly at faces, and wipe slippery spills immediately.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: gaze, smile, kick, or turn away; 6-12 months: reach, vocalize, imitate, or sign.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Choice Basket Communication
CATEGORY:
Open-Ended Exploration
OBJECTIVE:
Encourage intentional choice-making using gaze, reach, movement, or sign.
DESCRIPTION:
Infants choose between two familiar toys and receive the selected item immediately.
MATERIALS:
Two favorite toys, shallow basket, more and all done cards
SETUP:
Place two clean toys within view but not touching one another.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Wait quietly, interpret carefully, and avoid deciding too quickly for the infant.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“You looked at the shaker. You chose shaker. More play or all done?”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Present two toys at equal distance.
2. Wait for a clear gaze, reach, body turn, sound, or gesture.
3. Name the chosen item and give it to the infant.
4. After a short play turn, model more and all done.
5. Honor the infant's response and repeat as appropriate.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Agency, decision-making, receptive language, communication, trust
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Observe consistency of choices, response time, reaching accuracy, and frustration level.
VOCABULARY:
Choose, toy, more, all done, play
EXTENSIONS:
Let mobile infants place the chosen item back in the basket when finished.
ADAPTATIONS:
Offer one item at a time for infants overwhelmed by two choices.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Use only large age-appropriate toys and sanitize mouthed materials.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: gaze or body orientation; 6-12 months: reach, point, retrieve, exchange, or sign.

TUESDAY

DAILY_THEME:
Milk and Eat

DAILY_OBJECTIVES:
Connect signs to familiar feeding routines
Strengthen anticipation and receptive language
Support calm communication before and during meals

DAILY_VOCABULARY:
Milk, eat, hungry, cup, spoon, finished

DAILY_MATERIALS:
Normal feeding supplies, soft doll, milk and eat photo cards, board book

DAILY_LEARNING_DOMAINS:
Language & Literacy, Social Emotional, Cognitive Development, Fine Motor

DAILY_BOOK:
My First Signs | Annie Kubler

DAILY_SONG:
Milk and Eat Routine Song

CIRCLE_TIME:
Use a soft doll to model milk and eat signs, naming each action slowly and keeping the experience separate from actual feeding unless it matches an infant's routine.

OUTDOOR_PLAY:
Before or after an outdoor bottle or snack routine, model the appropriate sign and describe what is happening.

DAILY_OBSERVATIONS:
Observe anticipation, calming response to predictable language, hand movement, mouth movement, and feeding cues.

DAILY_ADAPTATIONS:
Always follow each infant's feeding plan. Use signs only as supportive language and never delay food while waiting for a response.

SAFETY_NOTES:
Follow all feeding, allergy, bottle, choking-prevention, and sanitation procedures. Infants must remain appropriately positioned and supervised.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Feeding Routine Sign Talk
CATEGORY:
Language & Literacy
OBJECTIVE:
Build receptive language and reduce uncertainty during feeding routines.
DESCRIPTION:
Teachers pair milk, eat, more, and all done signs with the infant's regular feeding sequence.
MATERIALS:
Infant's approved feeding supplies, photo cards if useful
SETUP:
Prepare feeding materials according to the child's individual plan before beginning.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Use calm predictable language, respond to hunger and fullness cues, and never require signing.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“Milk is ready. You are drinking milk. More? Your body says all done.”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Model milk or eat immediately before offering the approved feeding.
2. Name the food or bottle while the infant sees it.
3. Pause naturally between portions and model more.
4. Watch for hunger and fullness cues.
5. Model all done when the feeding ends and transition calmly.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Routine comprehension, receptive language, emotional regulation, communication
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Observe anticipation, eye contact, hand movement, acceptance, refusal, and fullness signals.
VOCABULARY:
Milk, eat, more, all done, hungry
EXTENSIONS:
Share the exact signs and wording with families for consistency.
ADAPTATIONS:
Use photo supports for infants who respond strongly to visual routines.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Never delay feeding to elicit a sign. Follow the child's medical and feeding plan exactly.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: receptive exposure and cue recognition; 6-12 months: possible imitation, gesture, or sign use.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Feed the Baby Doll
CATEGORY:
Dramatic Play
OBJECTIVE:
Connect familiar routine words and signs through simple imitation play.
DESCRIPTION:
Infants watch and participate as a caregiver pretends to feed a soft doll using eat, milk, more, and all done language.
MATERIALS:
Washable soft doll, empty large spoon, empty cup
SETUP:
Place the doll and props on a clean floor mat.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Model one simple action at a time and allow infants to touch, hold, or imitate.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“Baby eats. More? Baby is all done. Can you touch the spoon?”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Show the doll and say baby.
2. Pretend to offer the doll a spoon or cup.
3. Model eat or milk.
4. Invite the infant to hold the spoon, touch the doll, or copy the motion.
5. Finish by modeling all done and placing props in the basket.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Imitation, symbolic understanding, fine motor control, language comprehension
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Notice attention to the doll, imitation attempts, grasp, release, and response to routine words.
VOCABULARY:
Baby, eat, milk, spoon, all done
EXTENSIONS:
Add a cloth for pretending to wipe the doll's mouth.
ADAPTATIONS:
Use only the doll and spoken signs when props are distracting.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Use oversized props with no detachable parts and supervise all mouthing.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: watch, touch, and grasp; 6-12 months: imitate feeding, pass props, and place items in a basket.

WEDNESDAY

DAILY_THEME:
Help and Please

DAILY_OBJECTIVES:
Teach that communication can bring supportive adult help
Encourage safe problem-solving and persistence
Build trust through responsive assistance

DAILY_VOCABULARY:
Help, please, open, stuck, try, thank you

DAILY_MATERIALS:
Clear containers with loose lids, favorite toys, scarves, help card, mirror

DAILY_LEARNING_DOMAINS:
Social Emotional, Cognitive Development, Fine Motor, Language & Literacy

DAILY_BOOK:
Baby Signs | Joy Allen

DAILY_SONG:
If You're Happy and You Know It

CIRCLE_TIME:
Model help while opening a container, then thank you after another adult or puppet assists.

OUTDOOR_PLAY:
Use help naturally during supported standing, stroller transitions, or reaching for a safe toy.

DAILY_OBSERVATIONS:
Observe persistence, frustration cues, help-seeking gaze, reaching toward adults, and response to assistance.

DAILY_ADAPTATIONS:
Create only mild, brief challenges and assist before frustration escalates.

SAFETY_NOTES:
Never trap fingers or use tightly sealed containers. Keep all materials large and directly supervised.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Help Me Open It
CATEGORY:
Fine Motor
OBJECTIVE:
Connect help-seeking communication with successful adult support.
DESCRIPTION:
A favorite toy is placed in a clear container with a loose lid so the infant can attempt access and receive help promptly.
MATERIALS:
Clear infant-safe container with loose lid, favorite large toy, help card
SETUP:
Place the toy in the container and confirm the lid releases easily without pinching.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Allow a brief attempt, model help, respond before distress, and celebrate communication.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“The toy is inside. You are trying. Help? I can help. Open.”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Show the infant the toy inside the container.
2. Allow touching, turning, or pulling for a brief period.
3. Model help while holding the container near the infant's view.
4. Open it immediately after any help-seeking cue.
5. Give the toy and say, “I helped. You got it.”
LEARNING_GOALS:
Problem-solving, persistence, help-seeking, cause and effect, trust
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Observe attempts, gaze shifts to adult, vocalization, gesture, frustration threshold, and recovery.
VOCABULARY:
Help, open, inside, try, got it
EXTENSIONS:
Let older infants replace the toy and close the loose lid.
ADAPTATIONS:
Use a scarf partially covering the toy for infants who need an easier challenge.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Container must be large, smooth, unbreakable, and free from pinch points.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: reach and adult-assisted reveal; 6-12 months: manipulate lid, request help, retrieve, and replace.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Help Sign Mirror Imitation
CATEGORY:
Social Emotional
OBJECTIVE:
Develop awareness of hand movements, faces, and reciprocal imitation.
DESCRIPTION:
Caregiver and infant watch simple help and please gestures in an unbreakable mirror.
MATERIALS:
Unbreakable mirror, help and please cards
SETUP:
Secure the mirror at floor level or hold it safely during lap play.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Copy the infant first, then model one sign slowly without moving the infant's hands.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“I see your hands. Help. Please. You moved your hand. I saw you.”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Help the infant notice their reflection.
2. Copy a natural hand movement the infant makes.
3. Model help slowly near the caregiver's own chest.
4. Pause for any response.
5. Repeat with please only if the infant remains engaged.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Self-awareness, imitation, joint attention, communication
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Observe mirror interest, hand watching, imitation, smiles, and vocal turn-taking.
VOCABULARY:
Hands, help, please, mirror, you
EXTENSIONS:
Add thank you after the adult hands over a favorite toy.
ADAPTATIONS:
Use face imitation without signs for infants who need simpler input.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Use only an infant-safe unbreakable mirror with secure edges.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: observe and imitate facial expression; 6-12 months: imitate hand movement or emerging sign.

DAILY_THEME:
Sleep and Book

DAILY_OBJECTIVES:
Connect signs with calming routines
Build early book enjoyment and anticipation
Support emotional regulation through predictable language

DAILY_VOCABULARY:
Sleep, tired, book, read, quiet, good night

DAILY_MATERIALS:
Board books, soft doll, small blanket used only during supervised play, sleep and book cards

DAILY_LEARNING_DOMAINS:
Language & Literacy, Social Emotional, Cognitive Development

DAILY_BOOK:
Ten Tiny Babies | Karen Katz

DAILY_SONG:
Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star

CIRCLE_TIME:
Model book before reading and sleep while helping a doll through a pretend bedtime routine.

OUTDOOR_PLAY:

DAILY_OBSERVATIONS:
Observe calming responses, anticipation when seeing a book, page attention, and fatigue cues.

Use very short books, one page at a time, and stop when infants turn away.

SAFETY_NOTES:

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Book Sign Story Choice
CATEGORY:
Language & Literacy
OBJECTIVE:
Encourage book choice, shared reading, and receptive language.
DESCRIPTION:
Infants choose between two board books while the teacher models book and read.
MATERIALS:
Two board books, book photo card
SETUP:
Place books within view and safe reach.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Honor gaze or reach as a choice and keep reading responsive and brief.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“Book. You chose this book. Let's read. Turn the page.”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Present two board books.
2. Wait for gaze, reach, touch, or movement toward one.
3. Name the selection and model book.
4. Read a few pages with pauses for touching and vocalizing.
5. Model all done when the infant disengages.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Book awareness, choice-making, joint attention, receptive language
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Observe choice consistency, page attention, grasp, page-turn attempts, and vocal response.
VOCABULARY:
Book, read, page, choose, all done
EXTENSIONS:
Invite mobile infants to return the book to a basket.
ADAPTATIONS:
Present one high-contrast book for younger infants.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Inspect books for torn pieces and supervise mouthing.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: look, listen, and touch; 6-12 months: choose, hold, turn thick pages, and return.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Doll Bedtime Routine
CATEGORY:
Dramatic Play
OBJECTIVE:
Connect sleep language and signs to a calm predictable sequence.
DESCRIPTION:
Infants watch or participate in a simple pretend bedtime routine for a soft doll.
MATERIALS:
Soft doll, small supervised-play cloth, sleep card
SETUP:
Place the doll on a clean mat with the cloth beside it.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Use a calm voice, model sleep, and connect the routine to familiar caregiving language.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“Baby is tired. Sleep. Good night, baby. The blanket comes off when play is done.”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Show the doll and say baby is tired.
2. Model the sleep sign.
3. Let the infant pat, touch, or hold the doll.
4. Sing one quiet verse of Twinkle, Twinkle.
5. Remove the cloth and place all items away before transition.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Routine understanding, symbolic play, emotional regulation, imitation
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Notice calming, patting, vocalization, sign attention, and imitation.
VOCABULARY:
Sleep, tired, baby, quiet, good night
EXTENSIONS:
Add a board book before the pretend sleep routine.
ADAPTATIONS:
Use only the doll and song when the cloth is distracting.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Never place loose blankets or dolls in an infant sleep space. This is awake supervised play only.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: observe, listen, and touch; 6-12 months: pat doll, imitate sleep gesture, and place doll on mat.

FRIDAY

DAILY_THEME:
Communication Celebration

DAILY_OBJECTIVES:
Review useful signs and routine words
Celebrate all forms of infant communication
Document individual cues and emerging skills

DAILY_VOCABULARY:
More, all done, milk, eat, help, sleep, book

DAILY_MATERIALS:
Week's photo cards, favorite toys, books, mirror, shakers

DAILY_LEARNING_DOMAINS:
Language & Literacy, Social Emotional, Cognitive Development, Fine Motor, Music & Movement

DAILY_BOOK:
My First Signs | Annie Kubler

DAILY_SONG:
If You're Happy and You Know It

CIRCLE_TIME:
Review three or four signs through songs and familiar objects, keeping the pace slow and following each infant's engagement.

OUTDOOR_PLAY:
Use more, help, and all done naturally during outdoor movement and transitions.

DAILY_OBSERVATIONS:
Document each infant's clearest communication methods, preferred signs or routines, and adult responses that work best.

DAILY_ADAPTATIONS:
Limit review to the signs most meaningful to each infant and provide quiet one-on-one interaction as needed.

SAFETY_NOTES:
Communication practice must never delay care, feeding, comfort, or sleep routines.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
My Communication Favorites
CATEGORY:
Open-Ended Exploration
OBJECTIVE:
Identify and celebrate each infant's preferred communication methods.
DESCRIPTION:
Teachers offer familiar objects and respond to gaze, reach, movement, sound, gesture, or sign while reviewing useful words.
MATERIALS:
Favorite toy, book, shaker, photo cards
SETUP:
Arrange two familiar choices in a calm area.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Observe carefully, respond consistently, and document what the infant communicates and how.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“You looked at the book. Book. You want more. I understand you.”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Offer two familiar choices.
2. Wait for the infant's communication.
3. Name the choice and model the related sign.
4. Respond immediately and continue briefly.
5. Record the cue and whether the infant repeated it.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Communication, agency, memory, trust, receptive language
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Record gaze, reach, gesture, sound, sign, persistence, and response to adult interpretation.
VOCABULARY:
Choose, more, help, book, all done
EXTENSIONS:
Create a family note listing the infant's strongest current communication cues.
ADAPTATIONS:
Use one object and one sign at a time for infants needing reduced stimulation.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Use only familiar age-appropriate objects and maintain direct supervision.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: gaze, facial expression, body movement, and vocalization; 6-12 months: reach, point, exchange, gesture, and sign.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Sign and Song Review
CATEGORY:
Music & Movement
OBJECTIVE:
Reinforce communication through rhythm, repetition, gestures, and responsive interaction.
DESCRIPTION:
A short song incorporates more, all done, help, book, and sleep signs with matching objects or pictures.
MATERIALS:
Photo cards, shaker, board book, soft doll
SETUP:
Seat infants safely with caregivers and place materials within adult reach.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Sing slowly, model clearly, pause for responses, and reduce stimulation when needed.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
“More song? Help with the shaker. Book time. All done.”
DIRECTIONS:
1. Begin with a quiet welcome beat.
2. Show one object and model its sign.
3. Sing a short repeated line using the word.
4. Pause for any infant response.
5. Finish with all done and a calm goodbye.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Listening, imitation, rhythm, receptive language, social connection
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Observe anticipation, hand movement, vocal participation, attention, and regulation.
VOCABULARY:
More, help, book, sleep, all done
EXTENSIONS:
Send families a short list of the signs practiced most successfully.
ADAPTATIONS:
Use humming and one sign at a time for infants sensitive to group music.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Use sealed infant-safe instruments and supervise all materials.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
0-6 months: watch, listen, and respond with body movement; 6-12 months: imitate gestures, shake, choose, or sign.
