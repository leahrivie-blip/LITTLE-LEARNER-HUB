Lesson title:
Tiny Artist Studio

Age band:
Infant 0–6 Months

Weekly overview:
Tiny Artist Studio introduces babies to their first creative experiences through adorable caregiver-assisted handprint and footprint keepsakes, mess-free color exploration, safe sensory art, movement, texture, and visual discovery. Babies are never expected to complete a craft. Instead, caregivers build meaningful art experiences around natural infant behaviors such as looking, kicking, reaching, opening and closing hands, patting, pressing, and responding to new sensations. The week combines cute family-worthy keepsakes with genuine developmental experiences involving body awareness, sensory processing, visual attention, cause and effect, communication, and caregiver bonding.

Learning objectives:
Explore color through safe visual and sensory experiences.
Build awareness of hands, fingers, feet, and toes.
Practice natural reaching, kicking, patting, and pressing movements.
Experience simple cause and effect.
Strengthen visual attention and tracking.
Experience contrasting textures.
Respond to caregiver narration and facial expressions.
Build caregiver-infant connection through one-to-one interaction.
Show preferences through gaze, movement, expression, or vocalization.
Participate in early creative experiences without pressure for a finished product.

Teacher preparation / Toolkit:
Prepare everything before bringing the infant into an art experience. Handprint and footprint activities should be short and caregiver-led. Never force a baby's fingers or toes open or attempt repeated prints just to make the artwork look perfect. Have warm washcloths ready before paint touches skin. Use very small amounts of program-approved washable paint. Any sensory bag must be heavy duty, completely sealed, inspected, and secured to the activity surface before baby has access. Remove it immediately if damaged. Focus documentation on how the baby looked, moved, reacted, reached, kicked, vocalized, or repeated an action.

Prep checklist:
Prepare paint.
Prepare cardstock.
Cover surfaces.
Prepare warm washcloths.
Inspect and secure sensory bags.
Gather large safe texture materials.
Sanitize toy car and roller.
Prepare large color cards.
Prepare drying area.
Prepare camera if allowed.
Remove choking hazards.
Confirm all art materials meet program policy.

Observation focus:
Notice responses to new textures.
Watch for hand opening and closing.
Observe kicking and toe movement.
Notice which colors hold visual attention.
Observe reaching toward materials.
Watch for repeated patting or pressing.
Notice responses to cool or wet sensations.
Listen for coos and vocalizations.
Watch for repeated actions after an interesting result.
Observe signs of enjoyment, uncertainty, overstimulation, or disengagement.

Family connection:
Send home at least one meaningful handprint or footprint keepsake from the week. When appropriate, explain the development behind the experience, such as sensory exploration, body awareness, visual attention, or cause and effect. If program policy permits, pair an artwork with a photograph of the baby participating.

Monday:
Activity name: My First Handprint
Category/developmental domain: Sensory Exploration and Body Awareness
Recommended age: Infant 0–6 Months
Estimated duration: 3–5 minutes
Activity objective: Introduce gentle tactile exploration while helping baby notice their hand and fingers.
What children will do:
Baby feels a very small amount of washable paint on the palm while a caregiver gently creates one natural handprint.
Materials:
Washable non-toxic paint
Heavy cardstock
Warm washcloth
Protective surface
Teacher preparation:
Have cardstock, paint, and a warm washcloth completely ready before beginning.
Setup:
Hold or position baby securely with continuous adult support.
Step-by-step directions:
Talk about baby's hand.
Allow baby to see the caregiver's face and hear calm narration.
Apply a thin amount of paint to the palm.
Pause and observe baby's reaction.
If baby remains comfortable, gently bring the hand to cardstock.
Allow the natural hand position to create the print.
Lift the hand without forcing fingers open.
Clean immediately.
Suggested questions to ask:
Here is your hand.
Look at your little fingers.
Do you feel that?
Press.
You made a mark.
Learning and observation focus:
Observe tactile response, natural hand opening, visual attention, and caregiver interaction.
Safety and supervision:
Never force fingers open. Stop for distress, mouthing, or skin irritation.
Cleanup:
Wash baby's hand thoroughly and sanitize the area.
Teacher tips:
One imperfect print is enough.
Vocabulary:
Hand
Finger
Touch
Paint
Press
Mark

Activity name: Squishy Hand Painting
Category/developmental domain: Sensory Exploration and Cause and Effect
Recommended age: Infant 0–6 Months
Estimated duration: 5–8 minutes
Activity objective: Explore movement and color through a safe mess-free sensory experience.
What children will do:
Baby presses, pats, or rests their hands against a securely sealed paint sensory bag.
Materials:
Heavy-duty sensory bag
Washable paint
Adult-only sealing tape
White cardstock underneath
Teacher preparation:
Add small paint amounts, remove excess air, completely seal the bag, and secure it to the surface.
Setup:
Place the secured bag within baby's natural reach.
Step-by-step directions:
Position baby safely.
Allow baby's hand to naturally contact the bag.
Pause.
Describe the color moving.
Model one gentle press if needed.
Allow spontaneous patting or pressing.
Suggested questions to ask:
Look at the color move.
Press.
Pat, pat.
You made it squish.
Learning and observation focus:
Cause and effect, hand movement, visual tracking, repeated action.
Safety and supervision:
A loose plastic bag must never be accessible. Inspect continuously.
Cleanup:
Remove the sensory bag immediately after use.
Teacher tips:
Give baby time instead of repeatedly moving their hand for them.
Vocabulary:
Squish
Press
Pat
Color
Move
Hand
Image requirement: required
Setup example brief:
Infant pressing a securely taped sensory paint bag.

Activity name: Baby Chooses a Color
Category/developmental domain: Visual Attention and Early Choice Making
Recommended age: Infant 0–6 Months
Estimated duration: 5 minutes
Activity objective: Observe early visual preferences.
What children will do:
Baby views two large contrasting color cards while caregiver watches for sustained gaze, head turn, reaching, or movement toward one.
Materials:
Two large color cards
Matching washable paint
Paper
Teacher preparation:
Choose two clearly different colors.
Setup:
Hold both cards within baby's comfortable visual range.
Step-by-step directions:
Show both colors.
Name each.
Pause.
Observe gaze.
Switch positions and show again.
Use the color that received the strongest repeated attention in one simple caregiver-assisted mark.
Suggested questions to ask:
Here is yellow.
Here is blue.
You are looking at yellow.
I see where you're looking.
Learning and observation focus:
Gaze duration, head turning, reaching, visual preference.
Safety and supervision:
Caregiver holds all color cards.
Cleanup:
Store materials.
Teacher tips:
Record today's observed preference rather than labeling it baby's permanent favorite.
Vocabulary:

Blue
Choose

Shallow tray
