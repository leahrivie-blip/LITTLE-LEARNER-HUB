WEEKLY_OVERVIEW:
High-contrast black-and-white patterns support early visual focus and tracking. The week centers on looking, tummy-time visuals, faces, short books, songs, and caregiver talk — not crafts or worksheets.

LEARNING_OBJECTIVES:
Strengthen visual focus with simple high-contrast patterns
Practice slow tracking of bold cards/objects
Support tummy-time looking with contrast visuals
Use face cards and caregiver faces as primary targets
Offer safe reaching toward large contrast toys when ready

WEEKLY_MATERIALS:
High-contrast pattern cards (printable draft)
Face cards (printable draft)
Tummy-time visual strip (printable draft)
High-contrast board/soft books
Baby-safe mirror
Black/white fabric squares
Large soft contrast ring/toy

Hello Black Hello White (LLH original)

FAMILY_CONNECTION:
Share that simple black-and-white cards during alert awake time can support looking — always with your face and voice.

OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Fixates on high-contrast pattern
Tracks slow card movement
Looks at face card or caregiver face
Lifts head toward tummy-time visuals

ADAPTATIONS:
Younger infants need closer (8–12 in), slower moves, fewer cards. Reduce if overstimulated. Older infants may reach for large contrast toys.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
High-Contrast Card Focus

CATEGORY:
Sensory Play

OBJECTIVE:
Support visual focus with one bold black-and-white card.

DESCRIPTION:
Baby looks at a single high-contrast card held 8–12 inches away while you describe the pattern.

MATERIALS:
High-contrast pattern card (printable)
Mat

SETUP:
Baby comfortable on mat; card ready.

TEACHER_ROLE:
One card at a time; put extras away.

TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
Look — black and white stripes.

DIRECTIONS:
1. Hold card steady in view
2. Allow time to focus
3. Move slightly side to side
4. Name pattern simply (stripes/spots)
5. Rest eyes

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Bold Card Gaze Garden

CATEGORY:
Sensory Play

OBJECTIVE:
Offer a calm sequence of 2–3 contrast cards for gazing.

DESCRIPTION:
You present one card at a time in a quiet ‘garden’ of visuals, watching for focus and break cues.

MATERIALS:
2–3 pattern cards
Mat

SETUP:
Sit with cards face down; show one at a time.

TEACHER_ROLE:
Gaze aversion means break, not ‘try harder’.

TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
New pattern… watching.

DIRECTIONS:
1. Show card 1 still
2. Allow gaze
3. Switch to card 2
4. Optional card 3 if still alert
5. Stop at first fatigue cue

LEARNING_GOALS:
Attention regulation
Visual focus

OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Maintains calm alert looking; switches attention.

VOCABULARY:

new
pattern

EXTENSIONS:

ADAPTATIONS:

SAFETY_NOTES:
No card stacks within baby’s kick zone as clutter.

Infant 0–6 months

DESCRIPTION:
Move a patterned card in a slow left–center–right arc while baby watches.

MATERIALS:
Tracking card (printable)
Mat

SETUP:
Baby on back; card in starting left position.

TEACHER_ROLE:
If baby loses track, shorten the arc.

TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
Watch… moving slowly.

DIRECTIONS:

2. Move slowly left to center
3. Pause
4. Move slowly to right
