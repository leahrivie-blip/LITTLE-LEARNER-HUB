TITLE:
The People Who Love Me

AGE_GROUP:
Toddler

THEME:
Family Connections — The People Who Love Me

PLAN:
Pro

STATUS:
published

LEARNING_DOMAINS:
Social Emotional, Language & Literacy, Physical Development, Science, Creative Arts

WEEKLY_OVERVIEW:
Toddlers explore important people through stories, songs, dramatic play, art, and hands-on experiences. Every family is unique as children build communication, social-emotional skills, and a sense of belonging.

LEARNING_OBJECTIVES:
Recognize and talk about family members.
Build positive relationships with peers and teachers.
Strengthen communication and vocabulary.
Develop fine and gross motor skills.
Explore art, sensory, and dramatic play.
Participate in group activities.

WEEKLY_MATERIALS:
Family photos
Baby dolls
Doll clothes
Blankets
Toy kitchen
Toy phones
Mirrors
Play houses
Washable paint
Cardstock
Dot markers
Playdough
Cookie cutters
Blocks
Scarves
Instruments
Sensory bin materials
Large paper
Crayons
Glue sticks
Stickers

VOCABULARY:
Family
Love
Home
Together
Friend

BOOKS:
The Family Book | Todd Parr
Families, Families, Families! | Suzanne Lang
I Love You Through and Through | Bernadette Rossetti-Shustak
My Family, Your Family | Lisa Bullard
Everywhere Babies | Susan Meyers

SONGS:
Skidamarink
Finger Family Song
The More We Get Together
If You're Happy and You Know It
You Are My Sunshine

FAMILY_CONNECTION:
Ask families to send a favorite family photo and share one tradition. Display photos at child height.

OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Observe family vocabulary, dramatic play, social interactions, fine motor, gross motor, communication, songs/stories, and interest in family photos.

ADAPTATIONS:
Younger Toddlers (12–24 Months): one-on-one support, simpler art, shorter groups, larger manipulatives. Older Toddlers (24–36 Months): longer conversations, sorting/matching, independence, and imaginative dramatic play.

MONDAY

DAILY_THEME:
Family Photos and Loving Faces

DAILY_OBJECTIVES:
Look at family photos and name people they see.
Explore family dramatic play materials.
Practice fine and gross motor skills through art and movement.

DAILY_VOCABULARY:
Family, Love, Home, Together, Friend

DAILY_MATERIALS:
Family photos
Baby dolls
Blankets
Toy kitchen
Crayons
Paper
Playdough
Cookie cutters
Stickers
Scarves

DAILY_LEARNING_DOMAINS:
Social Emotional, Language & Literacy, Physical Development, Science, Creative Arts

DAILY_BOOK:
The Family Book | Todd Parr

DAILY_SONG:
Skidamarink

CIRCLE_TIME:
Look at family photos together and invite each child to share who they see. Read The Family Book and pause for names, smiles, and simple responses.

OUTDOOR_PLAY:
Take a neighborhood walk to look at homes and talk about families who live inside.

DAILY_OBSERVATIONS:
Notice family vocabulary, photo interest, dramatic play, fine motor grasping, and movement participation.

DAILY_ADAPTATIONS:
Younger toddlers: one photo at a time and hand-over-hand support. Older toddlers: encourage naming and longer conversations.

SAFETY_NOTES:
Secure photos and supervise kitchen props; use washable art materials only.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Family Dramatic Play Invitation
CATEGORY:
Dramatic Play
OBJECTIVE:
Explore family roles through doll and kitchen play.
DESCRIPTION:
Toddlers pretend to care for baby dolls, cook, and create family routines with blankets and kitchen props.
MATERIALS:
Baby dolls
Doll clothes
Blankets
Toy kitchen
Toy phones
SETUP:
Prepare materials at child height and clear enough space for movement before toddlers arrive.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Stay nearby, model language, and support toddlers as they explore, share, and try new skills.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
"Look. family. You are trying. I see you working. We can do it together."
DIRECTIONS:
1. Set out materials at child height and invite toddlers to explore.
2. Model the first step with simple words and gestures.
3. Support each child as they try, naming actions and vocabulary.
4. Encourage turn-taking, effort, and peer awareness when appropriate.
5. Clean up together and transition with a short song or routine.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Dramatic Play skills, language development, social-emotional growth, and active participation.
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Note engagement, vocabulary use, motor skills, peer interactions, and independence.
VOCABULARY:
Family, Baby, Cook, Home
EXTENSIONS:
Invite children to revisit the activity later during choice time or with a peer partner.
ADAPTATIONS:
Offer more one-on-one support and simpler steps for younger toddlers; extend conversations and independence for older toddlers.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Use washable, toddler-safe materials. Supervise closely during movement, sensory, and art activities.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
Younger Toddlers (12–24 Months): shorter groups, larger manipulatives, more teacher modeling, and simpler choices. Older Toddlers (24–36 Months): longer conversations, sorting/matching, cooperative play, and more independent exploration.

DIRECTIONS:
1. Set out materials at child height and invite toddlers to explore.
2. Model the first step with simple words and gestures.
3. Support each child as they try, naming actions and vocabulary.
4. Encourage turn-taking, effort, and peer awareness when appropriate.
5. Clean up together and transition with a short song or routine.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Gross Motor skills, language development, social-emotional growth, and active participation.
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Note engagement, vocabulary use, motor skills, peer interactions, and independence.
VOCABULARY:
Run, Pop, Reach, Go
EXTENSIONS:
Invite children to revisit the activity later during choice time or with a peer partner.
ADAPTATIONS:
Offer more one-on-one support and simpler steps for younger toddlers; extend conversations and independence for older toddlers.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Use washable, toddler-safe materials. Supervise closely during movement, sensory, and art activities.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
Younger Toddlers (12–24 Months): shorter groups, larger manipulatives, more teacher modeling, and simpler choices. Older Toddlers (24–36 Months): longer conversations, sorting/matching, cooperative play, and more independent exploration.
