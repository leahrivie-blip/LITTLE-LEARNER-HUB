TITLE:
My Home & My Family

AGE_GROUP:
Toddler

THEME:
Family Connections — My Home & My Family

PLAN:
Pro

STATUS:
published

LEARNING_DOMAINS:
Social Emotional, Language & Literacy, Physical Development, Science, Creative Arts

WEEKLY_OVERVIEW:
Toddlers explore the people, routines, and spaces that make home safe and special. Every family and home is unique as children build belonging through play, art, and movement.

LEARNING_OBJECTIVES:
Identify familiar people and places at home.
Build language about home and family routines.
Strengthen fine and gross motor skills.
Engage in dramatic play with household roles.
Practice social-emotional and cooperative skills.
Create process art with creativity and expression.

WEEKLY_MATERIALS:
Toy kitchen
Baby dolls
Doll accessories
Toy furniture
Family photos
Cardboard boxes
Blocks
Washable paint
Dot markers
Crayons
Glue sticks
Construction paper
Playdough
Toy phones
Stuffed animals
Blankets
Bubble machine
Instruments
Rice/bean sensory
Toy dishes

VOCABULARY:
Home
Family
Bedroom
Kitchen
Together

BOOKS:
Home | Carson Ellis
The Family Book | Todd Parr
Goodnight Moon | Margaret Wise Brown
A House for Hermit Crab | Eric Carle
Everywhere Babies | Susan Meyers

SONGS:
Skidamarink
Twinkle Twinkle
The More We Get Together
If You're Happy and You Know It
Wheels on the Bus (family version)

FAMILY_CONNECTION:
Share one favorite thing you enjoy together at home and talk about it during circle time.

OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Observe talks about family, dramatic play, new vocabulary, fine motor, gross motor balance, social skills, problem-solving, and engagement with books and songs.

ADAPTATIONS:
Younger Toddlers (12–24 Months): shorter activities, larger materials, more guidance, and simple dramatic play. Older Toddlers (24–36 Months): storytelling, role-play, independence, and extended home conversations.

MONDAY

DAILY_THEME:
Homes and Who Lives Inside

DAILY_OBJECTIVES:
Talk about what homes look like and who lives inside.
Explore pretend home play.
Paint and build homes through art and blocks.

DAILY_VOCABULARY:
Home, Family, Bedroom, Kitchen, Together

DAILY_MATERIALS:
Family photos
Dolls
Blankets
Toy furniture
Sponges
Paint
Playdough
Blocks

DAILY_LEARNING_DOMAINS:
Social Emotional, Language & Literacy, Physical Development, Science, Creative Arts

DAILY_BOOK:
The Family Book | Todd Parr

DAILY_SONG:
Skidamarink

CIRCLE_TIME:
Talk about homes and who lives inside using photos and simple questions. Read The Family Book.

OUTDOOR_PLAY:
Take a neighborhood walk to look at different homes.

DAILY_OBSERVATIONS:
Notice home vocabulary, pretend play, sponge painting, block building, and scarf dancing.

DAILY_ADAPTATIONS:
Younger toddlers: larger blocks and hand-over-hand painting. Older toddlers: name rooms and extend pretend routines.

SAFETY_NOTES:
Supervise paint and ensure furniture props are stable.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Pretend Home with Dolls
CATEGORY:
Dramatic Play
OBJECTIVE:
Explore home routines with dolls, blankets, and furniture.
DESCRIPTION:
Toddlers create a pretend home with dolls, blankets, and toy furniture.
MATERIALS:
Baby dolls
Blankets
Toy furniture
Toy phones
SETUP:
Prepare materials at child height and clear enough space for movement before toddlers arrive.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Stay nearby, model language, and support toddlers as they explore, share, and try new skills.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
"Look. family. You are trying. I see you working. We can do it together."
DIRECTIONS:
1. Set out materials at child height and invite toddlers to explore.
2. Model the first step with simple words and gestures.
3. Support each child as they try, naming actions and vocabulary.
4. Encourage turn-taking, effort, and peer awareness when appropriate.
5. Clean up together and transition with a short song or routine.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Dramatic Play skills, language development, social-emotional growth, and active participation.
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Note engagement, vocabulary use, motor skills, peer interactions, and independence.
VOCABULARY:
Home, Baby, Sleep, Family
EXTENSIONS:
Invite children to revisit the activity later during choice time or with a peer partner.
ADAPTATIONS:
Offer more one-on-one support and simpler steps for younger toddlers; extend conversations and independence for older toddlers.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Use washable, toddler-safe materials. Supervise closely during movement, sensory, and art activities.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
Younger Toddlers (12–24 Months): shorter groups, larger manipulatives, more teacher modeling, and simpler choices. Older Toddlers (24–36 Months): longer conversations, sorting/matching, cooperative play, and more independent exploration.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Favorite Songs from the Week
CATEGORY:
Music & Movement
OBJECTIVE:
Sing and dance to favorite classroom songs.
DESCRIPTION:
Toddlers sing and dance to favorite songs from the week.
MATERIALS:
Music player
Scarves
SETUP:
Prepare materials at child height and clear enough space for movement before toddlers arrive.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Stay nearby, model language, and support toddlers as they explore, share, and try new skills.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:
"Look. family. You are trying. I see you working. We can do it together."
DIRECTIONS:
1. Set out materials at child height and invite toddlers to explore.
2. Model the first step with simple words and gestures.
3. Support each child as they try, naming actions and vocabulary.
4. Encourage turn-taking, effort, and peer awareness when appropriate.
5. Clean up together and transition with a short song or routine.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Music & Movement skills, language development, social-emotional growth, and active participation.
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Note engagement, vocabulary use, motor skills, peer interactions, and independence.
VOCABULARY:
Sing, Dance, Happy, Home
EXTENSIONS:
Invite children to revisit the activity later during choice time or with a peer partner.
ADAPTATIONS:
Offer more one-on-one support and simpler steps for younger toddlers; extend conversations and independence for older toddlers.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Use washable, toddler-safe materials. Supervise closely during movement, sensory, and art activities.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
Younger Toddlers (12–24 Months): shorter groups, larger manipulatives, more teacher modeling, and simpler choices. Older Toddlers (24–36 Months): longer conversations, sorting/matching, cooperative play, and more independent exploration.
