TITLE: Amazing Insects
AGE_GROUP: Toddler (2–3 Years)
THEME: Amazing Insects
PLAN: PRO
STATUS: Published
LEARNING_DOMAINS: Science; Language & Literacy; Fine Motor; Gross Motor; Math; Creative Arts; Social-Emotional Development

WEEKLY_OVERVIEW:
Toddlers explore common insects through movement, sensory play, art, sorting, simple science observations, and outdoor discovery. The week emphasizes curiosity, gentle treatment of living things, descriptive language, counting, matching, and play-based problem solving.

LEARNING_OBJECTIVES:
- Identify a few familiar insects.
- Practice counting, matching, and sorting.
- Use words such as bug, wings, crawl, fly, spot, and antenna.
- Strengthen fine and gross motor skills.
- Observe insects respectfully and safely.
- Participate in cooperative play and simple investigations.

WEEKLY_MATERIALS:
Toy insects; insect picture cards; magnifying glasses; sensory bin; shredded paper; plastic leaves; pom-poms; clothespins; playdough; pipe cleaners; washable paint; paper; dot stickers; blocks; hoops; scarves; books about insects; bug viewers used only with adult supervision.

VOCABULARY:
Insect; bug; antenna; wings; crawl; fly; ladybug; butterfly; bee; ant; beetle; spots; legs; tiny; garden.

BOOKS:
The Very Hungry Caterpillar by Eric Carle; The Very Busy Spider by Eric Carle; Waiting for Wings by Lois Ehlert; Bugs! Bugs! Bugs! by Bob Barner.

SONGS:
The Itsy Bitsy Spider; Butterfly, Butterfly; Five Little Ladybugs; The Ants Go Marching.

FAMILY_CONNECTION:
Invite families to take a short insect walk and notice one bug without touching it. Families may send a photo or drawing of what they observed.

OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Notice insect identification, vocabulary use, counting attempts, visual tracking, fine motor control, movement imitation, turn-taking, and comfort with sensory materials.

ADAPTATIONS:
Use large toy insects and picture choices; offer tools instead of direct sensory contact; reduce counting quantities; provide seated movement options; repeat simple phrases and model gestures.

MONDAY:
DAILY_THEME: Meet the Insects
DAILY_OBJECTIVES: Identify familiar insects; practice insect sounds and movements; explore textures.
DAILY_VOCABULARY: Bug; insect; crawl; fly; wings.
DAILY_MATERIALS: Toy insects; picture cards; sensory bin; magnifiers; blocks.
DAILY_LEARNING_DOMAINS: Science; Language; Fine Motor; Dramatic Play.
CIRCLE_TIME_IDEAS: Introduce toy insects one at a time. Compare how they move and sing The Ants Go Marching.
OUTDOOR_PLAY: Move like insects around cones—crawl like ants, flutter like butterflies, and buzz like bees.
DAILY_OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES: Naming insects, imitating movement, joint attention, and sensory engagement.
DAILY_ADAPTATIONS: Offer picture-pointing instead of verbal responses and allow children to watch before joining.
SAFETY_NOTES: Use only large, non-choking materials and supervise all sensory play.

ACTIVITY_1:
CATEGORY: Sensory Play
ACTIVITY_NAME: Hidden Bug Sensory Bin
OBJECTIVE: Explore textures while locating and naming insects.
DESCRIPTION: Children search through shredded paper and leaves to find large toy insects.
MATERIALS: Bin; shredded paper; silk leaves; large toy insects; scoops; cups.
SETUP: Hide insects throughout the filled bin and place tools beside it.
DIRECTIONS: 1. Invite children to scoop and search. 2. Name each insect found. 3. Compare colors and sizes. 4. Sort insects into cups. 5. Return them for repeated play.
TEACHER_ROLE: Model vocabulary, narrate actions, and ask simple open-ended questions.
LEARNING_GOALS: Sensory exploration; vocabulary; classification; fine motor control.
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES: Grasp, search strategy, naming, and sorting choices.
ADAPTATIONS: Offer tongs or a dry tray with only toy insects.
SAFETY_NOTES: Closely supervise and prevent mouthing of filler.

ACTIVITY_2:
CATEGORY: Gross Motor
ACTIVITY_NAME: Bug Movement Trail
OBJECTIVE: Imitate insect movements and follow simple directions.
DESCRIPTION: Children travel through a short path using different insect movements.
MATERIALS: Hoops; floor spots; scarves; insect cards.
SETUP: Arrange a safe path with one insect card at each station.
DIRECTIONS: 1. Show the first insect card. 2. Model its movement. 3. Invite children through the station. 4. Change movements at each card. 5. Finish with slow butterfly breathing.
TEACHER_ROLE: Demonstrate, encourage, and adjust the challenge.
LEARNING_GOALS: Balance; coordination; listening; body awareness.
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES: Motor planning, imitation, and ability to stop and start.
ADAPTATIONS: Allow seated arm movements or hand-over-hand scarf play.
SAFETY_NOTES: Keep the path clear and movements low-impact.

ACTIVITY_3:
CATEGORY: Engineering
ACTIVITY_NAME: Build a Bug Home
OBJECTIVE: Construct a simple shelter for toy insects.
DESCRIPTION: Children use blocks and loose parts to make tiny bug homes.
MATERIALS: Blocks; cardboard tubes; felt leaves; toy insects.
SETUP: Place materials on a low building surface.
DIRECTIONS: 1. Choose an insect. 2. Select building pieces. 3. Build walls or a roof. 4. Place the insect inside. 5. Test whether the home stays standing.
TEACHER_ROLE: Support problem solving without taking over.
LEARNING_GOALS: Spatial reasoning; persistence; imaginative play.
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES: Stacking, planning, and response to collapse.
ADAPTATIONS: Provide a simple base or prebuilt wall.
SAFETY_NOTES: Use large, lightweight building materials.

TUESDAY:
DAILY_THEME: Ladybugs and Spots
DAILY_OBJECTIVES: Count small groups; notice patterns; strengthen pincer grasp.
DAILY_VOCABULARY: Ladybug; red; black; spot; count.
DAILY_MATERIALS: Red paper circles; black dot stickers; playdough; pom-poms; number cards.
DAILY_LEARNING_DOMAINS: Math; Fine Motor; Art; Language.
CIRCLE_TIME_IDEAS: Count spots on a large ladybug picture and sing Five Little Ladybugs.
OUTDOOR_PLAY: Place red hoops outside and invite children to collect black beanbags as ladybug spots.
DAILY_OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES: One-to-one counting, color recognition, and sticker control.
DAILY_ADAPTATIONS: Use larger dots and count only one to three.
SAFETY_NOTES: Supervise stickers and small manipulatives.

ACTIVITY_1:
CATEGORY: Creative Arts
ACTIVITY_NAME: Dot Sticker Ladybugs
OBJECTIVE: Create a ladybug while practicing placement and counting.
DESCRIPTION: Children add black dot stickers to red paper ladybugs.
MATERIALS: Red paper ladybugs; large black stickers; crayons.
SETUP: Prepare large ladybug shapes and loosen sticker edges.
DIRECTIONS: 1. Choose a ladybug. 2. Peel a sticker. 3. Place it as a spot. 4. Count spots together. 5. Add legs or marks with crayons.
TEACHER_ROLE: Assist peeling and model counting naturally.
LEARNING_GOALS: Fine motor strength; counting; creativity.
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES: Pincer grasp, placement, and number words.
ADAPTATIONS: Use sponge stamping instead of stickers.

ACTIVITY_2:
CATEGORY: Math
ACTIVITY_NAME: Ladybug Spot Match
OBJECTIVE: Match quantities from one to three.
DESCRIPTION: Children place pom-pom spots on ladybug mats.
MATERIALS: Ladybug mats; large pom-poms; numeral or dot cards.
SETUP: Put one to three dot cards beside matching mats.
DIRECTIONS: 1. Pick a card. 2. Count its dots. 3. Add the same number of spots. 4. Check together. 5. Repeat with another card.
TEACHER_ROLE: Use one-to-one pointing and celebrate attempts.
LEARNING_GOALS: Quantity awareness; matching; counting.
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES: Stable counting and visual matching.
ADAPTATIONS: Begin with one and two only.
SAFETY_NOTES: Use oversized pom-poms and supervise.

ACTIVITY_3:
CATEGORY: Fine Motor
ACTIVITY_NAME: Ladybug Playdough
OBJECTIVE: Roll, press, and pinch while creating insects.
DESCRIPTION: Children form red playdough bodies and add large black pieces as spots.
MATERIALS: Red playdough; black dough; rollers; safe cutters.
SETUP: Portion dough and place tools on trays.
DIRECTIONS: 1. Roll a red ball. 2. Flatten it gently. 3. Pinch small black spots. 4. Press spots onto the body. 5. Count and describe the creation.
TEACHER_ROLE: Model actions and descriptive words.
LEARNING_GOALS: Hand strength; bilateral coordination; symbolic representation.
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES: Rolling, pressing, and creative choices.
ADAPTATIONS: Use softer dough and larger pieces.
SAFETY_NOTES: Ensure dough is non-toxic and not eaten.

DAILY_OBJECTIVES: Explore symmetry, color, and fluttering movement.
DAILY_VOCABULARY: Butterfly; wing; color; open; close.
DAILY_MATERIALS: Paint; folded paper; scarves; butterfly pictures; clothespins.
DAILY_LEARNING_DOMAINS: Art; Science; Gross Motor; Language.
CIRCLE_TIME_IDEAS: Look at butterfly pictures and open and close arms like wings.

DAILY_OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES: Color language, bilateral movement, and visual attention.
DAILY_ADAPTATIONS: Offer seated scarf play and limited paint colors.
SAFETY_NOTES: Use washable, non-toxic materials and clear movement space.

ACTIVITY_1:
CATEGORY: Creative Arts
ACTIVITY_NAME: Fold-Paint Butterflies
OBJECTIVE: Explore color mixing and simple symmetry.
DESCRIPTION: Children place paint on one side of folded paper and press it together.
MATERIALS: Butterfly-shaped folded paper; washable paint; smocks.
SETUP: Pre-fold paper and place small paint amounts on trays.
DIRECTIONS: 1. Add paint to one side. 2. Fold the paper. 3. Press and rub gently. 4. Open the wings. 5. Describe the colors and shapes.
TEACHER_ROLE: Support folding and introduce words such as same and both.
LEARNING_GOALS: Cause and effect; color exploration; bilateral coordination.
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES: Pressure control, surprise response, and color naming.
ADAPTATIONS: Use paint daubers or sealed paint bags.
SAFETY_NOTES: Prevent paint ingestion and clean spills promptly.

ACTIVITY_2:
CATEGORY: Gross Motor
ACTIVITY_NAME: Butterfly Flight Dance
OBJECTIVE: Practice controlled whole-body movement.
DESCRIPTION: Children use scarves as wings while moving fast, slow, high, and low.
MATERIALS: Scarves; music; flower spots.
SETUP: Spread flower spots across an open area.
DIRECTIONS: 1. Hold scarves as wings. 2. Flutter slowly. 3. Fly to a flower. 4. Rest with wings closed. 5. Repeat with different movement words.
TEACHER_ROLE: Model safe movement and contrast words.
LEARNING_GOALS: Coordination; listening; spatial awareness.
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES: Speed control, balance, and response to cues.
ADAPTATIONS: Move scarves while seated or with a partner.
SAFETY_NOTES: Maintain spacing and avoid wrapping scarves around necks.

ACTIVITY_3:
CATEGORY: Fine Motor
ACTIVITY_NAME: Clip the Butterfly Wings
OBJECTIVE: Strengthen hands using clothespins.
DESCRIPTION: Children attach clothespins around a butterfly body as colorful wing edges.
MATERIALS: Cardboard butterfly bodies; easy-open clothespins; color cards.
SETUP: Place each body and clips on an individual tray.
DIRECTIONS: 1. Choose a clip. 2. Squeeze it open. 3. Attach it to a wing. 4. Match or name colors. 5. Remove and repeat.
TEACHER_ROLE: Demonstrate grip and assist as needed.
LEARNING_GOALS: Hand strength; color matching; persistence.
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES: Grip pattern, hand preference, and frustration tolerance.
ADAPTATIONS: Use large chip clips or Velcro pieces.
SAFETY_NOTES: Supervise clips closely and check for pinching.

DAILY_OBJECTIVES: Practice cooperation, carrying, line movement, and simple counting.
DAILY_VOCABULARY: Ant; line; carry; together; nest.
DAILY_MATERIALS: Black counters; tunnels; blocks; trays; food picture cards.
DAILY_LEARNING_DOMAINS: Social-Emotional; Gross Motor; Math; Engineering.
CIRCLE_TIME_IDEAS: Talk about ants working together and march in a short line.
OUTDOOR_PLAY: Carry lightweight pretend food from one basket to an ant nest station.
DAILY_OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES: Cooperation, waiting, carrying, and positional language.
DAILY_ADAPTATIONS: Shorten the path and pair children with an adult.
SAFETY_NOTES: Use lightweight objects and supervise tunnels.

CATEGORY: Cooperative Play
ACTIVITY_NAME: Ant Food Delivery
OBJECTIVE: Work with peers to move objects to a shared location.
DESCRIPTION: Children carry pretend food pieces to a block ant nest.
MATERIALS: Large pretend food; baskets; block nest; path markers.
SETUP: Place food at one end and the nest at the other.
DIRECTIONS: 1. Pick one food item. 2. Walk along the path. 3. Place it in the nest. 4. Return for another. 5. Count the collected food together.
TEACHER_ROLE: Encourage turn-taking and teamwork language.
LEARNING_GOALS: Cooperation; carrying; counting; following a route.
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES: Peer awareness, balance, and task persistence.
ADAPTATIONS: Use a shorter route or wagon.
SAFETY_NOTES: Keep items light and paths uncluttered.

ACTIVITY_2:
CATEGORY: Engineering
ACTIVITY_NAME: Build an Ant Tunnel
OBJECTIVE: Connect tubes and blocks to create a pathway.
DESCRIPTION: Children create tunnels for toy ants.
MATERIALS: Cardboard tubes; blocks; toy ants; ramps.
SETUP: Arrange loose parts on the floor.
DIRECTIONS: 1. Choose tunnel pieces. 2. Connect or line them up. 3. Place an ant at the entrance. 4. Move it through. 5. Change the tunnel and test again.
TEACHER_ROLE: Ask what happens if pieces move or become taller.

OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES: Planning, revising, and persistence.

MATERIALS: Large ant counters; path mats; dot cards one to five.

FRIDAY:
DAILY_THEME: Bug Scientists
DAILY_OBJECTIVES: Observe, compare, review vocabulary, and share discoveries.
DAILY_VOCABULARY: Observe; magnify; same; different; discover.
DAILY_MATERIALS: Toy insects; magnifiers; sorting trays; paper; crayons; outdoor observation sheet.
DAILY_LEARNING_DOMAINS: Science; Language; Math; Creative Arts.
CIRCLE_TIME_IDEAS: Review insect cards and ask children to choose a favorite and show how it moves.
OUTDOOR_PLAY: Conduct a brief teacher-led bug search without touching or collecting live insects.
DAILY_OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES: Comparing, recall, preference expression, and respectful nature behavior.
DAILY_ADAPTATIONS: Use classroom photos if outdoor insects are unavailable.
SAFETY_NOTES: Do not touch unknown insects; check for allergies and remain in designated areas.

ACTIVITY_1:
CATEGORY: Science
ACTIVITY_NAME: Insect Observation Lab
OBJECTIVE: Observe details using a magnifier.
DESCRIPTION: Children examine large toy insects and compare body features.
MATERIALS: Toy insects; magnifiers; trays; picture cards.
SETUP: Place one insect on each tray.
DIRECTIONS: 1. Choose an insect. 2. Look closely. 3. Notice colors and parts. 4. Compare it with another. 5. Share one observation.
TEACHER_ROLE: Model observation language and validate gestures or single-word answers.
LEARNING_GOALS: Attention to detail; comparison; science vocabulary.
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES: Visual focus, comparison, and expressive language.
ADAPTATIONS: Use enlarged photographs and reduce choices.
SAFETY_NOTES: Use shatter-resistant magnifiers.

ACTIVITY_2:
CATEGORY: Math
ACTIVITY_NAME: Sort the Bugs
OBJECTIVE: Sort insects by one visible feature.
DESCRIPTION: Children sort toy insects by color, wings, or type.
MATERIALS: Large toy insects; colored bowls; sorting mats.
SETUP: Choose one simple sorting rule and model it.
DIRECTIONS: 1. Pick an insect. 2. Notice its feature. 3. Place it in a matching group. 4. Continue sorting. 5. Compare group sizes.
TEACHER_ROLE: Keep criteria simple and narrate choices.
LEARNING_GOALS: Classification; comparison; reasoning.
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES: Rule understanding and flexible thinking.
ADAPTATIONS: Offer only two groups or use identical matching cards.
SAFETY_NOTES: Supervise manipulatives.

OBJECTIVE: Represent and communicate a preferred insect.
DESCRIPTION: Children draw or mark a picture of their favorite insect and dictate a word or sentence.
MATERIALS: Paper; chunky crayons; insect pictures.
SETUP: Display insect choices at the art table.
DIRECTIONS: 1. Choose a favorite insect. 2. Select crayons. 3. Draw lines, spots, or wings. 4. Name the insect. 5. Teacher records the child's words.
TEACHER_ROLE: Honor all mark-making and write dictated language.
LEARNING_GOALS: Choice-making; early writing; recall; confidence.
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES: Grip, symbolic marks, and expressive communication.
