TITLE:
Toddler Color Hunt Friends

AGE_GROUP:
Toddler

THEME:
Color Hunt Friends

PLAN:
Free

STATUS:
draft

LEARNING_DOMAINS:
Math, Language & Literacy, Physical Development, Social Emotional

WEEKLY_OVERVIEW:
Toddlers hunt for colors around the room, sort soft objects, and practice naming favorite colors through short active games and calm art.

LEARNING_OBJECTIVES:
Toddlers will find and name at least two familiar colors.
Toddlers will sort objects by color with support.
Toddlers will practice turn-taking during color games.

WEEKLY_MATERIALS:
Colored scarves, sorting bowls, pom-poms, color cards, tape spots on floor, crayons, paper, baskets, music speaker

VOCABULARY:
red, blue, yellow, green, find, same, sort, match, look

BOOKS:
Brown Bear Brown Bear What Do You See? | Bill Martin Jr. / Eric Carle | Color naming chorus
Color Dance | Ann Jonas | Movement and color connection

SONGS:
Colors Colors Can You Find | Call out colors around the room
If You're Wearing Red | Stand or wave when color is called

FAMILY_CONNECTION:
Ask families to notice one color on the way to care and tell you about it at drop-off so you can celebrate that color during the day.

OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Note which colors children recognize independently, how they handle sorting, and peer sharing during hunts.

ADAPTATIONS:
Offer two-choice color prompts for emerging talkers. Use larger objects for easier grasp. Pair a visual card with every spoken color name.

MONDAY

DAILY_THEME:
Color Hunt Friends: Color Spot Hop and related play (introduce the theme through movement, sensory play, and simple language)

DAILY_OBJECTIVES:
Support color hunt friends learning through color spot hop.
Support color hunt friends learning through soft color scarf toss.

DAILY_VOCABULARY:
red, blue, yellow, green, find, same

DAILY_MATERIALS:
Colored tape or spot markers, open space
Colored scarves

DAILY_LEARNING_DOMAINS:
Math, Language & Literacy, Physical Development, Social Emotional

CIRCLE_TIME:
Welcome song and name greeting connected to color hunt friends.
Sing "Colors Colors Can You Find" with simple movements or props.
Show one prop or picture from today's activity (Color Spot Hop) and invite toddlers to name what they see.

OUTDOOR_PLAY:
Outdoor gross-motor play tied to color hunt friends: animal walks, pushing/pulling, or simple theme movement games for 5–15 minutes with close supervision.

DAILY_OBSERVATIONS:
Engagement during Color Spot Hop
Engagement during Soft Color Scarf Toss
Participation and interest in Color Hunt Friends play

DAILY_ADAPTATIONS:
Offer two-choice color prompts for emerging talkers. Use larger objects for easier grasp. Pair a visual card with every spoken color name.

SAFETY_NOTES:
Close supervision during movement and sensory play; avoid tiny pieces; check outdoor equipment before use.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Color Spot Hop
CATEGORY:
Gross Motor
OBJECTIVE:
Support color hunt friends learning through color spot hop.
DESCRIPTION:
Toddlers hop or step onto colored floor spots when the color is named. Children explore color hunt friends through color spot hop.
MATERIALS:
Colored tape or spot markers, open space
SETUP:
Toddlers hop or step onto colored floor spots when the color is named.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Model simple steps, stay close for support, and use short theme vocabulary during color spot hop.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:

DIRECTIONS:
1. Show two color spots and name them.
2. Call a color and model stepping onto it.
3. Invite toddlers to find the matching spot.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Match spoken color to a location
Practice balance and body control
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Engagement during Color Spot Hop
Use of color hunt friends vocabulary or interest
Peer interaction and independence
VOCABULARY:

EXTENSIONS:

ADAPTATIONS:
Offer larger materials, hand-over-hand support, and a shorter turn. Allow parallel play instead of forced sharing.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Supervise closely, keep materials too large to choke on, and clear walkways for active movement. Check outdoor surfaces before play.
AGE_MODIFICATIONS:
