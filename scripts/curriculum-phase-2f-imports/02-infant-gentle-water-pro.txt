TITLE:
Infant Gentle Water Play

AGE_GROUP:
Infant 0–6 Months

THEME:
Gentle Water Discovery

PLAN:
Pro

STATUS:
draft

LEARNING_DOMAINS:
Science, Physical Development, Social Emotional, Language & Literacy

WEEKLY_OVERVIEW:
Infants explore safe water play through supervised splash trays, wet cloths, and calm pouring. Keep water shallow, stay within arm's reach, and dry babies promptly.

LEARNING_OBJECTIVES:
Infants will notice wet and dry textures with caregiver support.
Infants will practice reaching, patting, and early pouring motions.
Infants will stay regulated during short sensory water moments.

WEEKLY_MATERIALS:
Shallow sensory trays, warm water, washcloths, soft sponges, cups, towels, waterproof smocks, non-slip mats, soft ducks

VOCABULARY:
wet, dry, splash, drip, warm, wipe, pour, soft

BOOKS:
Splash! | Ann Jonas | Short water-themed looking time
Time for a Bath | Phaidon / Baby board book | Connect to caregiving routines

SONGS:
Row Row Row Your Boat | Gentle rocking version
Rain Is Falling Down | Soft finger taps on tray

FAMILY_CONNECTION:
Invite families to describe bath-time preferences (warmth, songs, comfort cues) so classroom water play can feel familiar and safe.

OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Watch for enjoyment vs. startle responses, reaching into water, and calming strategies that help each infant stay regulated.

ADAPTATIONS:
Offer a damp cloth only for babies who dislike open water trays. Support seated infants with rolled towels. Keep sessions very short for sensitive babies.

MONDAY

DAILY_THEME:
Gentle Water Discovery: Warm Washcloth Explore and related play (introduce calm bonding, faces, and soft sensory play)

DAILY_OBJECTIVES:
Support gentle water discovery learning through warm washcloth explore.
Support gentle water discovery learning through boat rock lullaby.

DAILY_VOCABULARY:
wet, dry, splash, drip, warm, wipe

DAILY_MATERIALS:
Warm washcloths, towels
Calm seating space

DAILY_LEARNING_DOMAINS:
Science, Physical Development, Social Emotional, Language & Literacy

CIRCLE_TIME:
Soft hello with each infant's name and a calm face-to-face greeting.
Hum or sing "Row Row Row Your Boat" at a low volume while offering gentle movement or visual interest tied to gentle water discovery.
End with a brief look at Splash! by Ann Jonas or a theme picture card, watching for engagement cues.

OUTDOOR_PLAY:
Take infants outdoors for fresh air on a shaded blanket or supervised stroller time. Narrate gentle water discovery sounds and sights for 1–5 minutes, staying within arm's reach and watching for overstimulation.

DAILY_OBSERVATIONS:
Engagement during Warm Washcloth Explore
Engagement during Boat Rock Lullaby
Participation and interest in Gentle Water Discovery play

DAILY_ADAPTATIONS:
Offer a damp cloth only for babies who dislike open water trays. Support seated infants with rolled towels. Keep sessions very short for sensitive babies.

SAFETY_NOTES:
Arm's-reach supervision, choke-safe materials only, supervised tummy time, and no unattended water or elevated surfaces.

ACTIVITY_NAME:
Warm Washcloth Explore
CATEGORY:
Sensory Play
OBJECTIVE:
Support gentle water discovery learning through warm washcloth explore.
DESCRIPTION:
Infants feel warm damp washcloths on hands and arms with caregiver support. Children explore gentle water discovery through warm washcloth explore.
MATERIALS:
Warm washcloths, towels
SETUP:
Infants feel warm damp washcloths on hands and arms with caregiver support.
TEACHER_ROLE:
Stay within arm's reach, follow the infant's cues, and narrate gentle water discovery softly during warm washcloth explore.
TEACHER_LANGUAGE:

DIRECTIONS:
1. Offer a warm damp cloth for touching.
2. Gently wipe hands while naming wet and warm.
3. Switch to a dry towel and name dry.
LEARNING_GOALS:
Notice wet and dry contrasts
Stay calm during sensory caregiving
OBSERVATION_OPPORTUNITIES:
Engagement during Warm Washcloth Explore
Use of gentle water discovery vocabulary or interest
Cue reading and calm participation
VOCABULARY:

EXTENSIONS:

ADAPTATIONS:
Shorten to 1–5 minutes. Offer side-lying or supported positions. Stop immediately if the infant shows overstimulation cues.
SAFETY_NOTES:
Remain within arm's reach at all times. Use only large, choke-safe materials. Never leave an infant unattended on a mat, changing surface, or near water.
