# 1. WEEKLY OVERVIEW

Children become pumpkin patch helpers as they explore pumpkins and harvest work through observation, sorting, counting, measuring, sensory play, dramatic play, art, movement, literacy, and simple science. The week begins with noticing pumpkin features, moves into patch jobs and garden helpers, investigates what is inside a pumpkin, explores how pumpkins change when scooped, cooked, or crafted, and ends with a child-led harvest helper celebration.

Learning stays invitational and play-based. Children compare sizes and textures, wonder about seeds and pulp, practice caring for tools and shared spaces, cooperate in pretend patch and market play, and represent discoveries through movement, art, conversation, and early documentation. The teacher is a co-helper who models curiosity, safe tool use, and inclusive participation.

The plan uses common classroom materials and can be completed with real pumpkins, miniature pumpkins, gourds, photographs, or reusable pretend pumpkins. No child is required to taste pumpkin foods. Food experiences must follow program allergy, sanitation, family-permission, and safe-food policies. Carving knives stay with adults only; children use safe scooping and sensory tools.

---

# 2. ESSENTIAL QUESTION

What can we discover by helping in a pumpkin patch and looking closely at pumpkins?

---

# 3. BIG IDEAS

- Pumpkins can have different colors, sizes, shapes, textures, stems, and weights.
- Pumpkins grow on vines in gardens and patches and are part of harvest time.
- Helpers use tools, teamwork, and careful observation to grow, gather, clean, scoop, and share pumpkins.
- Pumpkins can be counted, sorted, compared, measured, patterned, and represented.
- Scooping, washing, cooking, drying, and crafting can change pumpkins.
- Scientists ask questions, make predictions, observe, describe, compare, and revise ideas.

---

# 4. LEARNING OBJECTIVES

By the end of the week, children will have opportunities to:

1. Use descriptive language for pumpkin color, size, shape, texture, weight, stem, pulp, and seeds.
2. Ask questions and make predictions during pumpkin investigations.
3. Sort and classify pumpkins or pumpkin representations by observable attributes.
4. Count with one-to-one correspondence during patch, seed, and helper play.
5. Compare quantities and sizes using more, fewer, same, larger, smaller, heavier, and lighter.
6. Identify basic pumpkin parts, including skin, stem, pulp, seeds, and cavity.
7. Participate in collaborative dramatic play as patch helpers, gardeners, and market friends.
8. Strengthen hand muscles through scooping, transferring, pinching, rolling, painting, and building.
9. Use whole-body movement to imitate vines, rolling pumpkins, carrying, and harvest work.
10. Express ideas through drawing, painting, collage, construction, storytelling, and dictation.
11. Practice turn-taking, shared planning, negotiation, and caring for materials and tools.
12. Connect classroom learning to family harvest, garden, market, or autumn experiences without assuming every family shares the same traditions.

- Several pumpkins in varied sizes (mini to medium) and/or gourds

# 10. FAMILY CONNECTION

Invite families to share one optional response: a favorite fall food or garden memory, a photo from a market/farm stand visit, or a short story about helping at home. Make clear that families do not need to buy pumpkins or send food. Conversation prompt: “When do we help each other at home, and what jobs do we do together?” Optional home extension: sort orange/yellow objects, count steps while walking, or notice vines/leaves outdoors. Do not require homework or documentation. Offer inclusive language for families who do not celebrate Halloween.

# 11. OBSERVATION OPPORTUNITIES

- Descriptive vocabulary during sensory and scooping exploration
- Sorting by one attribute vs changing rules with explanation
- One-to-one counting with seeds, mini pumpkins, or baskets
- Measurement strategies for size, weight, and circumference
- Predictions and revised ideas during investigations
- Fine-motor control with scoops, tongs, dough, and drawing
- Cooperation and role negotiation in patch dramatic play
- Drawings, dictation, photos, and quotes for portfolios
- Communication via gesture, signs, pictures, home language, or assistive tools

---

# 12. ADAPTATIONS AND INCLUSION

- Offer whole pumpkins, photographs, sealed sensory bags, or pretend pumpkins when pulp contact is not preferred
- Provide large-handled scoops, adaptive scissors, thick crayons, and non-slip mats
- Use visual sequences, first-then language, modeled gestures, and short directions
- Allow observe-first, tool-only, or dry alternatives to wet pulp play
- Provide seated, standing, and floor-level participation choices
- Use picture vocabulary cards; honor home-language and nonverbal responses
- Never require tasting; looking, smelling, scooping, drawing, and comparing are equal options
- Keep Halloween content optional, gentle, and non-scary; provide harvest/helper alternatives
- Extend with numerals, graphs, invented sorting rules, labels, or leadership helper roles

---

# 13. SAFETY NOTES (WEEK-WIDE)

- Adults only open pumpkins with knives; children never handle carving tools
- Wash hands before/after food or pulp play; sanitize surfaces
- Watch for choking hazards with seeds for children who mouth objects; use larger seed bowls with supervision or sealed discovery bottles
- Lift heavy pumpkins with adult help; teach “ask a helper” instead of straining
- Allergy protocols for any optional tasting
- Outdoor patch walks require sun/shade, hydration, and clear boundaries
- Emotional safety: no jump scares, haunted themes, or forced costume play

### Monday activities

1. **Pumpkin Feature Lab** — Sensory / Science  
   - Objective: Observe and describe pumpkin attributes  
   - Description: Children explore pumpkins with magnifiers and record notices on a group chart  
   - Materials: Pumpkins, magnifiers, clipboards, crayons  
   - Setup: Station trays with 2–3 pumpkins; vocabulary cards nearby  
   - Steps: 1) Look closely. 2) Touch gently. 3) Tell a partner one notice. 4) Add a word/drawing to the chart.  
   - Teacher role: Model descriptive language; scribe dictation  
   - Teacher language: “What do your eyes notice? What do your hands notice?”  
   - Learning goals: Science inquiry; vocabulary  
   - Observation opportunities: Attribute language; peer sharing  
   - Vocabulary: bumpy, smooth, stem, heavy  
   - Extensions: Compare two pumpkins with “same/different” frames  
   - Adaptations: Sealed bags; photos; pointer sticks  
   - Safety notes: Wipe spills; wash hands  
   - Age modifications: Younger preschool — one attribute at a time; older — multiple attributes

2. **Heavy or Light Helper Scale** — Math  
   - Objective: Compare weight using a balance scale  
   - Description: Children place pumpkins/objects on a balance and decide heavier/lighter  
   - Materials: Balance scale, mini pumpkins, cubes, baskets  
   - Setup: Scale on sturdy table; mats under  
   - Steps: 1) Choose two items. 2) Place on scale. 3) Watch which side goes down. 4) Say heavier/lighter.  
   - Teacher role: Coach comparison language  
   - Teacher language: “Which side is heavier? How do you know?”  
   - Learning goals: Measurement; compare  
   - Observation opportunities: Reasoning language  
   - Vocabulary: heavier, lighter, balance  
   - Extensions: Order three items light→heavy  
   - Adaptations: Pre-weighed pairs; picture cards  
   - Safety notes: Spot heavy items with adult  
   - Age modifications: Start with obvious pairs

3. **Helper Hands Dramatic Patch** — Dramatic Play / Social Emotional  
   - Objective: Practice cooperative helper roles  
   - Description: Children run a pretend pumpkin patch with jobs (waterer, picker, greeter)  
   - Materials: Crates, gloves, signs, pretend pumpkins  
   - Setup: Define patch area with tape/crates  
   - Steps: 1) Choose a helper job. 2) Greet friends. 3) Move pumpkins gently. 4) Switch jobs.  
   - Teacher role: Coach turn-taking and kind phrases  
   - Teacher language: “Helpers ask, ‘May I help you?’”  
   - Learning goals: Cooperation; role play  
   - Observation opportunities: Negotiation; kindness  
   - Vocabulary: helper, patch, share  
   - Extensions: Make open/closed signs  
   - Adaptations: Job picture cards; parallel play options  
   - Safety notes: Clear walkways  
   - Age modifications: Fewer jobs for younger groups

4. **Pumpkin Stem Prints** — Creative Arts  
   - Objective: Represent pumpkin shapes through process art  
   - Description: Children stamp/paint round pumpkin forms and add stems  
   - Materials: Paint, sponges, paper, green crayons  
   - Setup: Paint station with smocks  
   - Steps: 1) Stamp or paint a round shape. 2) Add a stem. 3) Optional leaf. 4) Dictate a title.  
   - Teacher role: Protect process over product  
   - Teacher language: “Your pumpkin can be any size.”  
   - Learning goals: Fine motor; representation  
   - Observation opportunities: Grip; planning  
   - Vocabulary: stem, round, print  
   - Extensions: Pattern of sizes  
   - Adaptations: Large brushes; vertical easel  
   - Safety notes: Washable paint only  
   - Age modifications: Pre-drawn circles optional

   - Extensions: Write/dictation captions  
   - Adaptations: Two-step sequences

   - Age modifications: Match cards to photos

2. **Careful Carriers Course** — Gross Motor  
   - Objective: Carry/roll pumpkins safely through a path  
   - Description: Obstacle path with gentle rolling and two-hand carries (lightweight props)  
   - Materials: Soft balls/mini pumpkins, cones, tape path  
   - Setup: Outdoor/indoor path  
   - Steps: 1) Walk the vine path. 2) Carry or roll. 3) Place in crate. 4) High-five a helper.  
   - Teacher role: Spot and coach pacing  
   - Teacher language: “Slow feet, strong helper hands.”  
   - Learning goals: Balance; coordination  
   - Observation opportunities: Body control  
   - Vocabulary: carry, roll, balance  
   - Extensions: Time estimation language (not racing)  
   - Adaptations: Shorter path; seated pass  
   - Safety notes: Soft props only  
   - Age modifications: Wider lanes

3. **Crate Count Crew** — Math  
   - Objective: Count and compare crate collections  
   - Description: Fill crates to target numbers and compare more/fewer

   - Setup: Targets 1–10 posted

   - Teacher language: “Touch one as you count.”  
   - Learning goals: Cardinality; compare  
   - Observation opportunities: Counting accuracy  
   - Vocabulary: more, fewer, same  
   - Extensions: Combine two crates  
   - Adaptations: Numeral with dots  
   - Safety notes: Stable crates  
   - Age modifications: Numbers 1–5 first

4. **Thank-You Helper Notes** — Literacy / Social Emotional
