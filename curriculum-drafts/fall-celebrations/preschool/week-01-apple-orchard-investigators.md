# 1. WEEKLY OVERVIEW

Children become apple orchard investigators as they explore real apples through observation, sorting, counting, sensory play, dramatic play, art, movement, literacy, and simple science. The week begins with noticing apple features, moves into orchard life and harvesting, investigates what is inside an apple, explores ways apples change when prepared, and ends with a child-led orchard market celebration.

The plan is intentionally hands-on and play-based. Children are invited to compare, wonder, test ideas, communicate observations, cooperate with peers, and represent learning through movement, art, conversation, construction, pretend play, and early documentation. The teacher acts as a co-investigator who models curiosity rather than giving every answer.

The plan uses common classroom materials and can be completed with real apples, reusable pretend apples, or a combination of both. No child is required to taste apples. Food experiences must follow program allergy, sanitation, family-permission, and safe-food policies.

---

# 2. ESSENTIAL QUESTION

What can we discover by looking closely at apples and pretending to work in an orchard?

---

# 3. BIG IDEAS

- Apples can have different colors, sizes, textures, smells, tastes, and parts.
- Apples grow on trees and are harvested in orchards.
- People use tools, cooperation, and careful observation to grow, collect, transport, and sell apples.
- Apples can be counted, sorted, compared, measured, patterned, and represented.
- Cutting, cooking, drying, pressing, and mixing can change apples.
- Scientists ask questions, make predictions, observe, describe, compare, and revise ideas.

---

# 4. LEARNING OBJECTIVES

By the end of the week, children will have opportunities to:

1. Use descriptive language to discuss apple color, shape, size, texture, smell, parts, and change.
2. Ask questions and make predictions during apple investigations.
3. Sort and classify apples or apple representations using one or more observable attributes.
4. Count objects with one-to-one correspondence during meaningful play.
5. Compare quantities and sizes using language such as more, fewer, same, larger, smaller, heavier, and lighter.
6. Identify basic parts of an apple, including skin, flesh, stem, core, and seeds.
7. Participate in collaborative dramatic play based on an apple orchard and market.
8. Strengthen hand muscles and coordination through transferring, pinching, squeezing, painting, building, and manipulating materials.
9. Use whole-body movement to imitate orchard work, tree growth, wind, picking, carrying, and delivery.
10. Express ideas through drawing, painting, sculpture, construction, storytelling, and dictation.
11. Practice turn-taking, shared planning, negotiation, and caring for classroom materials.
12. Connect classroom learning to family experiences, foods, traditions, and community helpers without assuming every family has the same experiences.

- Describe apple features using increasingly specific words.
- Ask questions or communicate curiosity through words, gestures, facial expressions, or actions.
- Make, test, and revise predictions.
- Sort objects and explain or demonstrate a sorting rule.
- Count with one-to-one correspondence and recognize small quantities.

- Use tools such as magnifiers, tongs, scales, writing tools, and scoops.
- Participate in pretend roles and sustain shared play.
- Negotiate roles, wait, take turns, solve peer problems, and contribute ideas.
- Recall and sequence steps in an investigation or recipe.
- Represent observations through drawing, marks, sculpture, construction, movement, or dictation.
- Demonstrate persistence when a structure falls, a tool is difficult, or a prediction is not confirmed.

Suggested documentation statement: **“During the apple investigation, the child…”** followed by a specific action, quote, strategy, or interaction without judgmental language.

# 12. ADAPTATIONS AND INCLUSION SUPPORTS

- Offer real apples, pretend apples, photographs, and tactile symbols so children can participate without tasting or direct food contact.
- Use visual sequence cards for washing, observing, cutting, comparing, and cleaning.
- Provide adaptive tongs, larger handles, nonslip mats, shallow trays, and seated work options.
- Allow children to point, gesture, sign, use a communication device, select picture choices, or dictate rather than answer verbally.
- Preteach vocabulary with real objects and photographs.
- Reduce the number of choices for children who become overwhelmed by large displays.
- Offer scent-free dough and nonfood sensory materials.
- Provide gloves or tools for children who avoid sticky or wet textures.
- Create a quiet observation tray away from busy group areas.
- Pair children strategically for peer modeling without forcing assistance.
- Use first/then language and predictable cleanup cues.
- For children ready for more challenge, invite multi-attribute sorting, simple graphing, estimating, recording numerals, comparing varieties, or designing a fair test.
- Avoid requiring eye contact, speaking in front of the group, tasting, touching messy materials, or participating in costumes.

# MONDAY — MEET THE APPLE INVESTIGATORS

## Daily Theme
Looking closely at apples

## Daily Objectives

- Notice and describe visible and tactile apple features.
- Ask questions and share prior knowledge.
- Compare apples by color, size, shape, and texture.
- Practice careful tool use and collaborative investigation.

## Daily Learning Domains
Science; Language & Literacy; Math; Social Emotional; Physical Development; Creative Arts

## Daily Vocabulary
investigate, observe, apple, color, shape, size, smooth, bumpy, stem, compare

## Daily Materials
Varied apples or pretend apples, magnifiers, trays, clipboards, paper, crayons, balance scale, baskets, chart paper, blocks, art materials

## Daily Book
**Apples** by Gail Gibbons — Read selected pages and pause for child questions.

## Daily Song
**Way Up High in the Apple Tree** — Use hand motions and invite children to suggest apple colors.

## Circle Time
Reveal a covered basket containing several apples. Invite children to guess what is inside based on clues. Uncover the basket and introduce the role of an investigator: someone who looks closely, asks questions, tests ideas, and notices details. Pass one apple or pretend apple for visual observation. Record children’s questions on chart paper under **“What We Wonder About Apples.”** Do not correct every idea immediately; save questions for investigation.

## Transition
Call children by an observable apple attribute: “If you are wearing something red like an apple, walk to the table,” followed by other colors, shapes, or positional clues.

## Outdoor Play
**Orchard Observation Walk:** Search for trees, leaves, branches, bark, fruit, seeds, and evidence of seasonal change. Children may collect only safe fallen leaves if allowed. Compare the outdoor trees with orchard photographs. Ask, “What would an apple tree need in this space?”

## Daily Family Connection
Invite children to ask someone at home, “What kind of apple or fruit do you like?” Families can respond with any fruit.

## Daily Observation Prompts

- What details does the child notice without adult prompting?
- Which comparison words does the child use or understand?
- How does the child handle tools and materials?
- Does the child ask a question, make a claim, or revise an idea?

## Daily Adaptations
Use two highly contrasting apples, photo choices, tactile symbols, larger magnifiers, and a stable tray. Permit observation without touching. Offer sentence frames: “I notice…,” “It feels…,” “I wonder…,” or picture-supported choices.

## Daily Safety Notes
Inspect apples and natural items. Supervise magnifiers and small materials. No tasting today unless separately planned and approved.

### Activity 1: Apple Investigation Lab

**Activity Category:** STEM/Discovery  
**Objective:** Children will use their senses and investigation tools to observe and compare apples.  
**Description:** Small groups rotate through a table where they examine apples closely, discuss features, compare specimens, and document observations through drawing, marks, photographs, or dictation.  
**Learning Domains:** Science; Language & Literacy; Math; Physical Development  
**Materials:** Apples or pretend apples, trays, magnifiers, clipboards, paper, crayons, balance scale, linking cubes or yarn  
**Setup:** Place two to four apples with visible differences on each tray. Add magnifiers and one optional measuring tool. Leave enough open space for children to move objects safely.  
**Steps:**
1. Invite children to choose one apple and look without touching for several seconds.
2. Ask what they notice first.
3. Let children hold, turn, smell, and gently tap the apple if comfortable.
4. Introduce magnifiers and model keeping them above the apple rather than pressing them into it.
5. Compare two apples using child-selected features.
6. Offer a scale, cubes, or yarn for informal measurement.
7. Invite children to record one discovery with a drawing, marks, labels, or dictation.
8. Add a child quote or discovery to the class wonder chart.

**Teacher Role:** Model curiosity, protect child-led exploration, introduce accurate vocabulary after children share their own descriptions, and avoid turning the experience into a quiz.  
**Teacher Language:** “What do you notice?” “What makes you say that?” “How are these alike?” “How are they different?” “What could we use to compare them?” “Did anything surprise you?”  
**Learning Goals:** Careful observation; comparison; descriptive language; tool use; representation of ideas  
**Observation Opportunities:** Note descriptive vocabulary, attention to detail, measurement strategies, grip and tool control, and ability to explain a comparison.  
**Vocabulary:** observe, investigate, compare, smooth, stem, heavier, lighter  
**Extensions:** Create a class photo chart; compare apples to another fruit; invite children to invent a new tool for examining apples.  
**Adaptations:** Offer one object at a time, a hands-free magnifier stand, picture vocabulary, gloves, or a nonfood model.  
**Safety Notes:** Apples are for observation unless tasting is approved. Watch for mouthing of small tools.  
**Age Modifications:** For younger preschoolers, focus on two contrasting features. For older preschoolers, invite estimates, numeral recording, or multi-attribute comparisons.

### Activity 2: Sort It Your Way

**Activity Category:** Math  
**Objective:** Children will sort apple representations and explain or demonstrate their sorting rule.  
**Description:** Children use pretend apples, colored counters, apple cards, or real apples to create their own groups, then reorganize the collection using a new rule.  
**Learning Domains:** Math; Language & Literacy; Social Emotional  
**Materials:** Pretend apples or apple cards, hoops or trays, baskets, labels, numeral cards  
**Setup:** Place mixed apple materials in a central basket with two to four empty sorting spaces.  
**Steps:**
1. Invite a child to divide the apples into groups in any way that makes sense.
2. Ask the child to show or explain the rule.
3. Invite peers to predict where a new apple belongs.
4. Mix the set and choose a different attribute.
5. Count each group if children are interested.
6. Compare which group has more, fewer, or the same.

**Teacher Role:** Accept valid child-created rules, help peers listen to one another, and introduce mathematical language in context.  
**Teacher Language:** “How did you decide?” “Where would this one go?” “Could there be another way?” “What changed when we used a new rule?”  
**Learning Goals:** Classification; flexible thinking; quantity comparison; explanation  
**Observation Opportunities:** Record whether the child sorts consistently, notices more than one attribute, counts accurately, or changes strategy.  
**Vocabulary:** sort, group, rule, same, different, more, fewer  
**Extensions:** Sort by two attributes; graph favorite colors; create labels for each group.  
**Adaptations:** Begin with two colors, use tactile or high-contrast objects, and allow nonverbal demonstration.  
**Safety Notes:** Supervise small counters and keep real apples stable.  
**Age Modifications:** Younger preschoolers match identical objects. Older preschoolers justify rules and explore overlapping categories.

### Activity 3: Collaborative Apple Mural

**Activity Category:** Art  
**Objective:** Children will represent observations through open-ended collaborative art.  
**Description:** Children create a large orchard mural using painting, printing, drawing, collage, and dictated labels. The mural remains available all week for additions.  
**Learning Domains:** Creative Arts; Language & Literacy; Social Emotional; Physical Development  
**Materials:** Large paper, washable paint, sponges or corks, brushes, paper scraps, glue, crayons, leaf shapes, photographs for reference  
**Setup:** Attach paper at child height on a wall or table. Offer tools in organized containers without a required sample product.  
**Steps:**
1. Revisit details children noticed during the investigation.
2. Invite children to decide what an orchard mural might need.
3. Offer painting, printing, drawing, and collage choices.
4. Encourage children to negotiate shared space and combine ideas.
5. Record dictated labels, questions, or stories beside the artwork.
6. Leave the mural accessible for additions throughout the week.

**Teacher Role:** Support collaboration and safe material use without directing children to make identical apples.  
**Teacher Language:** “What part are you adding?” “How could we make room for both ideas?” “What detail did you notice that you want to show?”  
**Learning Goals:** Visual representation; cooperation; fine-motor development; symbolic thinking  
**Observation Opportunities:** Note planning, tool choice, persistence, peer negotiation, and connections between art and observed details.  
**Vocabulary:** mural, orchard, branch, detail, print, collage  
**Extensions:** Add child photographs, written signs, weather changes, or orchard workers later in the week.  
**Adaptations:** Offer sponge handles, glue sticks, pre-torn paper, seated access, and a personal page that can later be attached.  
**Safety Notes:** Use nontoxic materials, protect floors, and clean wet spills.  
**Age Modifications:** Younger preschoolers focus on sensory mark-making. Older preschoolers add plans, labels, maps, or connected scenes.

# TUESDAY — LIFE IN AN APPLE ORCHARD

## Daily Theme
How apples grow and how people care for orchards

## Daily Objectives

- Sequence basic stages of apple growth.
- Explore roles and tools connected to orchards.
- Cooperate in dramatic play and construction.
- Use movement to represent growth and harvesting.

## Daily Learning Domains
Science; Language & Literacy; Social Emotional; Physical Development; Creative Arts; Math

## Daily Vocabulary
orchard, blossom, branch, grow, harvest, picker, farmer, ladder, crate, season

## Daily Materials
Growth photographs or cards, blocks, boxes, baskets, fabric leaves, pretend apples, aprons, signs, toy vehicles, paper, markers, scarves

## Daily Book
**The Apple Pie Tree** by Zoe Hall

## Daily Song
**This Is the Way We Pick the Apples**

## Circle Time
Use photographs or props to tell the simplified story of an apple tree: resting tree, blossom, tiny fruit, growing apple, ripe apple, harvest. Invite children to physically arrange sequence cards. Discuss that weather, tree variety, location, and season can affect growth. Introduce orchard jobs without suggesting one person does everything.

## Transition
Children move like parts of an orchard: roots stretch low, branches reach wide, blossoms open, apples grow round, pickers carry baskets carefully.

## Outdoor Play
**Apple Delivery Course:** Children transport pretend apples or balls through a path using baskets, wagons, or wheelbarrows. Add signs for orchard, washing station, packing area, and market. Emphasize teamwork rather than speed.

## Daily Family Connection
Ask families whether anyone has visited a farm, orchard, garden, grocery store, produce stand, or community market. Any food-growing or shopping experience is welcome.

## Daily Observation Prompts

- Can the child place two or more growth events in a meaningful order?
- How does the child enter, sustain, or expand dramatic play?
- What roles does the child choose and how are roles negotiated?
- How does the child adjust body movement while carrying materials?

## Daily Adaptations
Use a first-next-last sequence, real photographs, role cards, lighter baskets, seated transport, and clearly defined play spaces. Offer parallel roles such as sign maker, checker, driver, photographer, or customer.

## Daily Safety Notes
No climbing on classroom furniture. Use floor-level pretend ladders or taped ladder shapes. Limit basket loads and maintain clear pathways.

### Activity 1: Build an Orchard

**Activity Category:** Open-Ended Exploration  
**Objective:** Children will design and construct an orchard environment using blocks and loose parts.  
**Description:** Small groups build trees, rows, paths, fences, packing spaces, and market areas. The construction becomes part of dramatic play.  
**Learning Domains:** Math; Science; Creative Arts; Social Emotional; Language & Literacy  
**Materials:** Blocks, cardboard tubes, boxes, fabric leaves, pretend apples, toy people, vehicles, signs, paper, markers  
**Setup:** Display orchard photographs nearby. Arrange materials by type but do not provide a model to copy.  
**Steps:**
1. Ask children what spaces an orchard might need.
2. Invite them to sketch, point to, or verbally describe an idea.
3. Begin building trees, rows, roads, fences, or structures.
4. Test whether baskets and vehicles can move through the design.
5. Revise unstable or crowded sections.
6. Add labels and figures for pretend play.

**Teacher Role:** Help children communicate plans, solve structural problems, and share materials.  
**Teacher Language:** “What is your plan?” “How will the truck get through?” “What could make the tree stand?” “Where will workers put the apples?”  
**Learning Goals:** Spatial reasoning; engineering; collaboration; symbolic representation  
**Observation Opportunities:** Note planning, balance strategies, persistence, spatial words, and group problem-solving.  
**Vocabulary:** orchard, row, path, structure, stable, transport  
**Extensions:** Map the orchard; add irrigation channels; count trees by row; photograph revisions.  
**Adaptations:** Use larger lightweight blocks, Velcro-backed pieces, floor or tabletop options, and individual construction trays.  
**Safety Notes:** Keep structures below unsafe heights and clear traffic paths.  
**Age Modifications:** Younger preschoolers build individual trees or roads. Older preschoolers create connected systems and explain design choices.

### Activity 2: Orchard Workers Dramatic Play

**Activity Category:** Dramatic Play  
**Objective:** Children will use cooperative pretend play to explore orchard and market roles.  
**Description:** Children pick, inspect, sort, pack, transport, and sell pretend apples using an evolving orchard setup.  
**Learning Domains:** Social Emotional; Language & Literacy; Math; Creative Arts  
**Materials:** Pretend apples, baskets, crates, aprons, role cards, toy phone, order pads, signs, scale, reusable bags, pretend money  
**Setup:** Create flexible areas for orchard, inspection table, packing station, delivery, and market.  
**Steps:**
1. Brainstorm possible roles.
2. Model how to enter play respectfully: “Can I be the driver?” or “What job still needs someone?”
3. Let children choose or invent roles.
4. Add a simple problem, such as a large order or mixed apples that need sorting.
5. Encourage children to write, draw, or mark orders and signs.
6. Reflect briefly on how the team worked together.

**Teacher Role:** Observe before joining, then enter as a customer or coworker to extend language, math, and cooperation.  
**Teacher Language:** “What does your job involve?” “How will you know these are ready?” “The market ordered six apples. What should we do?” “How can both of you use the truck?”  
**Learning Goals:** Role negotiation; sustained play; functional writing; counting; social problem-solving  
**Observation Opportunities:** Note role flexibility, symbolic use of materials, peer language, quantity use, and conflict-resolution strategies.  
**Vocabulary:** worker, picker, inspector, order, pack, deliver, market  
**Extensions:** Add price signs, receipts, maps, customer requests, or a repair station.  
**Adaptations:** Use visual role badges, short scripted phrases, duplicate high-demand props, and nonverbal jobs.  
**Safety Notes:** Avoid strings around necks and overloaded crates.  
**Age Modifications:** Younger preschoolers engage in simple picking and carrying. Older preschoolers coordinate multi-step orders and changing roles.

### Activity 3: From Blossom to Apple Movement Story

**Activity Category:** Music & Movement  
**Objective:** Children will use movement to represent stages of apple growth and seasonal change.  
**Description:** Children participate in a narrated movement story that begins with a resting tree and ends with harvest and seed dispersal.  
**Learning Domains:** Physical Development; Science; Creative Arts; Language & Literacy  
**Materials:** Scarves, open floor space, optional growth-stage images, soft instrumental music  
**Setup:** Clear obstacles and place visual sequence cards where children can see them.  
**Steps:**
1. Curl low like a quiet winter tree.
2. Slowly stretch roots and trunk.
3. Open fingers like blossoms.
4. Form small round apples with hands.
5. Grow larger while swaying in wind and rain.
6. Pretend to be carefully harvested and carried.
7. End by becoming seeds ready for a new cycle.

**Teacher Role:** Narrate with pauses, offer multiple movement choices, and connect actions to growth vocabulary.  
**Teacher Language:** “How can your body show a tiny blossom?” “What changes as the apple grows?” “How can we carry apples without dropping them?”  
**Learning Goals:** Sequencing; body control; imagination; concept representation  
**Observation Opportunities:** Note balance, directional movement, response to sequence, and creative variation.  
**Vocabulary:** blossom, grow, sway, harvest, seed, season  
**Extensions:** Children narrate; add weather instruments; photograph poses for a sequence book.  
**Adaptations:** All motions can be completed seated, with arms, eyes, facial expressions, or props.  
**Safety Notes:** Maintain personal space and use slow controlled motions.  
**Age Modifications:** Younger preschoolers imitate one motion at a time. Older preschoolers create and sequence their own movement stages.

# WEDNESDAY — WHAT IS INSIDE AN APPLE?

## Daily Theme
Apple parts, seeds, patterns, and simple investigation

## Daily Objectives

- Identify visible parts of an apple.
- Predict and observe what happens when an apple is opened.
- Count and compare seeds.
- Represent internal structures through art and modeling.

## Daily Learning Domains
Science; Math; Language & Literacy; Creative Arts; Physical Development

## Daily Vocabulary
skin, flesh, stem, core, seed, inside, outside, half, pattern, predict

## Daily Materials
Whole apples, teacher-cut apple halves, trays, magnifiers, seed substitutes if needed, chart paper, paint, paper, play dough, apple-part labels, clear cups

## Daily Book
**Hello, World! How Do Apples Grow?** by Jill McDonald

## Daily Song
**Five Red Apples**

## Circle Time
Display a whole apple and ask children to draw, gesture, or describe what they predict is inside. Record predictions. An adult cuts the apple horizontally and vertically where children can observe from a safe distance. Compare the two cuts and look for the seed-star pattern. Name parts while honoring child descriptions.

## Transition
Give each child a part or position word: stem, skin, core, seed, inside, outside, top, bottom. Children move when their word appears in a short teacher-created story.

## Outdoor Play
**Seed Travelers:** Children move like seeds transported by wind, animals, water, or people. Use chalk circles as places where seeds land. Discuss that apple trees are usually grown and cared for by people, while the movement game explores general ways seeds can move.

## Daily Family Connection
Send a photo or child-safe diagram of an apple cross-section with the prompt, “What patterns can you find inside fruits or vegetables at home?”

## Daily Observation Prompts

- Does the child distinguish inside and outside features?
- How does the child respond when evidence differs from a prediction?
- What counting strategies are used?
- Can the child represent parts spatially in a model or drawing?

## Daily Adaptations
Use large photo cards, tactile models, pre-cut paper pieces, a seed-free option, and side-by-side whole/half models. Let children watch without approaching food.

## Daily Safety Notes
Adults cut apples. Keep knives inaccessible. Remove seeds and small pieces from reach when needed. Clean food-contact surfaces and discard aging apple pieces.

### Activity 1: Apple Cross-Section Investigation

**Activity Category:** STEM/Discovery  
**Objective:** Children will compare whole and cut apples and identify visible parts.  
**Description:** Children observe teacher-prepared apple sections, compare cutting directions, count visible seeds, and record findings.  
**Learning Domains:** Science; Math; Language & Literacy  
**Materials:** Whole and pre-cut apples, trays, magnifiers, chart paper, markers, part labels, gloves or tools  
**Setup:** Prepare sections immediately before the activity. Place each on a tray and keep cutting tools out of the learning area.  
**Steps:**
1. Revisit predictions.
2. Observe the whole apple’s outside.
3. Reveal vertical and horizontal sections.
4. Identify skin, flesh, core, stem, and seeds.
5. Count visible seeds without requiring removal.
6. Compare sections from different apples.
7. Record discoveries and revise predictions.

**Teacher Role:** Handle all cutting, support evidence-based discussion, and emphasize that predictions are ideas to test rather than answers to grade.  
**Teacher Language:** “What stayed the same?” “What is new now that we can see inside?” “Was your prediction confirmed or changed?” “Why might the seed counts look different?”  
**Learning Goals:** Observation; part-whole understanding; prediction; counting; evidence use  
**Observation Opportunities:** Note how children revise ideas, identify parts, count, compare, and communicate evidence.  
**Vocabulary:** cross-section, core, seed, flesh, inside, predict  
**Extensions:** Compare with pear or bell pepper photographs; photograph sections for a class science book.  
**Adaptations:** Use enlarged photos, plastic models, no-touch viewing, and visual answer choices.  
**Safety Notes:** Adult-only cutting; no tasting from investigation specimens; supervise seeds.  
**Age Modifications:** Younger preschoolers identify inside/outside and basic parts. Older preschoolers compare cut directions and record counts.

### Activity 2: Apple Print Pattern Studio

**Activity Category:** Art  
**Objective:** Children will explore shape, symmetry, repetition, and printmaking using apple-inspired forms.  
**Description:** Children print with teacher-prepared apple halves, sponges, or reusable apple-shaped stampers, creating free designs and repeating patterns.  
**Learning Domains:** Creative Arts; Math; Physical Development; Science  
**Materials:** Apple halves or stampers, washable paint, trays, large paper, smocks, markers  
**Setup:** Place a thin layer of paint on trays and provide separate printing stations.  
**Steps:**
1. Examine the shape of each printing surface.
2. Demonstrate press-lift rather than sliding.
3. Invite free printing first.
4. Notice shapes, spaces, overlaps, and seed-star impressions.
5. Offer pattern language to interested children.
6. Add drawn stems, leaves, labels, or stories after drying.

**Teacher Role:** Protect experimentation and discuss mathematical and visual discoveries without requiring a prescribed craft.  
**Teacher Language:** “What changed when you used more paint?” “What do you notice about the shape?” “Would you like to repeat something?”  
**Learning Goals:** Cause and effect; patterning; visual composition; hand control  
**Observation Opportunities:** Note pressure control, repetition, planning, response to unexpected prints, and pattern creation.  
**Vocabulary:** print, press, lift, repeat, pattern, overlap, shape  
**Extensions:** Layer colors; compare horizontal and vertical prints; make wrapping paper for the market.  
**Adaptations:** Use large-handled stampers, nonslip paper, sponge shapes, or finger-free tools.  
**Safety Notes:** Do not use printed apples for eating. Clean surfaces and spills promptly.  
**Age Modifications:** Younger preschoolers explore cause and effect. Older preschoolers design AB, AAB, or self-created patterns.

### Activity 3: Build an Apple Model

**Activity Category:** Fine Motor  
**Objective:** Children will create a three-dimensional model showing apple parts.  
**Description:** Children use dough and loose parts to create open-ended apple models, then explain or dictate what their model represents.  
**Learning Domains:** Physical Development; Science; Creative Arts; Language & Literacy  
**Materials:** Red, green, yellow, white, and brown dough; craft sticks; yarn; fabric leaves; trays; picture references  
**Setup:** Portion dough and place loose parts in divided trays. Avoid tiny items when unsafe.  
**Steps:**
1. Revisit apple parts.
2. Let children choose whether to model a whole apple, cut apple, seed, tree, or imagined apple.
3. Roll, pinch, flatten, connect, and shape materials.
4. Encourage children to identify represented parts.
5. Photograph or sketch models before reuse.

**Teacher Role:** Encourage planning and descriptive explanation while keeping the task open-ended.  
**Teacher Language:** “What part are you making?” “How could you show the inside?” “What does this piece represent?”  
**Learning Goals:** Fine-motor strength; spatial representation; part-whole concepts; expressive language  
**Observation Opportunities:** Note manipulation skills, representation, vocabulary, planning, and persistence.  
**Vocabulary:** model, core, seed, skin, connect, flatten  
**Extensions:** Label a photographed model; compare models; create an oversized collaborative apple.  
**Adaptations:** Use softer dough, larger loose parts, pre-rolled pieces, hand-over-hand support only with consent, or magnetic shapes.  
**Safety Notes:** Dough and loose parts are not for eating. Consider allergens in homemade dough.  
**Age Modifications:** Younger preschoolers make recognizable features. Older preschoolers create cutaway models and labels.

# THURSDAY — HOW APPLES CHANGE

## Daily Theme
Browning, pressing, mixing, cooking, and changing materials

## Daily Objectives

- Observe and describe changes in apple materials.
- Compare apple pieces under different conditions.
- Participate in a simple fair-test discussion.
- Sequence steps in a food or pretend-food process.

## Daily Learning Domains
Science; Language & Literacy; Math; Physical Development; Creative Arts; Social Emotional

## Daily Vocabulary
change, slice, brown, fresh, press, juice, sauce, recipe, mix, before, after

## Daily Materials
Apple slices prepared by an adult, clear cups, water, optional lemon water, timer, observation chart, applesauce or pretend ingredients, mashers, bowls, spoons, dramatic play kitchen materials

## Daily Book
**How to Make an Apple Pie and See the World** by Marjorie Priceman

## Daily Song
**Apples and Bananas**

## Circle Time
Show two fresh apple slices. Ask what children predict will happen if they sit out. Place one in plain air and one in an approved liquid such as water or diluted lemon water. Keep conditions simple and visible. Create a before sketch or photograph. Explain that the class will observe changes over time rather than waiting for an immediate result.

## Transition
Use sequencing prompts: “First wash, then slice, next mix, last clean.” Children act out each step while moving to the next area.

## Outdoor Play
**Apple Press Gross-Motor Station:** Children push large soft balls through a cardboard “press,” roll them down ramps, and collect them in baskets. Compare force, speed, and pathway. This is pretend equipment and not a real press.

## Daily Family Connection
Invite families to share a recipe name, food memory, or way they prepare fruit. No recipe ingredients or cultural tradition is treated as the default.

## Daily Observation Prompts

- Does the child describe change using before-and-after language?
- Can the child keep one comparison condition in mind?
- How does the child sequence process steps?
- Does the child notice gradual rather than immediate change?

## Daily Adaptations
Use picture sequences, one comparison pair, nonfood color-change materials, and recorded photo observations. Children may participate as photographers, timers, mixers, or narrators.

## Daily Safety Notes
Follow food policies. Adults prepare slices. Do not taste investigation samples. Manage citrus sensitivity and offer a plain-water comparison or nonfood alternative.

### Activity 1: Apple Browning Investigation

**Activity Category:** STEM/Discovery  
**Objective:** Children will observe how apple slices change under different conditions.  
**Description:** Children compare apple slices exposed to air and an approved liquid, making predictions and documenting changes over time.  
**Learning Domains:** Science; Language & Literacy; Math  
**Materials:** Teacher-cut apple slices, labeled clear cups or plates, water, optional diluted lemon water, timer, camera, chart  
**Setup:** Prepare equal-looking slices. Label conditions with pictures and words. Place out of eating reach if needed.  
**Steps:**
1. Observe fresh slices.
2. Predict what may happen.
3. Place slices in selected conditions.
4. Observe after short intervals.
5. Compare color, texture, and smell without tasting.
6. Record observations with drawings, marks, photos, or dictation.
7. Discuss whether the evidence matched predictions.

**Teacher Role:** Keep the comparison understandable, acknowledge uncertainty, and avoid overstating results from a small classroom demonstration.  
**Teacher Language:** “What are we keeping the same?” “What is different?” “What changed first?” “What evidence do you see?”  
**Learning Goals:** Change over time; comparison; prediction; documentation  
**Observation Opportunities:** Note attention to change, use of temporal words, evidence-based reasoning, and patience.  
**Vocabulary:** brown, fresh, condition, compare, before, after, evidence  
**Extensions:** Make a time-lapse photo sequence; compare covered and uncovered samples; revisit the next day.  
**Adaptations:** Use enlarged photographs, color cards, a visual timer, and no-contact observation.  
**Safety Notes:** Investigation pieces are not eaten. Dispose after use and sanitize surfaces.  
**Age Modifications:** Younger preschoolers notice color changes. Older preschoolers help plan conditions and represent results.

### Activity 2: Apple Mash and Mix

**Activity Category:** Sensory Play  
**Objective:** Children will explore how pressure and mixing change texture.  
**Description:** Using approved applesauce or a nonfood dough alternative, children mash, stir, scoop, and compare textures while discussing change.  
**Learning Domains:** Science; Physical Development; Language & Literacy; Social Emotional  
**Materials:** Unsweetened applesauce or nonfood substitute, bowls, potato mashers, spoons, scoops, trays, gloves if needed  
**Setup:** Confirm permissions and allergies. Portion materials into small bowls. Establish whether materials are for tasting or investigation only; never mix the two purposes casually.  
**Steps:**
1. Observe the starting material.
2. Press, mash, scoop, and stir with tools.
3. Describe texture changes.
4. Compare gentle and strong pressure.
5. Sequence actions used.
6. Clean tools and workspace together.

**Teacher Role:** Model safe tool use, introduce texture vocabulary, and respect children who decline sensory contact.  
**Teacher Language:** “What happened when you pressed?” “How is it different now?” “Which tool changes it most?”  
**Learning Goals:** Cause and effect; hand strength; descriptive language; self-regulation in sensory play  
**Observation Opportunities:** Note tool use, tolerance for textures, strategy changes, and descriptive vocabulary.  
**Vocabulary:** mash, press, stir, smooth, lumpy, soft, change  
**Extensions:** Compare tools; create a texture scale; use pretend recipe cards.  
**Adaptations:** Offer sealed sensory bags, tools only, gloves, dry dough, or visual observation.  
**Safety Notes:** Follow allergy and sanitation policies. Do not pressure tasting.  
**Age Modifications:** Younger preschoolers explore actions and effects. Older preschoolers compare tools and sequence a process.

### Activity 3: Recipe Sequence Theater

**Activity Category:** Dramatic Play  
**Objective:** Children will sequence and dramatize steps in making an apple food.  
**Description:** Children choose a pretend apple product and act out gathering, washing, preparing, mixing, cooking, serving, and cleaning using dramatic-play materials.  
**Learning Domains:** Language & Literacy; Social Emotional; Creative Arts; Math  
**Materials:** Picture sequence cards, pretend kitchen tools, fabric or paper apples, bowls, recipe pads, timer, aprons, serving materials  
**Setup:** Place open-ended materials in the dramatic-play area with simple visual step cards.  
**Steps:**
1. Choose an apple product to pretend to make.
2. Decide what steps and roles are needed.
3. Arrange sequence cards.
4. Act out the process.
5. Add a problem such as a missing ingredient or customer order.
6. Serve and explain the pretend product.
7. Include cleanup in the sequence.

**Teacher Role:** Extend narrative, sequencing, writing, and cooperation without controlling the storyline.  
**Teacher Language:** “What happens first?” “How will your team remember the order?” “What could you do if an ingredient is missing?”  
**Learning Goals:** Sequencing; narrative play; cooperation; functional literacy  
**Observation Opportunities:** Note temporal language, sustained roles, symbolic play, and group planning.  
**Vocabulary:** recipe, ingredient, first, next, last, serve, clean  
**Extensions:** Dictate a class recipe; make menus; create an order system.  
**Adaptations:** Use two- or three-step sequences, role visuals, duplicate tools, and seated access.  
**Safety Notes:** Pretend appliances only; monitor costume ties and small props.  
**Age Modifications:** Younger preschoolers act out familiar actions. Older preschoolers create and revise multi-step recipes.

# FRIDAY — ORCHARD MARKET CELEBRATION

## Daily Theme
Applying the week’s learning through child-led market play, design, and reflection

## Daily Objectives

- Apply counting, sorting, writing, and social skills in meaningful play.
- Collaboratively plan and operate an orchard market.
- Reflect on discoveries and communicate new learning.
- Celebrate participation without requiring food or performance.

## Daily Learning Domains
Social Emotional; Language & Literacy; Math; Science; Creative Arts; Physical Development

## Daily Vocabulary
market, customer, seller, price, order, receipt, display, choice, discovery, reflect

## Daily Materials

## Daily Book
**Apple Farmer Annie** by Monica Wellington

## Daily Song
**The Farmer in the Dell** with orchard-role verses

## Circle Time
Review the week’s wonder chart. Read several child questions and identify discoveries, questions still open, and ideas that changed. Invite children to help plan an orchard market: what spaces, roles, signs, products, and rules are needed? Record the plan and let children choose how to contribute.

## Transition
Children receive a market task card or choose a movement: carry, roll, sort, count, write, deliver, or greet.

- How does the child contribute to a group plan?

Offer jobs with different sensory, language, motor, and social demands. Use visual menus, number cards, role badges, and a quiet shopping time. Children may participate by making signs, organizing materials, photographing, or observing.

## Daily Safety Notes
Keep pathways clear. Use large pretend money and counters. No food is necessary. If food is included, follow all food safeguards and keep it separate from reusable play materials.

### Activity 1: Child-Led Orchard Market

**Activity Category:** Dramatic Play  
**Objective:** Children will apply literacy, math, cooperation, and problem-solving skills in sustained market play.  
**Description:** Children design and operate an orchard market with roles, displays, orders, pricing, sorting, weighing, packing, and delivery.  
**Learning Domains:** Social Emotional; Language & Literacy; Math; Creative Arts  
**Materials:** Pretend apples, baskets, crates, signs, scale, reusable bags, order pads, markers, large pretend money, calculators as props, name tags  
**Setup:** Use children’s planning ideas to organize flexible stations. Leave some materials unassigned so children can invent uses.  
**Steps:**
1. Review the group market plan.
2. Build displays and make signs.
3. Choose or create roles.
4. Open the market for free play.
5. Introduce customer requests involving color, number, size, or delivery.
6. Encourage role changes and peer-created problems.
7. Pause only when needed to help resolve access or conflict.
8. Reflect on what made the market work.

**Teacher Role:** Observe, document, and enter play strategically as a customer or coworker to extend skills.  
**Teacher Language:** “How will customers know where to go?” “I need four apples in two colors. Can you help?” “What could make the line work better?”  
**Learning Goals:** Applied numeracy; functional writing; role negotiation; sustained collaborative play  
**Observation Opportunities:** Note spontaneous counting, sorting, mark-making, role language, cooperation, and problem-solving.  
**Vocabulary:** customer, seller, price, order, receipt, display, total  
**Extensions:** Add delivery maps, coupons, inventory, closed/open signs, or community donations in pretend play.  
**Adaptations:** Provide visual shopping lists, one-step jobs, large props, a quiet station, and partner roles.  
**Safety Notes:** Supervise small props, maintain clear paths, and avoid real plastic bags with suffocation risk.  
**Age Modifications:** Younger preschoolers exchange and sort. Older preschoolers manage multi-item orders, inventory, and role systems.

### Activity 2: Design an Apple Carrier

**Activity Category:** STEM/Discovery  
**Objective:** Children will design, test, and revise a structure that carries apples safely.  
**Description:** Children use recycled materials to create a basket, crate, cart, tray, or package for transporting pretend apples through a short test route.  
**Learning Domains:** Science; Math; Creative Arts; Physical Development; Social Emotional  
**Materials:** Small boxes, cardboard, paper cups, tubes, tape, fabric, yarn used under supervision, craft sticks, pretend apples, test ramp or path  
**Setup:** Display materials and define the design problem: carry a chosen number of apples from orchard to market without losing them.  
**Steps:**
1. Choose how many apples the carrier should hold.
2. Sketch, discuss, or select a starting design.
3. Build the carrier.
4. Test it on the route.
5. Observe where apples shift, fall, or fit.
6. Revise and retest.
7. Share one change that improved the design.

**Teacher Role:** Focus on process rather than making a perfect product; support safe joining and cutting of materials.  
**Teacher Language:** “What does your carrier need to do?” “Where is the weak spot?” “What changed after your test?” “How do you know it improved?”  
**Learning Goals:** Engineering cycle; capacity; stability; persistence; collaborative design  
**Observation Opportunities:** Note planning, testing, revision, quantity reasoning, and response to failure.  
**Vocabulary:** design, carrier, capacity, stable, test, revise, transport  
**Extensions:** Compare carriers; create a bumpy route; design for one giant apple versus many small apples.  
**Adaptations:** Offer pre-cut pieces, easy-tear tape, Velcro, partner building, or ready-made containers to modify.  
**Safety Notes:** Adults manage sharp tools and difficult cutting. Avoid long loose cords.  
**Age Modifications:** Younger preschoolers select and modify containers. Older preschoolers define criteria, compare capacity, and document revisions.

### Activity 3: Our Apple Discoveries Book

**Activity Category:** Literacy  
**Objective:** Children will reflect on and communicate one discovery from the week.  
**Description:** Each child contributes a page, photograph, drawing, dictated statement, label, or symbol to a class book about apple investigations.  
**Learning Domains:** Language & Literacy; Science; Creative Arts; Social Emotional

**Setup:** Display investigation artifacts and photographs. Prepare multiple page formats, including blank pages and visual sentence supports.  
**Steps:**

**Learning Goals:** Reflection; recall; authorship; communication; print awareness  
**Observation Opportunities:** Note recall, concept selection, representation, vocabulary, and confidence sharing ideas.  
**Vocabulary:** discovery, remember, author, page, explain, question  
**Extensions:** Send a digital version to families; add audio recordings; create a table of contents.  
**Adaptations:** Offer photo choice, eye-gaze selection, stickers, stamps, partner dictation, and private sharing.
